# S10 — Operations

## Outcome

A regente opens **Ajustes · Registros · Cumplimiento** once a month and sees, per sede, the list of things a droguería is expected to keep: the concepto sanitario, the matrícula mercantil, the tarjeta profesional of the director técnico, the certificado de fumigación, the plan de residuos, the control de temperatura, the resolución de facturación. Each line says whether it is answered, which PDF answers it, who said so, when, and when that PDF expires. Attaching a document is one action in the row. A document expiring inside the tenant's warning horizon raises a flag on the Panel, sends one email to the people named in the settings group, and keeps doing so until somebody answers it. That is the whole of compliance in v1 and the boundary is deliberate: **Botica records what the pharmacy says it has done and holds the evidence.** It does not file anything with anyone, does not compute the monthly controlled-medicine consumption report, does not maintain the libro de control as a legal instrument, and does not certify compliance (§12). The checklist may carry a line saying *the libro de control is kept and is up to date* — recording that the pharmacy keeps one is not keeping one, and the section says so on screen, once, in Spanish, above the table.

A second thing exists at the end of this stage, and it is what turns a demo into a business: **a new droguería network can be brought from nothing to selling at a counter by one operator following one written flow**, rather than by an engineer improvising across nine Django admin screens. The tenant, its first `owner`, its sedes, its catalog, its opening stock and its enrolled tills each have an owner in an earlier stage, and this stage's provisioning does almost no writing of its own — it sequences those writers, names what cannot be automated and why, and refuses to start on a half-answered manifest. Two steps in that flow will never be automatable and are marked as such: opening stock needs a physical count, and a device claim needs a person standing at the till that will use it.

The third thing is the operational half, and it is the difference between a product that can be sold and one that can only be shown. **A backup nobody has restored is a belief, not a backup**, so this stage's deliverable is not a backup schedule — that is deploy automation — but a *restore drill that has been performed, timed and written down*, with the data-loss window it actually produced. The `stock_on_hand` rebuild that A3 promises is rehearsed on a live tenant during business hours, because a rebuild that has only ever been described is the same category of belief. An operator gets one view that answers *which till has not synced and since when*, which is the first question support is ever asked and today has no answer at all. Where the client's invoicing system is configured as a handoff target, the backlog of deliveries that have not landed has a number available at all times; where none is configured — the default, and the state every demo and every pilot's first week runs in — there is no figure, no queue and no signal, because there are no documents (§8). Five signals are watched — sync lag per device, push failure rate, handoff backlog, job queue depth and open negative-stock exceptions — and each has a threshold that emails somebody rather than waiting to be noticed on a dashboard nobody opens, with the handoff signal **simply absent** on an instance that has no target.

And there is a runbook, in the repository rather than in the product, for the six failures that will actually happen: a till that will not sync, a handoff target that refuses or times out, a projection that has drifted, a browser that lost its local store, a pharmacy that changed its resolución, and a location that opened. Each entry says what support hears, where to look first, what to do, and — the half that is usually missing — what must never be done, because four of these six have a fast wrong answer that destroys data the ledger was built to protect.

## Scope

### In

1. **`compliance_documents`** — the vault: `location_id` nullable (null = the whole network), `type`, `name`, `file_url`, `expires_at`, `uploaded_by`, `notes`, plus the coined columns in *Data* (§3, ledger).
2. **`checklist_templates`** — the list a droguería is expected to keep: `code`, `title`, `description`, `requires_document`, `frequency` (§3, ledger). Shipped with a **default set** the tenant activates and edits.
3. **`checklist_entries`** — per location, per template, per period: `status` (`pending` \| `done` \| `not_applicable`), `document_id` nullable, `verified_by`, `verified_at`, `expires_at` (§3, ledger, disputed columns).
4. **Document upload, replacement, download and archival** against the S3-compatible object storage §10 names, with the row created only after the object exists.
5. **The expiry engine** — one horizon per tenant, an optional per-template override, a daily sweep, an escalation step, and email to the recipients in the settings group.
6. **The expiry board** — one place naming all three expiry sources an operator worries about: `compliance_documents.expires_at` (owned here), `items.invima_expires_at` (S1, read) and `lots.expires_at` (S3, read). The two it does not own are **named and linked, never duplicated**.
7. **The `/api/settings/compliance` group** — which checklist template set applies, the expiry warning horizon, the escalation step and the notification recipients (ledger rule 5).
8. **The `Cumplimiento` settings section** (§B.8.4·6), which fixes its layout, its five columns, its badges and the sentence it carries at the top.
9. **One Panel region strip and one Reportes group** — §B.8.4·6 states that a document expiring inside 30 days raises the Panel's compliance figure, and §B.8.4·3 names `Cumplimiento` as a report group in the Reportes rail. Both are rendered by S10 into contribution points S9 shipped, and the compliance figure goes into **S9's region strip above the KPI row rather than into a fifth KPI card** — the handoff fixes that row at `repeat(4,1fr)` and the screen is S9's to change.
10. **The sedes half of `Ajustes · Organización · Sedes y dispositivos`** — create, edit and deactivate a sede, and the `locations` writes behind it (ledger, `locations` Writes = S0, S10). S2 owns the devices half of the same section.
11. **Tenant and location provisioning as a flow** — a written sequence, a `provision_tenant` management command covering the automatable steps from one manifest, and an explicit statement of the three steps that are not automatable and why.
12. **Tenant impersonation by a `platform_admin`**, with the `audit_log` row it writes on entry (S0's *Out* assigns this here). Selection-based, one tenant at a time, never a cross-tenant query (§2).
13. **The fleet operational view** — one Django-admin surface answering *which till has not synced and since when*, across every tenant, built as N pinned reads and **not** as a relaxed pin (S0).
14. **Five operational signals with thresholds and an owner** — sync lag per device, push failure rate, handoff backlog **where an invoicing target is configured and absent where none is**, job queue depth, open negative-stock exceptions — swept daily and emailed when they cross.
15. **The restore drill** — a documented, performed, timed restore from the newest backup onto a scratch database, with the observed data-loss window recorded.
16. **The projection rebuild drill** — S3's `inventory.projection_rebuild` executed on a live tenant during business hours, per location, with the before/after comparison recorded.
17. **The operator runbook** — six failures, each with symptom, first read, fix and prohibition; plus the two drills; held **in the repository**, not in the product.
18. **Audit trail** on every elevated-role mutation this stage exposes: document upload, replace, archive; checklist verify, unverify and `not_applicable`; template activation and edit; sede create, edit and deactivate; the settings write; and impersonation entry (ledger).

### Out

- **`daily_metrics`, the Panel's KPI cards, per-sede comparison, the Reportes rail itself and the export mechanism** — **S9** (§13). S10 supplies one region strip's endpoint and one report group's data and builds neither surface. **The KPI row is S9's and S10 adds no card to it** — a fifth tile would re-lay a screen the client signed off on, and re-laying it is a decision only its owner takes.
- **The devices table, the device list, the claim card, `sync_conflicts` and the whole sync protocol** — **S2**. S10 reads them; the fleet view is a second reader, not a second writer, and it raises no conflict row.
- **The `Envíos a facturación` work list, the canonical sale document, the mapping layer and the delivery job** — **S5** (§8, A9). The runbook links to that surface; it does not reproduce it. **`dian_resolutions` and `numbering_leases` are nobody's** — not built at v1 and in no migration (A6) — so no endpoint, no provisioning step, no runbook entry, no read-out and no settings key in this stage names one.
- **The stock ledger, the rebuild and the verify job themselves** — **S3** (A3, rule 7). S10 *rehearses* the rebuild and *surfaces* the verify job's drift. Building a second rebuild path, or a job that repairs the projection, is a defect (rule 7).
- **The catalog load tool and the sales-history loader** — **S1** and **S6**. Provisioning invokes them and writes no `imports` row of its own (ledger).
- **A regulatory reporting engine.** No monthly controlled-medicine consumption report, no libro de control as a legal instrument, no filing with INVIMA, the DIAN, a Secretaría de Salud or anyone else, and no certification of compliance (§12). This is the scope the client set and it is written down here so the first customer conversation negotiates against a document rather than against a memory.
- **INVIMA register validation.** `invima_registration` is captured, displayed and flagged on expiry, and is never checked against INVIMA's online register (§12, ledger).
- **Per-client deployment.** The Coolify stack, the four containers, the Postgres instance, secrets, the object-storage bucket and its lifecycle policy, Caddy's TLS and DNS, the backup schedule and its retention — **deploy automation, not application code** (§9, §10, and S0's own statement of the same rule). S10 states the contract the application expects from that half; it ships no installer, no provisioning of infrastructure from inside the app, and no credential-writing endpoint.
- **A tenant-facing import wizard** (§12, S1's *Gated on*). Provisioning is run by us, which is the deck's own rollout (§1).
- **Uptime monitoring, log aggregation, APM, alerting infrastructure.** The five signals are computed and emailed by the application; where those emails go, and whether a pager sits behind them, is the deploy half.
- **A cross-tenant analytics or billing surface.** The fleet view answers operational questions about devices, documents and queues. It reads no sales figures across tenants and computes no revenue.
- **Responsive behaviour below 1280 × 720** — open with design (§B.11, §B.17·2).

## Provisioning, and the line between the application and the deploy

This section exists because "creating a tenant" is currently nine Django admin screens in an order only the person who built them knows, and because the fastest way to build the wrong thing here is to build an installer inside the application.

**The flow, in order, with the writer named for every step.** S10 sequences these; it writes almost nothing itself, and that is the point — a provisioning flow that wrote `users` or `stock_moves` directly would be a second writer for a table that already has one, and the two would diverge the first time a rule changed.

| | Step | Who writes it | Owner | Automatable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The tenant: `name`, `slug`, `nit`, `status`, and the `tenant` settings group — timezone, currency, number format | S0's Django-admin CRUD and its rule-5 settings helper | **S0** | yes |
| 2 | The first `owner`: an invitation, consumed by the person it was sent to | S0's invitation path. **Never a directly-created `users` row** — the ledger gives `users` to S0 and invite-only creation is §3's rule, not a convenience | **S0** | yes, up to the email |
| 3 | The sedes: one `locations` row each, with checklist entries instantiated in the same transaction | `POST /api/locations` | **S10** | yes |
| 4 | The catalog: manufacturers, categories, suppliers, items, barcodes, supplier links, prices, customers | S1's load tool — a management command, dry-run unless told otherwise, exiting non-zero if any row failed | **S1** | yes, once the file exists |
| 5 | Opening stock and lots, per sede | S3's opening-stock run, through the ledger service as adjusting moves (rule 7, A3) | **S3** | **no** |
| 6 | **Optional — the invoicing handoff:** which system the client already invoices with, the mapping in force, and its credential | `PATCH /api/settings/invoicing` for the target and the mapping. The credential goes into the instance's secrets store, which is deploy automation — **no endpoint in this flow writes one** (§9, S5) | **S5** | yes, from the manifest — **and skipped entirely when the manifest names no target** |
| 7 | Device enrolment: one claim per till | `POST /api/devices/claim`, which returns the `device_key` **once** | **S2** | **no** |
| 8 | The checklist: the default template set activated and this period's entries created | `POST /api/compliance/templates/activate-defaults` | **S10** | yes |
| 9 | The remaining settings groups: `sync`, `inventory`, `purchasing`, `pricing`, `assistant`, `compliance` — `invoicing` is step 6 and is left empty where there is no target | each stage's own `PATCH /api/settings/{group}` on the rule-5 helper | **each owner** | yes, from the manifest |
| 10 | Sales history, where §11.2's export exists | S6's history loader, writing `sales` at `source = imported` | **S6** | yes |

**`provision_tenant` is a management command taking an explicit tenant argument and pinning before any work** (rule 6, and S0's own table names S10 in that context). It reads one manifest file describing steps 1, 3, 8 and 9 — and step 6 where the manifest names a target, which most will not — reports what it would do, and does nothing unless `--apply` is passed — the same discipline S1's loader established and for the same reason: a loader that inferred a tenant would be a loader that could write one network's data into another's, and under `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` an unpinned run writes nothing at all and says nothing about it (A1). It invokes step 4 rather than reimplementing it, records its run in `audit_log` and writes no `imports` row (ledger).

**Two steps will never be automatable, and saying so is the deliverable.** Step 5 needs a physical count — a droguería's opening stock is what is on the shelf, and a number taken from the previous system is the number that was wrong enough to buy new software. Step 7 needs a person standing at the till that will sell, because the `device_key` is returned once and a key emailed to an operator is a key in an inbox. Automating either of the two later is a decision somebody takes deliberately, not a gap somebody fills.

**Step 6 is optional, and a tenant that skips it is fully provisioned.** All provisioning does for the handoff is name a target and its mapping where the client has already given us their invoicing system's API — and that is the whole of it. Botica issues no fiscal document, allocates no fiscal number, requests nothing from the DIAN on a pharmacy's behalf and contracts no proveedor tecnológico (§8, A6, A9). **A tenant with no invoicing target configured is provisioned, complete and correct**: it sells, its receipt carries `sales.number`, and nothing in the flow waits on a credential, a contract or a document from an authority. That is the state every demo runs in and the state a pilot starts in, and a provisioning step that blocked on it would be a step blocking on a decision the client has not had to make yet.

**The go-live check** — one list an operator answers before a network sells, and a line with no answer is a line that stops the go-live rather than a note in a spreadsheet: the catalog is loaded and its `imports` row reports zero failed rows; every item that will be sold has a price in force; opening stock is entered for every sede that will sell; every till has claimed and synced once; `storage_persisted` is true on every till; every user has a role and every `cashier` has a home sede; the six settings groups a network needs are written rather than empty, `invoicing` being the seventh and **empty being its correct value where there is no target** (§8); the checklist is instantiated and the concepto sanitario and the resolución de facturación are attached; and one test sale has been closed and reads correctly on the sale detail. **Where an invoicing target is configured**, that sale produced exactly one `fiscal_documents` row and it reached `acknowledged` — which says the target holds our document and says nothing whatever about the DIAN, because Botica never learns that (§8). **Where no target is configured, it produced no row at all, and that is the pass condition rather than a gap.**

**What deploy automation owns, and what the application expects from it.** The split is stated so nobody builds half of one inside the other (§9, §10, and S0's identical rule for secrets).

| Deploy automation owns | The application expects |
|---|---|
| The Coolify stack on Hetzner: the four containers, one Postgres, one image for `web` and `worker` (§9) | Nothing. It reads its configuration and starts |
| Secrets — database credentials, the model-gateway key, object-storage keys, and **the invoicing target's credential where a target is configured** — in the instance's own store, never held centrally (S0) | To read them from the environment. **No endpoint in this stage writes a credential**, and where a target exists the invoicing settings section shows its credential only as `Configurada en el servidor` with no field to type one into (S5). **An instance with no invoicing target needs no credential of that kind at all** — the common case at v1 — so nothing in the build or the deployment waits on one, and no proveedor tecnológico is a dependency of either (§8, A9, §12) |
| The S3-compatible bucket, its region, its lifecycle policy and its access policy (§10) | The bucket to exist and to be private. `file_url` is an object reference and every read mints a short-lived presigned URL |
| Caddy's TLS, its renewal and the DNS behind it (§9) | To be served over TLS. `navigator.storage.persist()` and the service worker both require a secure context, so this is not cosmetic — a till on plain HTTP cannot hold its store (§5) |
| The backup schedule, its retention, its encryption and its off-instance copy | **A restorable backup no older than the interval the drill measured**, and a documented location. The application takes no backup, ships no `pg_dump`, and has no restore endpoint |
| Postgres upgrades, disk growth and the instance's own monitoring | Postgres 18 and a connection pool it opens itself (§9). No pgbouncer, no Redis |

**The fleet view is not an API path, and that is the decision.** S0 fixed that a `platform_admin` with no selection pins nothing, that there is no wildcard, and that a fleet-wide operational view is S10's problem rather than a relaxed pin. A `/api/` path that returned rows from every tenant would be exactly the relaxed pin S0 refused, and it would sit on the same router a tenant user reaches. So the fleet view is **a Django-admin page and a `fleet_health` management command**, both of which iterate tenants and issue one pinned read each, and neither of which is reachable from the tenant-facing application at all. The one figure read outside a pin is job queue depth, and it is legitimate: Procrastinate's tables carry no `tenant_id`, are not policy tables, and are platform state rather than tenant data (§9, A1). *If the per-tenant iteration ever stops being fast enough* — it is roughly one indexed read per tenant per signal and will hold well past the pilot — the answer is a platform-scoped snapshot table, and the ledger assigns S10 no such table, so it is in *Gated on* rather than invented here.

**Settings group** (ledger rule 5; the register names the first three concerns and this is them made into keys, plus one added inside the group S10 owns):

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `checklist_template_set` | `default` | Which template set applies. One value in v1; the key exists so a second set is data rather than a migration |

| `escalation_days` | `10` | The second, louder notice, 10 days after the first. **Chosen here rather than inherited**: the earlier draft mirrored S5's numbering-capacity alert, which the re-scope removed along with numbering (A6). The reasoning that survives is the one that mattered — one escalation ladder for every expiry notice in the product, because an operator learning two of them is learning one too many |
| `notification_recipients` | the tenant's `owner` addresses | Who receives expiry notices and operational alerts. **Added inside the group**, and deliberately not a per-user preference: an alert routed by preference is an alert one person can switch off for everyone |

## Jobs

Three. All take `tenant_id` in the payload and pin inside their own transaction before touching anything (rule 6, third context); none issues its own `SET LOCAL`.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `compliance.check_expiries` | Cron, daily, 06:00 tenant time, per tenant | `(tenant_id, date)` — a re-run on the same day re-notifies nobody | Compares every non-archived `compliance_documents.expires_at` and every `checklist_entries.expires_at` against `expiry_warning_days` (or the template's `notice_days`) and `escalation_days`, and sends **one digest email per tenant**, not one per document. Retried with backoff; a persistent failure leaves the Panel strip's own live figure standing and surfaces on the fleet view, because a compliance notice that silently stopped is worse than one that never started. Delivery failure never blocks anything (§10, S0) |
| `compliance.roll_checklist` | Cron, daily, 05:00 tenant time, per tenant | `(tenant_id, location_id, template_id, period_start)` — the same key as the table's unique constraint | Creates the next period's `checklist_entries` for every active location and active template whose `frequency` says a new period has begun. `on_expiry` templates roll when their linked document's `expires_at` passes rather than on the calendar. Naturally idempotent: a second run finds every row and writes none. A failure delays entries by a day and loses nothing, because the key is the period rather than the run |
| `operations.health_sweep` | Cron, hourly, per tenant, plus one platform-scoped pass for queue depth | `(tenant_id, signal, hour)` | Computes the five signals below — four of them for a tenant with no invoicing target, which is the normal case at v1 and is a complete pass rather than a partial one (§8) — writes one `audit_log` row per tenant per pass carrying the values, and emails the recipients only on a **threshold crossing** — not every hour, and not while a signal stays across. A flapping signal notifies once and then names its duration in the next digest. A failing sweep is itself visible on the fleet view as a stale `last_swept_at`, since a monitor whose own failure is silent is not a monitor |

**Backup is not a job in this system.** It is deploy automation (§9, §10) and the application does not schedule, take, encrypt or ship one. What the application owns is the *drill* — see *Acceptance* — and the runbook entry that says where the backup lives, how old the newest one is expected to be, and what to do with it. Putting `pg_dump` inside the worker would put the database's recovery path inside the thing being recovered.

### The five signals

| Signal | Read from | Raise when |
|---|---|---|
| **Sync lag per device** | `devices.last_synced_at`, `status = active` (S2) | Older than S2's `stale_device_hours` (default 48). **S2's threshold is reused, not re-chosen** — two numbers for "this till is quiet" is how an operator ends up believing neither |
| **Push failure rate** | `devices.last_pushed_at` against `last_seen_at`, plus open `sync_conflicts` of protocol type in the trailing hour (S2) | A device seen inside the hour with no successful push in four hours, or protocol rejections above 2% of batches for the tenant. A till that is *reaching us* and *not landing writes* is the case a raw lag number hides |
| **Handoff backlog** | `GET /api/fiscal-documents/summary` (S5), read and never recomputed, **and only where it answers `configured: true`** | Any handoff unsent for more than 24 h, or a `failed` count above zero for more than one business day. Failed deliveries are an administrator's work list (§8) and this signal only makes sure somebody looked. **Where `configured` is false the signal is absent — not zero, not green, not raised**: the sweep computes nothing for it, no threshold can be crossed and no email can be sent, because there are no documents (§8). A monitoring dashboard that alerts on an unconfigured optional integration is a dashboard people learn to ignore, and the alert then ignored is the one that mattered. **Nothing this signal reports is a DIAN outcome** — it counts our deliveries to the client's system and nothing else |
| **Job queue depth** | Procrastinate's own tables (§9), platform-scoped, outside any pin | Depth above 500, or an oldest queued job older than 15 minutes. The one signal that is not per tenant, because the queue is not |
| **Open negative-stock exceptions** | `sync_conflicts` of type `negative_stock`, `status = open` (S3, §5) | Any open beyond 72 h. §5 makes an oversell a normal reconcilable event; an oversell nobody has resolved in three days is a shelf nobody has counted |

**Projection drift is not a sixth signal.** S3's `inventory.projection_verify` already writes its result to `audit_log` nightly, and non-zero drift is a *defect signal, not a correction event* (S3, rule 7). The fleet view reads those rows and renders any non-zero drift at the top, above every threshold, because it means a code path wrote outside the ledger service and no threshold makes that acceptable.

## The two drills

A capability nobody has exercised is a claim. Both of these are rehearsed rather than described, and the artefact of each is a number written into the runbook with the date it was measured — not a paragraph saying it should work.

**The restore drill.** Take the newest backup, restore it onto a scratch database on the same instance class, point a throwaway application container at it, sign in, and read Existencias and the Panel. Record four things: the age of the backup that was used, the elapsed time from starting the restore to a signed-in user reading a page, the **observed data-loss window** — the gap between the newest transaction in the restored copy and the moment the backup was taken — and anything that had to be done by hand. Run it **quarterly and before each new customer goes live**, and re-run it after any change to the database version or the backup arrangement. The two failure modes it actually catches are the ones a schedule cannot: a backup that restores but is missing an extension or a role the application needs, and a restore that takes six hours nobody had budgeted for. *If the drill has not been run, the platform has no backup* — that is the standard this stage sets, and it is deliberately absolute, because every softer version of it degrades into a green tick on a schedule page.

**The projection rebuild drill.** A3 promises that `stock_on_hand` can be dropped and rebuilt from `stock_moves` at any time without loss, and S3 ships the operation. This stage proves it **on a live tenant during business hours**, which is the only proof that matters: rebuild one location while another sede is selling, then compare every quantity on Existencias before and after. S3's procedure already makes this safe — the rebuild takes the location's advisory lock that the ledger service also takes on every append, so a rebuild and a sale serialise rather than interleave, and it runs per location so a 20-sede network is twenty short locks rather than one long one. Record the location, the row count, the lock duration and the diff, which must be empty. Run it **quarterly and immediately after any change to the ledger service**. *Never run it as a repair.* A non-zero diff is a defect signal: it means a code path wrote outside the service, and rebuilding on top of that finding erases the evidence of the write path that caused it (rule 7).

Every surface here is office-side, `owner`/`admin` or `platform_admin`, and every token, density, badge family and state is design-system Part B rather than an invention of this document. Replacing is a row action; archiving is behind a destructive confirm whose button says `Archivar documento` and whose body states that the requirement it evidences will return to `Pendiente`.

**2 · The expiry board** — a third block inside the same section, and the one place a regente sees all three sources at once. A `Compact` table: `Qué vence 34 · Dónde 16 · Fuente 16 · Vence 12 · Estado 22`. `Fuente` reads **Cumplimiento**, **Registro INVIMA** or **Lote**, and the two S10 does not own are **links to their owning surfaces and carry no action here** — an INVIMA row opens the item on Inventario, a lot row opens Existencias filtered to it. Duplicating S1's `Registro vencido` badge or S3's lot expiry into a second editable surface is how two screens start disagreeing about the same date.

**3 · The Panel's compliance figure, in S9's region strip** — §B.8.4·6 states that a document expiring inside 30 days raises it, so the figure exists on the Panel by the design system's own assertion. **It renders through S9's region-strip contribution point above the KPI row, and not as a fifth KPI card**: the handoff fixes that row at four tiles and `repeat(4,1fr)`, the Panel is S9's screen, and a stage that re-lays another stage's signed-off row to make room for its own number is how a high-fidelity handoff stops being one. S9's contract is a family, a title, a detail line, at most one `sm` action, and nothing rendered when there is nothing to say, and S10 supplies exactly that: the **warning** family while the count is above zero, **critical** the moment a document has already expired or a requirement whose `requires_document` is true has none; title **`Cumplimiento · 30 días`**, whose horizon follows `expiry_warning_days` rather than a constant; detail line naming the split — **`3 documentos por vencer · 1 requisito sin soporte`**; and one `sm` secondary **`Ver cumplimiento`** opening `/panel?settings=compliance`, the same search param the section is already a link to. With nothing inside the horizon the strip renders nothing at all — the contract's own zero case, and the reason a tenant that is up to date sees a Panel identical to the handoff's. It reads `GET /api/compliance/summary` **live rather than `daily_metrics`**, on the precedent S5 set and for the same reason: a rollup computed nightly cannot answer what expires today. The endpoint is budgeted at < 50ms so the Panel's **1,2s p95 first paint at 20 sedes survives a second live strip** (§4) — which is the worst case and is measured as one, since S5's strip renders nothing at all where no invoicing target is configured and most v1 instances will have none. *If two live strips turn out to cost more than that budget allows*, this one moves to `daily_metrics` and loses a day of freshness, which compliance can afford and the handoff backlog cannot — that is the order in which they give way, decided here so nobody has to decide it under a slow Panel.

**4 · The `Cumplimiento` group in Reportes** — §B.8.4·3 names the group in the rail; S10 supplies two report definitions into the surface S9 built. `Estado de cumplimiento por sede` — the checklist as a matrix of sede against requirement — and `Vencimientos próximos` — the expiry board over a chosen window. Both take §B.8.4·3's fixed shape: a `t-20` title, a filter bar, one chart from §B.12's four forms, one table, and an `sm` secondary `Exportar` to CSV and XLSX, never a PDF of a table. Both carry the mandatory provenance line under the title — `Calculado en vivo · al 31/08 06:00` — because a report whose provenance is invisible is a report somebody will dispute in a meeting.

**5 · `Ajustes · Organización · Sedes y dispositivos` — the sedes half** — S2 owns the devices half of this section and S10 adds the sedes half above it, per §B.8.4·2's own statement that creating a sede is a settings action rather than a button on the Sedes screen. A `Compact` table: `Sede 22 · Código 10 · Tipo 12 · Ciudad 14 · Dispositivos 10 (der.) · Estado 14`. `Tipo` renders **Punto de venta**, **Bodega** or **Centro de distribución** — the `location_type` enum's three English values in Spanish, and the third exists and is never created in v1 (A10). Header action `Nueva sede`, `sm` primary, opening a 720px form modal (§B.8.5): `Código`, `Nombre`, `Tipo`, `Dirección`, `Ciudad`, `Teléfono`. Deactivating is a destructive confirm whose body names the consequence in full — `Las existencias de la sede se conservan y su historial queda intacto. Las cajas de esta sede dejan de vender y sus dispositivos quedan sin sede activa.` — and whose button says `Desactivar sede`. **There is no delete**, at any role: a sede with sales in it is a sede whose deletion would take the sales with it.

**6 · Provisioning and the fleet view — Django admin** — §B.8.4·7 puts provisioning in Django admin, outside this design system entirely, with no styling budget and two requirements only: nothing in it is ever shown to a tenant user, and **a newly provisioned tenant lands on an empty state with an action, not on a blank Panel**. S1 already resolved which action — `Nuevo producto` with an explanatory line, departing from §B.8.4·7's `Cargar catálogo` because no tenant-facing import wizard exists — and S10 inherits that decision rather than re-opening it. **The fleet view** is a second Django-admin page under the same rule, and it is shaped around the one question support is actually asked. Its default sort is **silence, descending** — the quietest till at the top — because an operator opening it has already been told something is wrong and is looking for which device it is. One row per `active` device across every tenant: `Tenant · Sede · Equipo · Última sincronización · Último envío · Conflictos abiertos · Envíos a facturación pendientes · Almacenamiento · Versión` — where **`Envíos a facturación pendientes` is empty for a tenant with no invoicing target and never a zero**, because a zero on that column reads as *nothing is stuck* for a tenant that has nothing that could get stuck (§8) — with the relative-time ladder of §B.9.1 on both stamps and the absolute stamp beyond twelve hours. Above the table sit the two figures that are not per device — job queue depth and the oldest queued job — and **above everything, pinned, any non-zero projection drift from S3's nightly verify**, because that is the only line on the page that means a defect rather than a threshold. A tenant with no device over any threshold renders one line saying so and no table; an empty grid on an operational page is indistinguishable from a failed load.

**Impersonation** is a Django-admin action, not an application route. It selects one tenant, pins it for the session exactly as any other request is pinned, and writes an `audit_log` row into **that tenant's** log on entry, before anything else in the session is read (§2, S0). Three rules attach: it is one tenant at a time and there is no wildcard and no cross-tenant query (S0); it reaches what `owner` reaches inside the pinned tenant (§B.8.3) and no more; and the entry is written even when the session reads nothing, because the record a customer is owed is *we were in your data on this date*, not *we changed something*.

**States**, for every surface above:

- **Loading** — geometry-matched skeletons at the real heights (§B.10.1): the checklist as fifteen rows at the real 40px with a bar per cell at that column's real width; the Panel's compliance strip at the real §B.10.3 Region height with a `t-14` title bar and a `t-12` detail bar. A re-fetch after a filter or page change dims the existing rows to `opacity:0.6` with the 2px progress line and never blanks them. No spinner outside a pressed button — `Subiendo…`.
- **Empty**, three kinds, and conflating them is the defect (§B.10.2):
  - *Never populated* — `Todavía no hay requisitos configurados` / `Botica trae una lista base de lo que suele mantener una droguería. Puede activarla y ajustarla a lo que su red necesita.` / `Usar la lista base`, primary.
  - *Filtered to nothing* — `Ningún requisito coincide con estos filtros` / the active filters echoed verbatim / `Quitar filtros`, **secondary** — the intent was to filter.
  - *Deliberately empty* — `Nada vence en los próximos 30 días` / `14 requisitos al día · 3 marcados como no aplica.` / a ghost link to the full list. No action, because there is nothing for the reader to do.
- **Error** (§B.10.3) — a failed upload is field or region scope and names the operation, the entity and the recovery: **`No pudimos subir el soporte.`** `El archivo pesa 14,2 MB y el máximo es 10 MB. Reduzca el archivo o súbalo en dos partes.` `[Reintentar]`. Object storage unreachable is a region error over a still-readable table: **`Los soportes no están disponibles en este momento.`** `El almacenamiento de documentos no responde. Los requisitos, sus fechas y sus estados siguen visibles; volver a intentar la descarga en unos minutos.` — which is §10's stated failure behaviour rendered rather than restated. A failed load is route scope with a retry and a `user-select:all` correlation id. No raw exception and no `Ocurrió un error` anywhere.
- **Denied** (§B.8.3) — a `cashier` reaches no surface in this stage: the settings sections are not rendered for them, and a pasted URL refuses inside the content region naming the role required, never a redirect and never a disabled item. An `admin` sees the compliance section in full and does not see `Nueva sede`, `Desactivar sede` or `Archivar documento` — **not rendered, not greyed**.

**Numbers and dates** go through the §A.11 formatter without exception: `MM/AAAA` for an expiry, thousands dot, decimal comma, tabular numerals, `hace 3 días` on §B.9.1's relative-time ladder where a stamp is under 12 hours old and the absolute form beyond it.

## Offline

**Every surface this stage builds is an online-only office surface, and each says so plainly rather than showing a cached figure.** Compliance, provisioning, the fleet view and the settings sections are `owner`/`admin`/`platform_admin`, seated, server-authoritative, server-paginated, and none of them is in S2's sync registry — **this stage amends that registry in no way** (A4, rule 9). With no network the settings dialog's compliance section renders the route-scope error geometry with a retry and a correlation id (§B.10.3), and the Panel's compliance strip renders `Cumplimiento · —` with `sin conexión` on its detail line rather than the last figure it saw (§B.9.2 tier 3). A stale count of expiring documents is a number somebody would act on, and acting on it is exactly what the strip is for.

**That nothing here reaches a till is a decision, not an omission.** The obvious-looking feature — showing a cashier the sede's concepto sanitario at the counter — would put a table and a set of PDFs into every device's operating set for a document nobody reads while a customer waits, against A4's rule that what syncs is one sede's operating set and nothing else. **The counter's cold start budget is 2.5s and its delta pull is 20ms p95** (§4), and both are spent on catalog, prices, stock and safety rules. Compliance earns none of it. *If an inspector at a counter ever needs a document on screen*, the answer is a link an administrator opens on their own machine, not a sync-registry amendment.

**The one genuinely offline-shaped thing in this stage is a runbook entry, not a surface.** A browser that lost its local store is the failure mode the whole offline contract is exposed to, and it is item 4 below. Its handling is entirely procedural: what data was at risk, how to tell whether anything was actually lost, and what may never be done to the device before that question is answered.

**Object storage is the one vendor on this stage's path, and it is not on any counter's path** (§10, and the hard rule that no vendor sits on the counter's critical path). Its failure behaviour is fixed by §10 and rendered above: uploads retry, the vault is read-only while it is unreachable, and the checklist — the requirements, the dates, the states, the verification stamps — is entirely in Postgres and stays fully usable, because the part a regente most needs during an inspection is the list, not the file.

**The fleet view and the runbook are the two things that must work when the product does not.** The fleet view degrades to the `fleet_health` management command, which runs from a shell against the same database and prints the same table — so an operator whose browser cannot reach the admin still gets the answer. **And the runbook lives in the repository, not in the product.** An operator runbook stored inside the system it recovers is not a runbook; the six entries below are a file in the repo, checked in, and the alert emails link to it by path rather than by URL.

## Demo seed

**S0 owns the demo-seed command, its `--profile` argument and the identity fixture — tenants, locations, users and invitations; S1 registers the catalog; S10 registers the last fixture with that command** (§1, ledger, cross-stage services). The command is S0's rather than a later stage's because S0 creates the first seedable tables and its own verification needs a tenant to run against, and a stage whose checks depend on a command a later stage builds cannot be verified in the session that builds it. S10's fixture runs after every other stage's, because a checklist entry hangs off a `locations` row and a compliance document is evidence of nothing until there is a sede to hold it.

Under **`--profile default`** the fixture is small: **one checklist template applied to the six sedes, with its entries in a realistic spread** — most `done` and carrying an uploaded document, two `pending`, one `not_applicable`, and one whose document expires inside the warning horizon so the expiry flag is visible rather than theoretical. Plus a handful of `compliance_documents` whose files are self-evidently synthetic placeholders, never real scans of anything.

The seed's provisioning state matters as much as its rows: the seeded tenant is **fully provisioned with no invoicing target configured**, because that is the default and the state every demo runs in (§8). An operator opening the seeded tenant sees a complete network with the handoff's compliance figure on the Panel's region strip and no fiscal backlog anywhere, which is what a correctly configured pilot actually looks like on day one.

**What the other four profiles build.** S0's command takes `--profile` and each stage's fixture answers to the profile it is given (§1), so what S10 builds under each is written here rather than discovered by a session that runs one and finds an exception. **Compliance carries no history**, which is why two of the five are deliberately identical to `default`: a requirement is not something a tenant earns by selling.

| Profile | What S10's fixture builds |
|---|---|
| `default` | the six-sede spread above, its documents, and the tenant fully provisioned with **no invoicing target** |
| `young` | the same rows as `default`. Twelve days of sales changes nothing about what a droguería is expected to keep, and a fixture that scaled the checklist to the sales window would be inventing a relationship the product does not have |
| `cold` | the same rows again, and for the sharper reason: a tenant that has never sold still has a concepto sanitario. A compliance section that only renders on a tenant with sales is a section nobody has seen on a first morning — which is the one morning it is opened |
| `scale` | **the full ten-template default set activated across all twenty locations, with a year of periods rolled**, a vault large enough to paginate, and expiries spread across the horizon, beyond it and behind it. Seven of the twenty sedes carry a document already expired, so the Panel strip is in its **critical** family at the size the budget is measured at rather than at six healthy rows |
| `minimal` | the one sede's entries and two documents — enough that S0's isolation reads count against a table with rows in it, rather than two empty tables agreeing with each other |

**The `scale` entry count is not written down anywhere, and that is deliberate.** Ten templates over twenty sedes for a year is whatever the frequency mix produces — twelve periods for a monthly requirement, four for a quarterly one, one for an annual and one for an `on_expiry` — so a literal in this document would be wrong the first time a template's `frequency` changed, and a check asserting it would fail on a run where nothing was broken. Every check below asserts the grid's `rowCount` against `select count(*) from checklist_entries` instead, which is the seed's own count read from the product (§1).

**This stage's completion test is §1's**: the compliance section and the operator views render convincingly from the seed, with the expiry flag raised on the one document that earns it. A checklist screen reviewed only in its empty state is a screen nobody has seen.

## Verification

*Acceptance* says what must be true; this section is how a session ends. **Everything below runs on a tenant built by S0's `seed_demo_tenant` at `--profile default` unless the check names another profile, and on nothing else** (§1) — no client data, no invoicing target, no pilot hardware — because that is the state every session starts in. **An unusual state is reached by asking for the profile that builds it, never by editing rows out from under a healthy one**: a check that begins *first, delete some sales* is a check nobody runs twice (§1). And **every figure asserted below is the seed's own** — a count this stage's fixtures build, or a count the product itself reports through `GET /api/compliance/summary`, the grid's `rowCount`, `GET /api/fiscal-documents/summary` or a `select count(*)` run in the same session. Nothing here is asserted against S2's registry sizing or S3's row counts: those describe what a real pilot will hold, the seed holds what its fixtures built, and a check expecting the pilot's figure from the seed's database fails on every run for a reason that has nothing to do with this stage. S10 is the stage whose deliverables are easiest to *describe* instead of *perform*: two of the checks are the drills, and a session that reads them rather than running them has verified nothing.

### Gates

Automated. Nothing below them is looked at until all of them are green.

- **Lint and format, server and client.** Never interesting alone, and a gate anyway: a diff nobody can read is where the diff that matters hides.
- **Type-check, server and client, with the typed client regenerated from `/api/openapi.json` first.** A failure after S10's endpoints land is almost always `GET /api/compliance/summary` moving shape — the body S9's region strip is built against — or `POST /api/locations` gaining a field the Sedes screen does not send.
- **The test suite.** Read the failing name before anything else: under the expiry sweep or the roll job it is an idempotency property (a digest that sent twice, a period that instantiated twice); under the vault it is the *row only after the object exists* rule; anywhere else it is usually a fixture that assumed an invoicing target was configured.
- **The client production build**, with the settings dialog's compliance section in it. A route that does not compile makes every bundle check below meaningless.
- **Migrations forward, then backward, against a database already carrying S0–S9's rows.** Three tables, three enums and four indexes must all roll back, and `locations`, `tenants` and `audit_log` must be left exactly as S0 shipped them. A reverse that leaves `checklist_frequency` or `compliance_document_type` behind means S10 can no longer be rolled back on its own — the last stage is the one nobody notices this about.
- **`seed_demo_tenant --profile default` completes twice in a row from an empty database and S10's fixture lands** — the six sedes' entries in the spread *Demo seed* names, with every seeded document's object actually present in storage. A row whose object is missing is exactly what the upload order exists to prevent, and the seed is where it surfaces. **`--profile scale` and `--profile minimal` complete too**, landing the twenty sedes' full template set with a year of periods and the one sede's entries respectively (*Demo seed*) — the budget check and the isolation check below each run on a profile that would otherwise never have been asked of this fixture, and a fixture that assumes six locations breaks on the first of them.
- **The default template set is data.** The ten `is_default` rows load as fixtures rather than code, and `activate-defaults` run twice against one tenant leaves the same entry count both times — the table's unique key doing the work, not a guard in the endpoint.
- **The sync registry is identical to S2's** (A4, ledger rule 9). Not "no compliance table is declared" — the declared collection list compared against the list before this stage's first commit. S10 is the last chance to put a table on a till by accident.
- **No module in this stage imports the ledger service or writes `stock_moves` or `stock_on_hand`** (A3, ledger rule 7), and exactly one database read in the whole stage runs outside a pin — Procrastinate's queue depth. Two is a defect, not a convenience.
- **Bundle grep.** `Botica guarda los soportes y avisa cuando vencen. No presenta reportes ante ninguna autoridad.` appears once as a literal; `Cumplido`, `Pendiente` and `No aplica` once each; and `pg_dump`, a backup schedule or a restore path appears nowhere in the worker (§9).

| S9 · four KPI cards and a 1,2s Panel (§4) | a fifth card, or a second live strip costing more than the budget allows | on `--profile scale`, the first row is still four cards at `repeat(4,1fr)` and the Panel paints under 1,2s p95 at its twenty sedes with both strips rendering |

| S2 · `devices`, read-only here | the fleet view or provisioning stamping `last_seen_at`, `status` or `storage_persisted` | the latest `updated_at` on `devices` is unchanged across a fleet-view load, an hourly sweep and a `fleet_health` run |
| S5 · silence where no target is configured (§8) | the sweep, the fleet view or the go-live check deciding for itself whether the handoff is on | on the seeded unconfigured tenant after a full sweep: `fiscal_documents` empty, no delivery job queued, the fleet column empty rather than `0`, and `handoff_enabled` called from exactly the three places S5 calls it |
| S1 · `items`, read-only here (ledger, disputed columns) | the expiry board or the sweep writing `invima_status` on a registration it found expired | latest `updated_at` on `items` unchanged across an expiry-board load and a `compliance.check_expiries` run |
| S0 · `tenants.settings` (ledger rule 5) | writing the `compliance` group read-modify-write and clobbering a neighbour | set a value in `sync`, PATCH `compliance`, re-read `sync`: unchanged. A PATCH matching no row raises rather than passing quietly |
| S0 · tenant pinning in every context (A1, ledger rule 6) | a job, a command or the fleet view touching the database unpinned | run the three jobs, `provision_tenant` and `fleet_health` with nothing pinned: each **exits non-zero** and writes nothing, rather than reporting success over a transaction `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` silently emptied — check 5. Exactly one read in the stage is legitimately unpinned |
| S0 · `locations`, whose second writer this stage becomes | a create from the settings section leaving a column the Django-admin path sets, or skipping the audit row | create and edit a sede from both surfaces: one `audit_log` row per mutation, and the two rows in `locations` have the same shape with no column null on one path and set on the other |
| S4 · the counter's budgets (§4) | anything in this stage reaching the till's path, where nothing of it belongs | `Cobrar` under 200ms p95 and cold start under 2.5s, both measured with object storage unreachable |

## Hands off

S10 is the last stage and blocks nothing. What it hands off is handed to whoever operates the platform rather than to a next build.

- **The compliance vault and the checklist**, with the boundary written into the product itself: Botica holds the evidence and warns on expiry, and files, computes and certifies nothing (§12). Every future conversation about compliance starts from that sentence, on screen, in Spanish.
- **`compliance_documents`, `checklist_templates` and `checklist_entries`**, with a default template set that is data rather than code, and `checklist_templates.code` as the stable English join key any later report or import uses.
- **The `/api/settings/compliance` group** — `checklist_template_set`, `expiry_warning_days`, `escalation_days`, `notification_recipients` — read by the Panel strip, the sweep and the digest, and never copied into a constant.
- **Provisioning as a written flow and a `provision_tenant` command**, with the two non-automatable steps named — opening stock needs a physical count, and a device claim needs a person at the till — and with the invoicing handoff as an **optional** step a tenant may skip and still be fully provisioned (§8). Anyone who later automates either of the two is changing a decision rather than filling a gap.
- **The go-live check** — one list an operator answers before a network sells, covering catalog, opening stock, prices, devices, users, home sedes, the checklist and the settings groups, with the handoff line answered *no target configured* rather than left blank on the instances that have none.
- **The fleet view and the `fleet_health` command**, built as N pinned reads, honouring S0's refusal of a wildcard pin. Any future cross-tenant surface takes the same shape or takes the ledger through a change.
- **Five signals with thresholds, an owner and an escalation**, of which one — sync lag — reuses S2's number rather than inventing a second, one — job queue depth — is the only legitimately unpinned read in the product, and one — the handoff backlog — is **absent rather than green** on an instance with no invoicing target configured.
- **A restore that has been performed, timed and written down**, with its measured data-loss window. That number, not the backup schedule, is what a customer is being promised.
- **A rebuild that has been rehearsed on a live tenant**, which is what turns A3 from an architectural claim into an operational fact.
- **The runbook, in the repository**, covering the six failures below, the two drills, and the prohibitions attached to each — the half that is usually missing and the half that prevents a fast wrong answer.

### The runbook — the six failures that will actually happen

**1 · A till that will not sync.** *What support hears:* "la caja dice sin conexión desde ayer" — or nothing at all, and the fleet view found it first. *First read:* the fleet view's row for that device — `last_seen_at` versus `last_synced_at` versus `last_pushed_at`, `status`, `app_version`, `clock_skew_ms`, `storage_persisted` — then S2's device record panel for its open conflicts. Seen but not pushing is a rejection, not a network problem; not seen at all is the shop's connection or a revoked key. *The fix:* a revoked device is re-claimed at the till and keeps its outbox; a session expired shows `degraded · sesión vencida` and is fixed by signing in; a rejected batch names its reason in `sync_conflicts`. *Never:* clear the browser's storage, reinstall the device, or revoke and re-claim **before** the outbox has drained — the unsent sales live only there, and the till is the only place that knows what physically happened in the shop (§5 rule 1).

**2 · A handoff target that refuses or times out.** *What support hears:* usually nothing. The handoff-backlog signal found it, or an administrator opened their own work list, and **no cashier saw anything at any point** — nothing about the handoff reaches a counter, by design (S5, §8). *First read:* S5's `Envíos a facturación` work list, whose segmented control is `Pendientes` · `Fallidos` · `Ventas sin enviar`, and which of the three holds the rows is most of the diagnosis. `Pendientes` growing with `Intentos` climbing is the target unreachable or slow and the retry ladder still working, which needs patience rather than action. `Fallidos` is the target refusing, and each row's `Motivo` says what a person must do — `El adquiriente no tiene número de documento` is a data fix in the sale's customer, `El sistema de facturación respondió 503` is a wait. `Ventas sin enviar` is a closed sale that produced no document at all, which is the orphan check reporting a defect rather than a delivery problem. Then the settings section's 24-hour read-out, and — where the credential is the suspect — whether the target block reports it resolving at all. *The fix:* correct the cause where the cause lives, in the sale's customer, in the catalog, in the mapping or in the credential in the instance's secrets store, and press `Reintentar`, which **rebuilds the payload from the sale as it now stands**. A mapping fix re-renders every stuck row on its next attempt; no fiscal row is edited and no migration is written. **The sales themselves are never at risk.** The document is a derivation of the sale, not a stored artefact, so a target that was down for a day costs a queue and nothing else — every affected sale is still in the database, still complete, and can be rendered and delivered later. *Never:* re-send blind after a timeout. An ambiguous outcome is resolved by a `query` on `document_key` and never by a second delivery, because a duplicate at the far end is signed, numbered and filed under the pharmacy's own resolution and costs a credit note to unwind, while a missing one costs a retry (§8). And never edit a `fiscal_documents` row to make a delivery look like it landed — `failed` is not terminal and `Reintentar` is the correction. **No state on that screen is a DIAN outcome:** `Confirmado` says the client's system holds our document, and Botica never learns what was filed with it.

**3 · A projection that has drifted.** *What support hears:* nothing — this one is found by the machine. S3's nightly `inventory.projection_verify` reported a non-zero difference between `stock_on_hand` and the sum of `stock_moves`, and the fleet view has it pinned above every threshold signal because it is the only line on that page that means a **defect** rather than a number crossing. *First read:* the `audit_log` row the verify job wrote — the tenant, the location, the items and the size of the drift — then `stock_moves` around those items for the window, looking for the quantity that has no move behind it. The question being answered is **which code path wrote outside the ledger service** (rule 7), and it is answerable precisely because every legitimate change to stock is an append carrying an actor and a reason. *The fix:* find the write path and fix it; only then rebuild the affected location through S3's operation, recording the before and after exactly as the drill does. *Never:* run the rebuild as the response. A rebuild recomputes the projection from the moves and makes the symptom disappear, which erases the evidence of the path that caused it and guarantees a second occurrence nobody can trace (rule 7, A3). And never correct `stock_on_hand` by hand at any role — a projection edited directly is a projection the next verify contradicts.

**4 · A browser that lost its local store.** *What support hears:* "se borró todo en la caja" — the till is at its first-run screen, or its ticket is gone. *First read:* before anything else, **`storage_persisted` on that device**. If it is false or null, `navigator.storage.persist()` was denied or never answered and the browser was free to evict; if it is true, this is a wipe by a person or by policy. Then `last_pushed_at`: everything pushed is on the server and is not lost, and the gap between `last_pushed_at` and the incident is the exposure. *The fix:* re-claim the browser as the same device, let the first sync repopulate the operating set, and reconcile the gap from the shop's own paper — sales that never reached the server cannot be recovered from us, and saying so quickly is better than looking for them. *Never:* tell a pharmacy the data is recoverable before reading `last_pushed_at`, and never leave a device running with `storage_persisted` false — that state is `degraded` with its own named reason (§B.9.4) and it is the one technical browser detail this product puts in front of a cashier for exactly this reason.

**5 · A pharmacy that changed its resolución.** *What support hears:* "la DIAN nos dio una resolución nueva". *What it actually is:* a compliance-vault event and nothing more. **Botica holds no resolution, allocates no fiscal number and has no range to update** (§8, A6) — the resolución is a paper the pharmacy obtained and keeps, the same category as its concepto sanitario, and the number on an invoice comes from the system the pharmacy already invoices with. *First read:* the `Resolución de facturación vigente` row on `Ajustes · Registros · Cumplimiento` for that sede, with its attached document and its `Vence`. *The fix:* attach the new PDF through the document's `Reemplazar` action, which archives the old row, links the two by `replaced_by_id`, and **re-stamps `expires_at` on every checklist entry that pointed at it** — one action in one row, and the Panel's compliance figure follows on its next read. Nothing else in the product changes: no endpoint, no device, no settings key, and no sede-level configuration of any kind. *Never:* archive the previous resolución instead of replacing it. The vault exists so that an inspection asking what was in force on a date in the past gets an answer, and the resolution that was dropped rather than superseded is the one an inspector asks about.

**6 · A location that opened.** *What support hears:* "abrimos una sede nueva el lunes". *The sequence, in order, and none of it is improvised:* create the sede in the settings section, which instantiates its checklist entries; confirm the catalog and prices apply — they are network-wide by default and usually need nothing; run S3's opening-stock run against a physical count; attach the sede's own papers to its new checklist entries, the concepto sanitario and the resolución de facturación first; claim each till at the till itself; assign the cashiers their home sede; and answer the go-live check before the first sale. **Nothing about the handoff is per sede:** the `invoicing` group is one per tenant, so the new sede's sales reach whatever target the network already uses, or none at all where none is configured — which is a supported state and not a step somebody forgot (§8). *Never:* copy another sede's `stock_on_hand` as a starting point. Opening stock is a counted fact and is entered as an adjusting move through the ledger service (rule 7, A3); a projection seeded by hand is a projection the first rebuild will silently correct, and the discrepancy will be discovered by a pharmacist counting boxes rather than by the system.

## Gated on

- **§11.6 — which sede goes first, and who at the client owns the catalog cleanup.** This is the gate on S10 and the architecture already calls it the usual schedule risk. It does not block writing or building this stage; it blocks the stage being *finished*, because provisioning is only demonstrable against a real network and the flow's fourth step is S1's load tool running on real data. **The schedule risk is not engineering.** Steps 1, 2, 3 and 8 of the provisioning flow take an hour; step 4 takes as long as the client's catalog cleanup takes, and nobody on our side can shorten it. It is worth asking in the specific form the flow needs an answer to: *which sede, on what date, whose person owns the product file, and who at the pharmacy can produce the concepto sanitario and the resolución* — because the last two are checklist entries on the first day, and a go-live check with two blank lines is a go-live that slips.
- **§11.5 — the pilot's connectivity and hardware**, partially. The five signal thresholds are chosen from the architecture's own numbers and from S2's and S5's settings, not from measurement. A pilot on a connection that drops for six hours at a time will make the 48-hour sync-lag threshold correct and the four-hour push-failure threshold noisy; a pilot on a good connection will make both quiet. **They are settings-shaped but are not settings in v1** — they are constants in the sweep, deliberately, because a threshold an operator can turn down is a threshold that gets turned down until nothing alerts. The first pilot month is what re-decides them, in one edit.
- **Nobody has set an RPO or an RTO.** The restore drill measures what the current backup arrangement actually delivers, which is the right first move, but the number it produces has nothing to be compared against. **A droguería that loses four hours of sales loses four hours of money and a day of trust**, and that is the sentence the target should be argued from. This is a commercial decision, not an engineering one, and it belongs in the same conversation as the pilot contract.
- **Document retention.** `compliance_documents` are the pharmacy's own business papers rather than personal data, so Ley 1581's sensitive-data reasoning (§7, §11.3) does not reach them. What is unanswered is how long a document is kept after it is archived and after a tenant leaves, and whether the object-storage lifecycle policy — a deploy concern — matches whatever the answer is. **The application currently deletes nothing, ever**, which is the safe default and is stated rather than assumed.
- **The default checklist set is a starting list, not a legal opinion**, and nobody at the client has confirmed it. The ten templates are drawn from what a Colombian droguería is commonly expected to keep; they are `is_default` rows a tenant edits, they are not asserted to be complete, and the section carries no claim that answering them constitutes compliance. **A pharmacist or the client's regente should review the list once**, and if the answer is that it is materially wrong, the fix is ten rows of seed data and no code.
- **The ledger assigns S10 no platform-scoped table.** The fleet view therefore iterates tenants and issues one pinned read per tenant per signal, which honours S0's refusal of a wildcard pin and is comfortably fast at pilot scale. *If a fleet of hundreds of tenants ever makes that too slow*, the answer is a snapshot table written by a per-tenant job and read outside any pin — and that table needs a ledger row before it exists, which is why it is named here rather than invented.
- **`imports` is written by S1 and S6 only** (ledger). Provisioning triggers both loaders and records its own run in `audit_log` instead. If a provisioning run should appear in the imports history beside the catalog and sales-history loads, the ledger must grant S10 a write on `imports`; S10 does not assume it. This is the same residual S7 recorded for its cap loader, and the two should be settled together or not at all.
- **Whether the compliance figure eventually earns a KPI card of its own — a request to S9, not a change S10 makes.** The handoff draws four KPI tiles (Pantalla 1) and §B.8.4·6 calls the compliance figure a *tile*; S9 reconciles the two by shipping the region-strip contribution point above the KPI row, and that is where S10 renders it. The Panel's first row therefore stays four tiles at `repeat(4,1fr)` and S10 re-lays nothing S9 owns. **The residual is a matter of weight, not of ownership:** a figure a regente is expected to act on every month reads more quietly in a strip than in a card, and if the pilot shows expiry notices being passed over on the Panel, **the fix is a request to S9 to promote it to a fifth card** — S9's screen, S9's `repeat(5,1fr)`, and a change the handoff's sign-off has to absorb. Either way the notification path is untouched: the digest and the escalation reach the recipients in the settings group whether or not anybody looks at the Panel, which is why the strip is the safe default to ship on.
- **Newly coined names**, all English, all in the register of the schema around them, none supplied by architecture.md §3 or the ledger: the enums `compliance_document_type`, `checklist_status` (values are the ledger's, the type name is not) and `checklist_frequency`; the `compliance_documents` columns `content_type`, `size_bytes`, `issued_at`, `replaced_by_id`, `archived_at` and `uploaded_by_email`; the `checklist_templates` columns `is_default`, `active`, `sort_order` and `notice_days`; the `checklist_entries` columns `period_start`, `notes` and `verified_by_email`; the settings key `escalation_days` (`checklist_template_set`, `expiry_warning_days` and `notification_recipients` are the register's own words made into keys); the jobs `compliance.check_expiries`, `compliance.roll_checklist` and `operations.health_sweep`; the commands `provision_tenant` and `fleet_health`; and the ten `checklist_templates.code` values in the default set. Any of them is one migration away from a different name while S10 is the only stage that reads them.
