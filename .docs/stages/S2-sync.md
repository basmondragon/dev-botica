# S2 — Sync

## Outcome

A browser at a counter becomes a **device**. A cashier signs in at a till for the first time, names it, gives it a sede, and the browser downloads that sede's operating set — the network's catalog, its barcodes, its laboratorios, its categorías, that sede's prices and the network's clientes — into durable local storage, once, with visible progress. From that moment the till reads its catalog at zero latency whether or not the network is there, and every screen a till renders carries one line saying how current what it is reading is: `Sincronizado hace 4 s` when nothing is pending, and something specific and actionable when something is (§5, design-system.md §B.9.1).

The write half is proven at the same time, on the one client-originated write that exists this early: a cashier registers a customer at the counter with the network unplugged, and the row sits in a local outbox until the connection returns, at which point it arrives on the server exactly once — including when the push times out after the server already committed it (A5). The same batch-apply service that accepts it is the one S3's stock moves and S4's sales will call, so the path that carries the money is exercised three stages before any money is on it.

An owner sees the network's tills from the office: which device is at which sede, when each last synchronised, whether each one's browser has promised not to evict its data, what version each is running, and how far each one's clock is from the server's. A device that has gone quiet for two days is on that list saying so. A device that was stolen or reassigned is revoked from that list, and stops synchronising on its next attempt.

And one thing is *not* possible at the end of this stage, deliberately: a browser cannot pull another sede's rows, and an office browser cannot pull anything at all. The set of tables that reaches a device, and the predicate that scopes each one to a location, is one declared registry (rule 9, A4). At the pilot's catalog size that registry is about 4,6 MB on the wire and under 12 MB on disk; the same registry with one predicate removed would be a 20-sede network syncing itself into a laptop, and the guard against that is installed here rather than discovered at a pilot.

## Scope

### In

1. **`devices`** — a browser install that sells, carrying its sede, its label, its `device_key`, its status, both freshness stamps, its reported clock skew, its storage-persistence state and its application version (§3, ledger). The unit of sync and of blame. It carries no fiscal role: numbering leases are not built at v1 (A6, deferred), so a device is never the holder of a range.
2. **Device enrolment.** A signed-in `cashier` on an unclaimed browser claims it: label and sede, then the persistence request. The server creates the row and returns a `device_key` **once**, stored hashed at rest. An `owner` or `admin` may also claim the browser they are sitting at, explicitly, from the device list — but is never asked to (see 4).
3. **`device_key` as the device credential.** Every `/api/sync/*` call carries the session *and* the key: the session resolves tenant and identity, the key resolves the device and therefore the location. Neither alone is sufficient, and the location is **never** a client-supplied parameter (A4, rule 6).
4. **An office browser is never a device and never replicates.** Enrolment is offered when a `cashier` signs in, not when an `owner` does. This is A4's boundary made operational: the counter is local-first over its own sede, the office is server-authoritative over the network and is never synced into a browser (§4).
5. **The sync registry** — one declared artefact naming every collection that reaches a device, its predicate, its scope, its push idempotency key, whether a device may write it, and a row-count and byte estimate per location (rule 9). A table absent from the registry does not reach a device, and a stage that wants one there amends the registry in this document with its own estimate.
6. **`GET /api/sync/pull`** — a stateless, per-collection delta over a **`(updated_at, id)` tuple cursor**, ordered and indexed on the same tuple, with a safety horizon that excludes rows too recently written to be safely ordered. The checkpoint is held by the client and validated, never stored server-side.
7. **`POST /api/sync/push`** — a batch applied inside one pinned transaction, returning a per-row outcome, deduplicated on `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` for every rule-8 table and on a declared natural key for a collection that has one (A5, rule 8).
8. **The idempotent client-write service** — the cross-stage service the ledger names S2 the owner of. Dedupe, one-transaction batch apply, per-row outcomes. S3, S4, S5 and S8 call it; none of them dedupes by hand (rule 8, cross-stage services).
9. **Batch rejection semantics**, decided here: a row naming another tenant or another location rejects the **whole batch**; a row that fails domain validation is rejected **on its own** and does not stop the rest draining. The reasoning and the consequence are in *Data*.
10. **Both clocks, kept and surfaced.** `occurred_at` from the device and `recorded_at` from the server are stored on every client-originated write (rule 8). The device reports its wall clock on every sync call, the server computes the skew, stores it on `devices` and returns its own time; skew is displayed and **never** used to correct `occurred_at` (§5 rule 4).
11. **`sync_conflicts`** — the office's arrival queue for anything the protocol refused or could not settle. S2 creates it and writes the protocol-level rows; S3 writes the negative-stock rows and S4 writes `stale_price` and `catalog_divergence`, which is why the ledger's `Writes` column on this table reads S2, S3, S4 and why all three of §5's named reconciliations have a writer.
12. **`navigator.storage.persist()`**, requested at claim, its result reported to the server on every sync call and displayed both on the device and in the office list (§5, design-system.md §B.9.4).
13. **Multi-tab over `BroadcastChannel`** — `multiInstance`, one elected leader owning the replication, every tab reading one store and rendering one `SyncStatus` (§5).
14. **Local retention and compaction** — what a till keeps, for how long, and the rule that nothing unpushed is ever compacted at any age.
15. **The `SyncStatus` component and its five states** — `synced`, `pending`, `offline`, `degraded`, `blocked` — rendered in Spanish, at rest as text with no dot, with only `blocked` interrupting (design-system.md §B.9.1). S2 ships the component, the state machine and the named degraded reasons; every later till surface renders it and none re-implements it.
16. **The sync panel** (design-system.md §B.9.3) — the read-out behind the status line, opened by click or `F8` (§B.13.3).
17. **The outbox** — a local-only collection holding every unpushed client write with its attempt count and last error. It is not replicated, has no server counterpart, and is the only durable record of a write the server has not seen.
18. **The poll schedule and its backoff** — when a till pulls, how it backs off when the server is unreachable, and what makes it pull immediately.
19. **The device and sync surfaces** — the claim card, the first-sync screen, the sync panel, and the devices half of the **Ajustes · Sedes y dispositivos** section including the `sync` settings group.
20. **The `sync` settings key group** — pull interval, page and batch sizes, the safety horizon, local retention, the clock-skew threshold, the customer recency window and the storage-persistence policy (rule 5, ledger's settings register).
21. **The delta cursor indexes** on every registry collection, migrated onto tables S2 does not own, under rule 4, with the query each one serves stated.
22. **The module boundary** §5 requires: all server sync code behind `core/sync/`, all client sync code in one module, and no domain code calling the replication API directly — so that if §4's measurements ever justify an engine, it replaces that module rather than the product.
23. **One job** — a daily stale-device check that raises a `sync_conflicts` row for a till that has stopped talking.

- **Selling — the ticket, the scanner path, turnos, payments, returns, and the Mostrador screen** — **S4**. S2 leaves that route empty; what a till can do offline at the end of S2 is read its catalog and register a customer.
- **The client-write quartet on `sales`, `sale_lines`, `payments`, `sale_returns`, `sale_return_lines`, `shifts`** — **S4**, and on `stock_moves`, `stock_counts`, `stock_count_lines` — **S3**, and on `assistant_queries`, `assistant_suggestions` — **S8** (rule 8, disputed columns). S2 owns the **convention and the helper**; the columns belong to each table's creator (rule 1).

Botica issues no fiscal document, so it allocates no fiscal number, so no range can be exhausted (§8); the lease was `blocked`'s only producer, and it is deferred with everything else on this line. S2 still defines the state, its banner and its copy, because retrofitting the product's only interruption into a state machine a cashier has already learned is worse than defining it unused, and because the day a till genuinely must stop is not the day to design that message from scratch. **Nothing at v1 raises it.**

- **`cross_sell_rules` and `item_warnings` on the device** — **S8**, which amends the registry under rule 9 with its own per-location cap (A8, disputed columns).
- **Conflict *resolution* surfaces.** S2 raises and lists conflicts; the counting screen that resolves an oversell is **S3** (§5), and the price and catalog divergences that arrive with an offline sale are **S4** (ledger, `sync_conflicts` — Writes S2, S3, S4).
- **The service worker and the offline application shell** — **S0** ships the shell; the Workbox registration for it is S0's too, and **the service worker never caches API responses** (§9). S2 does not add a second cache, because two caches are two truths.
- **`daily_metrics`, the Panel, and any aggregate read** — **S9**. Nothing aggregate is ever synced to a browser (§4, A4).
- **A sync engine** — PowerSync, ElectricSQL, logical replication, a second datastore. Ruled out in §5 with the measurement that would change the answer. All sync code lives behind `core/sync/` on the server and one client module; no domain code calls the replication API directly (§5).
- **Redis, pgbouncer, `LISTEN/NOTIFY` over SSE.** Polling is the transport at v1. §4 fixes the three measurements that promote SSE — sustained delta-pull load above ~500 req/s on one instance, p95 on the delta endpoint above 50 ms, or Panel queries a rollup cannot flatten — and none is answerable before the pilot (§11.5).
- **Responsive behaviour below 1280 × 720** — open with design (design-system.md §B.11, §B.17·2).

### The cursor, and why `updated_at` alone loses rows

**The cursor is the tuple `(updated_at, id)`, compared lexicographically and strictly**: `(updated_at, id) > (cursor.updated_at, cursor.id)`, ordered by the same tuple, served by the index above. Two distinct failures are closed by that shape, and each of them silently loses rows rather than erroring.

**Same-instant ties.** Postgres timestamps to the microsecond and a bulk catalog load writes thousands of rows inside one statement. A cursor of `updated_at > $C` skips every row that shares the last-seen timestamp; a cursor of `updated_at >= $C` re-serves them forever and never advances when the tie set is larger than one page. The tuple is a total order over rows, so a page always ends somewhere the next page can start, however many rows share an instant — which is precisely why the ledger's index carries `id` as its last column (rule 4).

**Commit ordering.** `updated_at` is stamped when the statement runs; the row becomes visible when the transaction commits. A slow transaction that stamps `10:00:01` and commits at `10:00:04` becomes visible *after* a fast transaction that stamped `10:00:02` and committed at `10:00:02`. A device that pulled at `10:00:03` advances its cursor past `10:00:02` and never sees the slow row again. **The pull therefore serves only rows at or below a horizon** — `updated_at <= server_now - pull_safety_horizon_seconds`, default 2 s — and S2 requires that `updated_at` on a registry table is stamped as late as possible in its writing transaction and that no transaction writing a registry table runs longer than the horizon. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a bulk catalog import that holds a transaction for ten seconds can write rows that no till ever pulls, and nothing raises. Three things contain it: S1's load tool commits in batches rather than in one transaction; the horizon is a setting, measured against the longest observed write transaction on registry tables at the pilot; and the client runs a daily digest check (below) that detects divergence and re-pulls from zero, which turns a silent permanent loss into a one-day-late repair.

**The digest check.** `GET /api/sync/digest` returns, per collection, the row count and a checksum over `(id, updated_at)` for that device's predicate at the horizon. The client compares it against its local store once a day on first idle. A mismatch resets that collection's checkpoint to zero and re-pulls it. This is the backstop that makes the horizon an engineering choice rather than a bet, and it costs one indexed aggregate per collection per device per day.

### The registry

**Version 1.** The declared set of collections that reach a device, and nothing else reaches one (rule 9, A4). `$T` is the pinned tenant; `$L` is the device's location, resolved from the `device_key` and **never from a request parameter**.

| Collection | Predicate | Scope | Push | Idempotency key | Rows / location | ≈ Bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `items` | `tenant_id = $T AND active` | tenant-wide | no | — | 4.284 | 1,80 MB |
| `item_barcodes` | `tenant_id = $T AND item_id IN (registry items)` | tenant-wide | no | — | 6.900 | 0,55 MB |
| `manufacturers` | `tenant_id = $T` | tenant-wide | no | — | 120 | 11 KB |
| `categories` | `tenant_id = $T` | tenant-wide | no | — | 80 | 6 KB |
| `item_prices` | `tenant_id = $T AND (location_id = $L OR location_id IS NULL) AND (effective_to IS NULL OR effective_to > now())` | **location** | no | — | 4.400 | 0,48 MB |
| `customers` | `tenant_id = $T AND updated_at >= now() - $customer_recency_months` | tenant-wide, windowed | **yes** | `(tenant_id, document_type, document)` | 9.000 | 1,71 MB |
| **Total** | | | | | **≈ 24.800** | **≈ 4,6 MB** |

Gzipped on the wire that is roughly **1,2 MB**; on disk, with RxDB's own indexes and revision metadata, budget **twice the document bytes — under 12 MB**, which is the number *Acceptance* measures. A first sync on the pilot's connection is one figure nobody can fake in a meeting, and it is criterion 4.

**Why `locations` is not in the registry.** A till needs its own sede's name and code, and it gets them from `/api/me` and the claim response, cached with the device record in local storage. Replicating a six-row table to obtain one row of it is a collection to version, compact and reason about for no gain. **What breaks if this is wrong:** when S3 renders `Quiebre · hay 96 en Suba`, the till needs every sede's name, not just its own — at which point S3 adds `locations` to the registry as a tenant-wide collection of about 20 rows and 2 KB, under rule 9. That is a one-line amendment, and it is the right stage to make it, because S3 is the stage that first reads another sede's anything.

**Why `customers` is windowed rather than whole.** A network's customer list has no ceiling and a till's storage does. The window is `updated_at` inside `customer_recency_months` — a proxy, because at S2 there are no sales to key recency on. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a customer who last bought three years ago is not found offline by document number and the cashier creates a second row for them; the natural-key merge on arrival collapses the two, so the cost is one duplicate that resolves itself rather than a split history. **S4 improves the predicate** to *has a sale at this location inside the window, or was updated inside it*, as a registry amendment under rule 9, once `sales.customer_id` exists.

**What the registry is guarding against.** At the pilot's six sedes the whole of S2's registry is 4,6 MB, and that is comfortable. The same figures with one predicate dropped are not: S3's `stock_on_hand` at item × location × lot grain runs to roughly one row per catalog item per sede before lot splits — about 4.284 at the pilot's catalog size — and around 7.000 with them. **This is production sizing per sede, and it is not the 4.284 S3's fixture quotes**, which is the seeded network's whole grid across six sedes and happens to land on the same number because the seed does not stock every item at every sede. Scoped to one location that is about 0,8 MB; scoped to one location that is about 0,8 MB, and unscoped across a 20-sede network it is 140.000 rows and some 16 MB — three times the entire catalog, for data nineteen sedes' worth of which the till never reads, arriving over a connection that drops several times a week (§4, A4). Nothing about that failure announces itself in code review. It announces itself as a pilot till taking ninety seconds to start, which is why the guard is a declared table with numbers in it rather than a principle.

### Batch semantics

A push is `{batch_id, client_time, rows: [{collection, client_uuid, occurred_at, payload}]}`, capped at `push_batch_max_rows` and `push_batch_max_bytes`, applied inside **one transaction pinned to the tenant the session resolves** (rule 6, fourth context). Rows are applied in `client_uuid` order, which is chronological because `client_uuid` is uuid v7 (A5). The response carries a per-row outcome and the server's own time.

| Outcome | Means | The client does |
|---|---|---|
| `applied` | The row was inserted | Removes it from the outbox |
| `duplicate` | `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` or the natural key already existed; the existing id is returned | **Removes it from the outbox.** `duplicate` is a success. A client that treats it as an error can never drain a queue whose push timed out after the server committed, which is the exact failure A5 exists to make safe |
| `merged` | A natural-key collision resolved onto an existing row, whose id is returned | Rewrites its local row's id and removes it from the outbox |
| `rejected` | Domain validation refused it, with a reason code | **Removes it from the outbox** and surfaces it. A row retried forever is a `degraded` state that never clears |

**A row naming another tenant, or a location this device is not at, rejects the whole batch** with no rows applied, and writes a `foreign_tenant` or `foreign_location` conflict (rule 6). It is filtered out of nothing: filtering would apply the remaining rows from a client we have just established we cannot trust about which tenant it is in. A domain rejection is the opposite case and is per-row, because one malformed customer must not wedge nine good sales behind it. **What breaks if this line is drawn wrong:** draw it too strictly and a single poisoned row stops a till syncing until an engineer intervenes; draw it too loosely and a compromised or badly broken client writes into a tenant it does not belong to. The split above puts integrity failures on the batch and data failures on the row, and criterion 11 checks both halves.

**Idempotency has exactly two forms and no third.** Every table in rule 8's list dedupes on `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` with a unique index, created by the table's owner and enforced by this stage's helper. A collection outside that list dedupes on a **declared natural key** — for `customers`, `(tenant_id, document_type, document)` — and that is ledger rule 8's second paragraph as a general rule, not an exception this stage granted itself: a table with neither a `client_uuid` nor a declared natural key is not pushable, and the push endpoint rejects it as the registry does. This is why the push path is real at S2 rather than a fixture: `customers` has a genuine natural key and a genuine merge semantics, and the quartet path is built here and first carries production traffic at S3.

**The outbox is local-only and has no server counterpart.** It holds every write the server has not confirmed, with the collection, the payload, the `client_uuid`, `occurred_at`, an attempt count and the last error. It is never replicated, never compacted while it has contents, and is the reason a device may be revoked, relabelled, moved between sedes or wiped of its reference collections without losing a write. **What breaks if it is put on the server instead:** the server becomes a second record of what a till has done, which is precisely the shape §5 rule 1 forbids — the till's store is a server snapshot plus its own pending events, and the pending events are the half the server does not have yet.

### The poll schedule

A till pulls every `pull_interval_seconds` (default 8) while the tab is visible and the leader, and every 60 s while the tab is hidden. It pulls **immediately** on four events: the browser's `online` event, the tab regaining focus, a successful push, and `Sincronizar ahora`. When a pull or a push fails on the network it backs off exponentially from the interval to a 60 s ceiling with jitter, and it resets the moment one call succeeds — jitter matters because twenty tills that lost the same link would otherwise return in lockstep. It does **not** back off on a `rejected` row, which is a data outcome and not a transport failure.

**The push runs before the pull in every cycle.** A till that has something to say says it before it listens, so a sale is on the server at the earliest possible moment and the pull that follows already reflects it. From S3 this also means the stock consequences of a till's own sale arrive on the same round trip that sent it.

At 20 sedes with two tills each and an 8 s interval, that is 40 devices × 6 collections ÷ 8 s ≈ **30 pull requests per second**, each an indexed tuple scan usually returning zero rows. §4's promotion threshold for `LISTEN/NOTIFY` over SSE is ~500 req/s or a p95 above 50 ms, and this is an order of magnitude below both — which is the arithmetic behind §4's "no Redis" rather than a restatement of it. A client pulling all six collections in one request would cut that to five per second; it is not done, because RxDB replicates per collection and a merged endpoint would trade a measured non-problem for a bespoke protocol.

## API surface

Every path carries the `/api/` prefix and is English (§3, S0's convention). Every endpoint runs behind S0's single permission dependency and inside the pinned transaction (§2, A1). Every `/api/sync/*` call carries the session **and** `X-Botica-Device-Key`, plus `X-Botica-Device-Clock`, `X-Botica-App-Version` and `X-Botica-Storage-Persisted`, which the server records on `devices` and answers with `server_time` — so skew is measured on every interaction and needs no endpoint of its own.

| Method | Path | Purpose | Who can call it |
|---|---|---|---|

| GET | `/api/sync/pull` | One collection's delta after a `(updated_at, id)` cursor, at or below the safety horizon, paged. | POST | `/api/sync/push` | Apply one batch in one pinned transaction; return a per-row outcome and `server_time` (A5, rule 6) | A session on an `active` device |

**A pull page carries departures as well as arrivals.** A row that has left the collection's predicate — an item deactivated, a price whose `effective_to` has passed, a customer that fell out of the recency window — is served with its id and a deletion marker so the device removes it, and the marker is computed from the predicate at read time rather than read from a delete log. That is only sound because **a registry collection is never hard-deleted**: a hard delete leaves no row to evaluate and no `updated_at` to serve, so the row would survive on every till indefinitely. S1's model already works this way — `items.active` and `item_prices.effective_to` are the mechanisms — and criterion 14 is the check that it stays that way. A stage that needs a registry row genuinely gone deactivates it and lets retention remove it, or brings a tombstone table to this document first.

**`/api/sync/pull` is the one endpoint in the product with a p95 budget in the architecture** — under 20 ms server time with no changes pending (§4). It is a single indexed tuple scan that usually returns zero rows, and it is called by every till every `pull_interval_seconds`. Anything that makes it a join is a defect.

**Revocation is not deletion, and a revoked device keeps its data.** A till whose key was revoked because it was reassigned may still be holding unsent rows; wiping it to punish it destroys exactly what the office needs. The office list shows a revoked device's last known pending count, and re-claiming the browser drains its outbox before it re-syncs.

## Jobs

One job. Everything else this stage does is either request-scoped or runs on the client.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sync.stale_device_check` | Cron, daily, per tenant. Pins the tenant from the job payload before touching anything (rule 6, third context) | `(tenant_id, device_id, date)` — a re-run on the same day updates the existing conflict row rather than adding a second | Any `active` device whose `last_synced_at` is older than `stale_device_hours` (default 48) raises or refreshes a `device_silent` conflict and is flagged in the device list. Procrastinate retries with backoff; a job that fails leaves the previous day's row standing and is visible in the worker's own logs. It never touches a device's data and never revokes anything — a till that is quiet because the shop was closed is not a till to disable |

**Compaction is a client job, not a server one**, and it is stated here so nobody looks for it in the worker: it runs in the leader tab on start and every six hours, never while a capture field has focus.

**There is no server-side push retry**, and there is no server-side queue of device work. The outbox lives on the device, which is the only place that survives the network being gone. A server that queued work *for* a till would be a second source of truth about what that till has done, and §5 rule 1 exists to prevent exactly that.

## UI

Every surface below conforms to design-system.md Part B: one focus-ring definition (§B.5.1), geometry-matched skeletons and no spinners outside a pressed button (§B.10.1), empty states that carry an action (§B.10.2), errors that name the operation and the recovery (§B.10.3), and no single-letter shortcuts on any till surface (§B.13.3).

**`SyncStatus`** — one component, one state machine, rendered by every surface that reads the local store and re-implemented by none (§B.9.1). At rest it is `Sincronizado hace 4 s` — bare text, no dot, 11px `#727272` in the filter bar's right slot on office surfaces, `t-12` inside a 44px hit target on counter surfaces. The dot appears only when the state leaves `synced`. States and strings are §B.9.1's table verbatim: `Sincronizando · 3 pendientes`, `Sin conexión · 12 por enviar`, `Sincronización con problemas · <razón>`, `Bloqueado · se agotó la numeración del dispositivo`. It never animates, never shows a percentage, dwells two seconds before it is replaced, and announces state changes on `aria-live="polite"` while the ticking clock stays silent.

**The named degraded reasons — five from a failed transport call, and one that is not a call at all**, because §B.9.1 forbids `Error de sincronización` and every reason must be named. `sesión vencida` — the session was rejected, and the one action is to sign in again. `el servidor rechazó los datos` — the last batch returned one or more `rejected` rows, with a link to the conflict. `almacenamiento lleno` — a local write threw a quota error; compaction runs and the count is shown. `versión desactualizada` — the server's `registry_version` is ahead of this client's, and the app must reload. **`este equipo fue dado de baja` — coined here**, for a revoked `device_key`, which none of the four covers and which is the reason a till will most often see after an office action. **The sixth is listed apart because it is not a transport failure:** `el navegador puede borrar los datos sin enviar`, for a browser that refused persistent storage (§5, §B.9.4). It is the persistent chip §5 requires — `degraded` and never `blocked` — and it does not clear on the next successful call, because nothing about a successful push makes the storage durable.

**The state machine, stated as transitions rather than as descriptions**, because five states with prose definitions is how two surfaces end up disagreeing about what `pending` means. The state is a pure function of four local facts — the outbox depth, whether the last transport call succeeded, whether a blocking condition holds, and whether the browser reports itself online — evaluated in that order of precedence, top wins.

| State | Entered when | Left when | Precedence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `blocked` | A blocking condition holds. **At v1 there is none, and no stage produces one** — the exhausted numbering lease was the only producer, and leases are not built (A6, §8). The state stays defined and unreachable, because the day a till genuinely must stop is not the day to design the product's only interruption from scratch | The condition clears | 1 — beats everything, including `offline` |
| `degraded` | The last push or pull failed for a **named** reason: `sesión vencida`, `el servidor rechazó los datos`, `almacenamiento lleno`, `versión desactualizada`, `este equipo fue dado de baja` — **or** the browser refused persistent storage, which is not a transport failure at all (§5, §B.9.4) | One call succeeds, or the named condition is resolved. The persistence reason clears only when the browser grants persistence, which is why it is a chip and not a passing line | 2 |
| `offline` | `navigator.onLine` is false, **or** two consecutive transport calls failed on the network with no server response | One call succeeds | 3 |
| `pending` | The outbox is not empty and the connection is up | The outbox drains to zero | 4 |
| `synced` | The outbox is empty and the last pull succeeded | Anything above becomes true | 5 — the resting state |

Two rules keep this honest. **A transport failure with no server response is `offline`, not `degraded`** — the difference is whether we heard back, and telling a cashier the system is failing when the shop's link is simply down teaches them to distrust the line that matters. **`degraded` never suppresses the pending count**: a till with nine unsent rows and an expired session shows the reason, and the nine are in the sync panel where a cashier can see they still exist. And every state dwells two seconds before it is replaced (§B.9.1), which is what stops a flapping link producing a flickering line.

## Offline

This stage is the offline substrate, so the section is longer than it will be anywhere else — and every later stage's answer inherits from this one.

### The five rules of §5, as properties rather than principles

A principle cannot fail a demo. Each of §5's five rules is restated below as something a person can check by watching, with the acceptance criterion that checks it — because a rule nobody can fail is a rule that quietly stops being true around the fourth stage.

| §5 | The property | Checked by |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · The till never invents truth** | Every value on a till surface is either a replicated server row or a row in this device's own outbox. No till surface computes a figure from a local aggregate and presents it unqualified, and no code path writes a derived value into a replicated collection. A figure that is neither of those two things renders as `—` with its reason, never as a zero (§B.9.2 tier 3) | 3, 8, 14 |
| **2 · Selling never blocks on the network, and never blocks on stock** | Nothing on a till surface awaits a network call before it renders or accepts input. At S2 the demonstrable half is the catalog: search, read and customer registration all complete with the link cut. The stock half becomes checkable at S3 and the selling half at S4, on this substrate and not on a second one | 3, 5, 8 |
| **3 · Every client-originated write is idempotent by construction** | The same batch replayed produces one row and returns `duplicate` with the same id. The property holds when the network fails **after** the server commits, which is the only case that matters and the only one worth engineering for | 10, 11, 12 |
| **4 · The clock is not trusted, and is not discarded** | A device with a wrong clock keeps syncing, has its skew displayed on the device and in the office, and has its `occurred_at` stored exactly as it sent it beside a server `recorded_at`. No code path anywhere adjusts `occurred_at` | 17 |
| **5 · Pushes carry events, not row states** | The push payload contains no computed totals and no projections. At S2 the only pushable collection is `customers`, which is a fact about a person rather than a state of anything; the registry's `Push` column is the enforcement, and a stage that wants to push a projection is refused by the registry rather than by a review | 11, and the registry |

### Schema and registry versions, and the one wipe rule

Every pull response carries `registry_version`. A client whose bundled version is behind the server's enters `degraded · versión desactualizada` and reloads the application shell, which is served by the service worker and updates itself. A version change that alters a collection's local schema forces a **local reset** of the affected collections and a re-pull from a zero cursor — safe, because a reference collection is a server snapshot and nothing is lost by fetching it again.

**A reset never discards an unpushed row.** The outbox is drained first; if it cannot be drained because the device is offline, **the reset does not happen** — the till keeps its old store, stays `degraded`, and retries when the link returns. **What breaks if this is inverted:** a forced wipe with a full outbox loses sales, which is the single failure this entire stage exists to prevent, and it would arrive as a deployment side effect rather than as anything a cashier did. The ordering is therefore stated here rather than left to whoever writes the migration.

**The catalog, offline: fully local, and unmarked.** Every registry collection is in IndexedDB before the till is used. Reading an item, its barcodes, its laboratorio, its categoría and **this sede's price** is a local query at zero latency, and it renders at full strength with **no staleness marker**, because it is tier 1 of §B.9.2 — the till's own sede, read from the local store. The price list's own freshness is stated once, in the sync panel, and never as a dot on every line (§B.9.2).

**Registering a customer, offline: local, queued, and exactly once.** The row is written to the local store and to the outbox immediately, with no loading state at all (§B.10.1, optimistic writes). The status line moves to `Sin conexión · 1 por enviar`. On reconnection the row is pushed, and the outcome is `applied`, `duplicate` or `merged` — all three of which are successes and all three of which remove it from the outbox. If it comes back `merged`, the local row adopts the server's id silently; the cashier registered a person who was already known, which is not an event worth telling them about. **This is the ledger's rule and not this stage's convenience** (rule 8, second paragraph): `customers` carries no `client_uuid` because it is S1's master-data table rather than an event log, so it is pushed under a declared natural key instead of the client-write quartet. **What breaks if a till cannot do this:** the canonical sale document S5 hands to the client's invoicing system identifies the acquirer by document type, number and name, captured at the counter and offline included (§8), so a counter that cannot name a customer during a blackout hands over an incomplete document — and a field the receiving system needs and we did not send is a field a cashier re-types. Which is why the offline path is built here, on the one write that exists this early, rather than discovered at S5.

**Claiming a device: online only, and it says so.** A browser cannot become a device without the network — the `device_key` is issued by the server. The claim card with no connection renders §B.10.3's region treatment: `Necesita conexión para registrar este equipo.` / `Este equipo todavía no tiene el catálogo. Conéctelo a una red para prepararlo.` / `[Reintentar]`. This is the one thing in the product that a blackout genuinely prevents, and it is prevented at installation rather than at a counter.

**First sync: online only, resumable, and never partial-then-sellable.** A till is not usable until its first sync completes, and the screen says which collection it is on. An interruption resumes from the checkpoint. **A device with an incomplete first sync never presents itself as ready**, because a catalog missing 1.400 products is worse than no catalog: the cashier searches, finds nothing, and concludes the product is broken.

**The sync panel: fully local.** Every figure in it — both timestamps, the queue by kind, the label, the sede, persistence, disk usage, skew — is local state. It renders identically with the network gone, which is the only condition under which anyone urgently wants to read it.

**The application shell offline.** S0's service worker caches the shell — HTML, JS, CSS, fonts — so a till loads with no network. **It never caches an API response** (§9): the local store is the offline data layer, and a second cache would be a second truth about the catalog with no way to tell which one is stale. S2 adds nothing to the service worker and registers no route in it; a stage that finds itself wanting to cache an API response has found a collection that belongs in the registry instead.

**The office device list and the `sync` settings: online only, server-authoritative, and they say so.** An office browser never replicates (A4), so with no network these render §B.10.3's route-scope error with a retry and a correlation id, not a stale table. Showing an owner yesterday's device list without saying so is exactly the confident-figure-that-is-wrong failure §5 rule 1 exists to prevent.

**Multi-tab.** `multiInstance` over `BroadcastChannel`: one store, one elected leader owning the replication, every tab rendering the same `SyncStatus` from the same state. Two tabs both replicating would be *safe* — the push is idempotent — and would double the pull load on every till in the network while giving two tabs two independent replication states to render one status line from. Leader election is therefore required rather than an optimisation: §B.9.1's line is one state, and two replicators are two answers to how current this till is. A tab that loses leadership mid-push does not resume it; the new leader retries the same `batch_id`, and idempotency makes that a no-op.

**Retention and compaction.** Reference collections carry no time window: a row that leaves its predicate — deactivated, re-scoped, expired — arrives as a delete and is removed locally. **A registry table is therefore never hard-deleted while the registry lists it**, because a hard delete leaves no `updated_at` to serve and the row lives on every till forever; deactivation is an update, which S1's `items.active` and `item_prices.effective_to` already are, and criterion 14 checks it. Event collections and the outbox are kept for `local_retention_days`, default 30, and **only after the event is confirmed applied — nothing unpushed is compacted at any age, ever.** Compaction runs in the leader tab on start and every six hours, never while a capture field has focus (§B.13.3). At S2 the only event collection is the outbox; S3 and S4 inherit the rule for local moves and sales rather than inventing one.

**Storage persistence.** `navigator.storage.persist()` is requested at claim and its state is displayed (§5, §B.9.4). Granted: one positive line in the sync panel, `Almacenamiento protegido`, and nothing else. Denied: the state goes `degraded` with the reason `el navegador puede borrar los datos sin enviar`, a one-time dialog at claim explains what to change, and the office list shows the device as `Almacenamiento sin proteger` until it changes. **Both authorities put it at `degraded` and neither at `blocked`:** §5 states that a browser refusing `navigator.storage.persist()` is `degraded` — a one-time dialog the operator dismisses plus a persistent chip, with the till still selling — and §B.9.4 says the same, reserving `blocked` for the numbering lease. A till that must sell today is not helped by an interruption it will learn to click through every morning; refusing to sell over a risk of eviction is worse than the risk, because a shop that cannot sell has a certain loss instead of a possible one. And `blocked` means something precise — *this device cannot legally issue a document* — which at v1 **has no producer at all**, because Botica issues no fiscal document and allocates no numbering range (§8, A6). A persistence refusal is not it, and keeping it out is what leaves the state worth its interruption on the day something does raise it. **What breaks if this is wrong:** an operator ignores the degraded line, the browser evicts an unsent sale, and money is lost. Three things contain it: the office sees the condition on the device list without asking anyone; `storage_persistence_policy = required` makes it a hard stop for a network that wants one; and the pilot's own checklist says a till that refuses persistence is not put into service (§11.5).

**The clock.** The device reports its wall clock on every sync call and the server answers with its own. `occurred_at` is the till's and is what the cashier will swear to; `recorded_at` is the server's and is what every report, rollup and fiscal deadline uses (§5 rule 4, rule 8). Skew beyond `clock_skew_warn_seconds` renders a line in the sync panel and on the office device list, and **is never silently corrected**. It does not change the sync state, because none of §B.9.1's five states means "this machine's clock is wrong" and inventing a sixth to carry it would put a clock problem in the vocabulary a cashier uses to judge whether their sales have been sent. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a till two days out stamps `occurred_at` two days out, and from S4 the `Turno abierto 09:14` line and the shift report read wrong on that device. No accounting figure moves, because every one of them reads `recorded_at`.

### Demo seed

S2 registers one fixture with S0's command (§1), and S4's fixture depends on it: **the tills**. `devices` is S2's table and S2 alone writes it (ledger), so no other stage can claim a till at a sede, and `sales.number` is composed from the device code — which is what makes a seeded ticket read like the handoff's `Venta C3-4821` rather than like a row from a generator.

On `default`, two enrolled devices at Chapinero and one at each of the other five sedes, each with a code, a `last_seen_at` inside the window and a `last_synced_at` a few seconds behind it, so the sync chip on a seeded tenant reads **`Sincronizado hace 4 s`** exactly as the handoff draws it. One device is deliberately left with a `last_synced_at` **three days old — past `stale_device_hours`, so the office list renders `Sin sincronizar hace 3 días` and the stale-device job has a row to raise**, because S10's operator view answers *"which till has not synced and since when"* and a fixture where every device is healthy never exercises the question support will actually be asked.

**The fixture answers to the profile it is given** (§1), and its rule is one till per sede plus a second at the busiest one — so every profile's device count is derived rather than chosen, and no check has to hand-edit `devices` to reach a fleet it needs.

| Profile | Devices |
|---|---|
| `default`, `young`, `cold` | **seven** — two at Chapinero, one at each of the other five sedes, one of the seven deliberately silent. `young` and `cold` change nothing here: the sync substrate reads no sales, and they are named so a stage reading this table does not have to guess |
| `scale` | **forty** — two per sede over twenty sedes, with three left silent. This is exactly the fleet *The poll schedule* does its arithmetic on, which is what turns the ~30 pull requests per second from a figure taken on trust into one somebody can measure |
| `minimal` | **one**, at the single sede, enrolled by the one `owner` — and **not** the silent one, because a fixture whose only device is stale fails every S0 check that needs a healthy till, for a reason S0 did not cause |

`minimal` seeds no `cashier`, so its device is enrolled by the `owner` from the device list — the path *Scope* 2 already allows, not an exception cut for the fixture.

The fixture claims no numbering and holds no fiscal state (§8, A6), on every profile.

## Acceptance

Every criterion is checkable by watching someone use the product. The performance budgets are §4's, and they are measured **on the pilot's hardware, browser and connection** — not on a developer's machine (§11.5).

1. A `cashier` signs in on a clean browser, names the till and its sede, and lands with the full registry in local storage. An `owner` signing in on the same clean browser is never shown a claim card and nothing replicates into their browser (A4).
2. `GET /api/sync/pull` with no changes pending answers in **under 20 ms p95 server time** (§4), measured over an hour of the pilot's real till traffic rather than a synthetic loop.
3. Typing into a product search over the synced catalog filters the list in **under 30 ms p95, with no network in the path** (§4). This is measurable at S2 against 4.284 items and it is measured at S2, because a budget first checked at S4 is a budget nobody can still fix.
4. A first sync of the whole registry completes over the pilot's connection with per-collection progress visible throughout, and the resulting IndexedDB footprint is **under 12 MB**. The figure is read from `navigator.storage.estimate()` on the device, not calculated.
5. Cold start on a device whose store is already populated reaches a rendered, queryable local catalog inside the counter app's **2,5 s** cold-start budget (§4), with the network unplugged.
6. Pulling the plug mid-first-sync and plugging it back in resumes from where it stopped. The counter on screen does not go backwards and the collection does not restart.
7. Two rows are written to a synced table inside the same millisecond by a bulk load; a device that pulls between them receives both. Deleting the tuple's `id` component from the cursor and repeating the test loses one — that is the demonstration that the tuple is load-bearing.
8. With the network unplugged, a cashier registers a customer, the status line reads `Sin conexión · 1 por enviar`, and the customer is immediately findable in the local store by name and document.
9. Reconnecting drains that outbox; the status line returns to `Sincronizado hace 4 s` and the row is on the server exactly once.
10. The same push replayed by hand — same `batch_id`, same `client_uuid` — returns `duplicate` with the same id and creates no second row (A5). A push interrupted by killing the connection **after** the server committed produces the same result on retry.
11. A batch containing one row naming another tenant is rejected whole: no row in it is applied, and a `foreign_tenant` conflict appears in the office queue. A batch containing one row that fails domain validation applies every other row and returns `rejected` for that one, which leaves the outbox.
12. Two tills at two sedes register the same person by document number while both are offline. Both push. One row exists on the server, both tills converge on its id, and neither cashier is shown an error.
13. A device's location is changed from the office; the till re-pulls its location-scoped collections and its `item_prices` are the new sede's on the next pull, without a re-claim.
14. No endpoint or migration in the system hard-deletes a row from a registry collection. Deactivating an item removes it from every till within one pull interval; a hard delete, attempted deliberately in a test, leaves the row on the till — which is why the check is on the code path and not on the outcome.
15. Two tabs open on one till share one store and one replication: the queue count is identical in both, and the server sees one pull stream, not two.
16. A browser denied storage persistence renders `degraded` with the reason `el navegador puede borrar los datos sin enviar`, shows the one-time dialog, and appears on the office device list as `Almacenamiento sin proteger`. With `storage_persistence_policy = required` the same browser cannot complete a claim.
17. A device whose clock is set two days forward continues to sync, displays the skew in its sync panel and on the office list, and has its `occurred_at` stored unmodified alongside a `recorded_at` that is the server's (§5 rule 4).
18. Revoking a device from the office stops its next sync with `Sincronización con problemas · este equipo fue dado de baja`, while its local data and its unsent queue remain intact and its pending count is still visible in the office.
19. A device silent for longer than `stale_device_hours` raises exactly one `device_silent` conflict per day; running the job twice on the same day produces no second row.
20. `SyncStatus` at rest is text with no dot on every surface that reads the local store, no state animates, and no state other than `blocked` interrupts (§B.9.1). The `blocked` banner's geometry renders correctly from a fixture carrying placeholder text — no fiscal or numbering string appears in the built bundle — and **nothing in the product produces it at v1** — not S2, and not a later stage (A6, §8).
21. Every empty region in the surfaces this stage ships renders a titled empty state carrying an action; none reads `Sin datos` or `No hay nada aquí` (§B.10.2). Every error names the operation, the entity and the recovery, and carries a selectable correlation id; none reads `Error de sincronización` (§B.10.3, §B.9.1).
22. An `owner` sets the pull interval through `PATCH /api/settings/sync`, reads it back, and finds the `tenant` group written by S0 untouched in the same `tenants.settings` column (rule 5).
23. Device claim, relabel, sede change and revoke each land an `audit_log` row carrying the actor, the entity and its before/after (S0's path, ledger).
24. Every table this stage created reports row security enabled **and** forced, and a query against either one outside a pinned transaction returns zero rows (A1, rule 6).
25. A till with an unsent queue and an expired session shows `Sincronización con problemas · sesión vencida` **and** still shows the queue count in the sync panel. Unplugging the network on the same till changes the line to `Sin conexión`, because a call that got no answer is not a call the server refused.
26. A flapping connection — up and down four times in a second — does not make the status line flicker: no state is replaced inside two seconds (§B.9.1).
27. A device pushes before it pulls in every cycle, and a customer registered offline is on the server before the pull that follows it returns.
28. `GET /api/sync/digest` disagreeing with a till's local store resets that collection's checkpoint and re-pulls it, and the till is correct afterwards without anyone touching the device.
29. Requesting `/api/sync/pull` with another location's id in any parameter returns this device's location's rows. There is no parameter that changes it.
30. No domain module imports the replication client, and no view outside `core/sync/` issues a cursor query — the boundary §5 requires, checked as a boundary rather than as a convention.
31. A registry version bump that changes a local schema resets and re-pulls the affected collections on a device with an empty outbox, and **does not** reset a device that is offline with unsent rows — that device stays `degraded` and completes the reset only after it has drained.

## Verification

*Acceptance* says what must be true; this section is how a person checks it at the end of a build session, in order, without inventing a procedure. **Every check below runs on a tenant built by S0's `seed_demo_tenant` and nothing else** (§1) — no client export, no pilot data, no row inserted by hand — because that is the state every session starts in. The seed is itself the last check, since a stage whose screens do not render convincingly from it is not finished.

**The profile is `default` unless a check names another**, and a check that needs a state the healthy network cannot show **asks for a profile rather than editing rows** (§1) — a check that begins "first, delete some sales" is a check nobody runs twice.

**And every count below is the seed's own, never the registry's production sizing.** *Data*'s 6.900 barcodes, 4.400 prices and 9.000 customers are what a real pilot holds; this seed's fixture builds around forty customers and roughly 4.700 barcodes, and a check asserting the pilot's figures against it fails on every run and teaches whoever runs it to skip that check. Where the seed's exact figure is awkward to state, the check asserts against **what the product itself reports** — `GET /api/sync/registry` for this device — rather than against a literal typed into this document.

### Gates

Automated. Nothing below them is looked at until all of them are green.

- **Lint and format, server and client.** Never interesting on its own, and a gate anyway: a diff nobody can read is where the diff that matters hides.
- **Type-check, server and client, with the typed client regenerated from `/api/openapi.json` first.** A type error in the generated client after S2's thirteen endpoints land does not mean the client is wrong — it means the schema moved and nobody regenerated, which is how a stage ships an endpoint the till cannot call.
- **The test suite.** A failure must name which property stopped holding, not which assertion moved; the five properties in *Offline* are the vocabulary — a dedupe that returns `applied` twice, a batch rejection drawn on the wrong line, a cursor that skips a tie, an `occurred_at` that was corrected, or a push carrying a computed total.
- **The client production build with the local-store module in it.** A failure here is usually the storage adapter not bundling for the pilot's browser target — a §11.5 answer arriving as a build error, which is the cheapest way to learn it.
- **Migrations forward and backward against a database already carrying S0's and S1's rows, with no un-migrated model change outstanding.** S2's two tables, three enums and seven indexes must all roll back. The one that legitimately refuses to apply is the unique index on `customers`: it fails on any tenant already holding two rows with one document number, and that failure is the gate working rather than the migration being wrong (see *What this stage would break*).
- **`seed_demo_tenant --profile default` completes on an empty tenant and its S2 fixture lands seven devices** — two at Chapinero, one at each of the other five sedes, one of them deliberately silent. A failure at this fixture is almost always an unregistered `requires` on S0's identity fixture, which is where `locations` comes from, and every later stage's fixture is queued behind it. **`--profile minimal` and `--profile scale` complete too**, landing one device and forty (*Demo seed*): `minimal` is what S0's own isolation checks run against, and a fixture that assumes six sedes breaks a stage that is not this one.
- **The registry is the only door.** A check comparing the declared registry against what `GET /api/sync/pull` will actually serve: a collection servable but undeclared, or declared without an index in one of the two shapes, fails the build. This is the guard rule 9 exists to install here rather than discover at a pilot.
- **The boundary and the bundle.** No module outside `core/sync/` issues a cursor query and no domain module imports the replication client (§5, criterion 30); and no fiscal or numbering string appears in the built bundle, because `blocked` ships with placeholder copy and nothing at v1 raises it (A6, §8).

### Checks

**1 · The sync spike — run before the rest of this stage is built, not after.** A pull/push loop over registry v1 at the pilot's real catalog size, in the browser that is actually on the pilot's tills, over the pilot's link (§11.5). Record five figures and nothing else: seconds to complete a first sync of the six collections; `navigator.storage.estimate()` in MB immediately after it; what `navigator.storage.persist()` returned — granted, denied, or prompted; keystroke to filtered list over 4.284 items with the network unplugged; and cold start to a queryable local catalog. **Until this is run, nothing in *Acceptance* has a threshold** — criterion 4 has no seconds, criterion 2 has no baseline, and every budget in §4 is measured here or nowhere. Three results change the stage rather than its schedule and are reported before it continues: persistence denied, a first sync past two minutes, or a footprint above 12 MB.

**2 · A device pulls a full catalog and the local store holds it.** Run `seed_demo_tenant --profile default`, sign in as the seeded Chapinero cashier on a clean browser profile, claim the till. **The assertion is an equality between three counts and not a comparison against a number typed into this document**: for each of the six collections, the first-sync card's total, what `GET /api/sync/registry` reports for that device, and a count against the local store once the card says the till is ready must all agree. Two of the six are worth naming, because the seed fixes them and a screen draws them: `Catálogo · 4.284 de 4.284`, which is S1's own item count and the same figure the catalog grid's footer reads, and `Clientes`, which is **around forty on this seed and not the 9.000 the registry sizes a pilot at** (*Data*, §1) — the barcode and price totals are read from the registry rather than quoted, because the seed's exact figures move with S1's fixture and this check must not. Then cut the network: the six local counts are unchanged and catalog search returns rows for a seeded item name. Wrong looks like a collection short of its registry total while the card says the till is ready, or a total the client computed for itself rather than read — which is the half this equality catches and a hard-coded figure never would.

**Then the location predicate, which the seed is built to expose.** S1's fixture writes one open network-wide price per item plus **a handful scoped to Chapinero and to no other sede**, so claim a second till at Suba and compare: Suba's `Precios` total must be **lower than Chapinero's by exactly that handful**, and each must equal what its own device's `GET /api/sync/registry` reports. Equal totals at the two sedes is the failure — the location predicate is not being applied and every till in the network is holding the whole price list (A4). On `--profile scale` the same comparison runs across twenty sedes, which is where an unscoped predicate stops being a rounding error and starts being the laptop-sized first sync *The registry* exists to prevent.

**3 · The location comes from the device, and no parameter moves it** (criterion 29). **Two failures look alike here and only one of them is a vulnerability.** A predicate scoped to the wrong sede is a bug, and check 2 catches it. A **parameter that overrides a correct predicate** is a till reading another sede's rows on request, and nothing in check 2 would notice — the predicate is right, and the request simply went around it. This check exists for the second.

With the Chapinero till claimed and its `device_key` in hand, call `GET /api/sync/pull` for `item_prices` with **Suba's `location_id` in every place a parameter can reach the handler**: as a query parameter under each of `location_id`, `location`, `sede` and `filter[location_id]`, in a JSON body on the same path, and as an `X-Botica-Location` header. Expected every time: **Chapinero's rows, byte-identical to the call with no parameter at all** — the location is resolved from the `device_key` and never from a request parameter (A4, *The registry*). Repeat against `GET /api/sync/digest`, which is built on the same predicate and is the endpoint most likely to grow a parameter later, and once against `/api/sync/registry`, whose per-collection totals must also be Chapinero's.

Then the ordering half: the same call carrying a **revoked** device's key and Suba's id must fail on the key, because a revoked device is refused before any predicate is built and a security check that passes only when the key is good has tested the happy path. **Wrong is one Suba price on a Chapinero till** — a single row is the whole finding, not a discrepancy to size — or a `422` naming `location_id`, which is worse than it looks: the parameter is not being honoured today, but it is in the endpoint's schema, and a parameter that exists is a parameter somebody eventually wires to the predicate.

**4 · A push, and the replay that must be a no-op.** Register a customer at the counter with the network up; confirm `SELECT count(*) FROM customers WHERE document = '<that document>'` returns 1. Post the identical batch again — same `batch_id`, same natural key — and the per-row outcome must be `duplicate` or `merged` carrying the first row's id, with that count still 1. Then the case that actually happens: cut the link after the server commits but before the response lands, and let the till retry from its outbox. Same count, same outcome, **and the sync panel must return to `Sincronizado hace 4 s` rather than sitting on `1 por enviar`** — a client that treats `duplicate` as an error is the same defect one layer up, and it never drains (A5).

**5 · A foreign-tenant row rejects the whole batch; a bad row rejects alone.** Post a batch of three rows whose middle row names a tenant id that is not the pinned one. Expected: no row applied — `SELECT count(*) FROM customers WHERE document IN ('<row 1>','<row 3>')` returns 0 — and exactly one `foreign_tenant` row in `GET /api/sync/conflicts` naming the device and the collection, whose `detail` carries a reason code and a correlation id **and no document number** (Ley 1581, *Data*). Repeat with a row naming another location: `foreign_location`, same whole-batch outcome. Then the opposite half — a batch of three whose middle row fails domain validation — must apply the other two, return `rejected` for the one, and that row must leave the outbox. Two of three applied on the first case, or zero of three on the second, is the line in *Batch semantics* drawn wrong.

**6 · The cursor does not lose a row written in the cursor's own instant.** Read the device's checkpoint from the sync panel, then write two rows to a registry collection inside one statement so they share `updated_at` to the microsecond — one `UPDATE` touching two `items` is enough. Pull twice: one row on the first page, the other on the next, both in the local store afterwards. Then the demonstration that the tuple is load-bearing (criterion 7): repeat with the cursor compared on `updated_at` alone and watch exactly one row never arrive. Finally the horizon — hold a write in an open transaction for longer than `pull_safety_horizon_seconds`, commit it late, pull, and confirm it is on the till; if it is not, `GET /api/sync/digest` must disagree with the local store and the re-pull must repair it. **A digest that agrees while the till is missing a row is the worse of the two failures**, because it removes the backstop rather than the row.

**7 · The store survives a browser restart with unsent work.** Network off, register two customers, confirm `Sin conexión · 2 por enviar`. Quit the browser entirely — not the tab — and reopen it. Before any network: both customers are findable in the local store by document number and the sync panel still reads two pending. Restore the link: the outbox drains to zero and both rows are on the server exactly once. Then the destructive half — with the two still unsent, bump `registry_version` so a schema reset would fire. **The reset must not happen**: the device stays `degraded`, keeps its store, and resets only after the outbox has drained (criterion 31). A reset that runs against a full outbox is the one failure this stage exists to prevent, and it arrives as a deployment side effect rather than as anything a cashier did.

**8 · Persistence is requested, and its refusal is `degraded`.** On a **browser** profile where persistence is granted — the word is the browser's here and not the seed's — the sync panel reads `Almacenamiento protegido` and the office list carries no badge for it. On a browser profile where it is denied, a private window will do: the same claim reaches `degraded · el navegador puede borrar los datos sin enviar`, shows the one-time dialog once and not again on the next load, and the device appears on **Ajustes · Sedes y dispositivos** as `Almacenamiento sin proteger` in the warning-hollow badge. Push successfully and confirm the reason **does not** clear, because nothing about a successful push makes storage durable. Then set `storage_persistence_policy` to `required` through `PATCH /api/settings/sync` and confirm the same browser cannot complete a claim. Two things count as wrong: `blocked` instead of `degraded` (§5 and §B.9.4 both put it at `degraded`), and `storage_persisted` written `false` when the browser has simply not answered — the column is nullable and null means *not yet reported*.

**9 · The seed renders convincingly.** On `--profile default`, with no client data anywhere, open **Ajustes · Sedes y dispositivos** as the `owner`: seven devices, two at Chapinero and one at each of the other five, each with a sede, an `app_version` and a `Última sincronización` on §B.9.1's relative ladder, and exactly one showing `Sin sincronizar hace 3 días` — a list where every device is healthy never exercises the question support is actually asked. Then the counter as the seeded cashier: `Sincronizado hace 4 s` as bare text with no dot, the never-populated Mostrador empty state with `Buscar en el catálogo` as its action, and that search answering from the local store with the network unplugged. **Convincing means a stranger cannot tell which figures are fixtures**: no zero standing in for a thing that does not exist, no `—` where a number belongs, no two devices called `Caja 1` at one sede, no `last_synced_at` ahead of the server's clock, and no empty region reading `Sin datos` (§B.9.2, §B.10.2). Then the same screen on `--profile scale`: forty devices over twenty sedes, three of them silent, server-paginated on the grid contract rather than rendered in one page — a device list that has only ever been opened at seven rows is a list nobody has tried at the size it is for.

### What this stage would break

S2 touches six of S1's tables and three of S0's paths without owning any of them, so the regression surface is larger than the stage's own schema suggests. One check each.

- **S1's catalog load and its grid.** S2 adds six delta-cursor indexes to S1's tables (rule 4), and an index on a hot table is a write cost on every load. Run S1's load tool over its own fixture set, confirm the `imports` row reports the same `rows_created` and finishes inside its previous time, then page the Inventario grid to its last page (`1-15 de 4.284`, page 172) and confirm server time is still inside §4's 400 ms.
- **S1's `customers` writes.** The new unique index on `(tenant_id, document_type, document)` forbids what S1 previously allowed. Run S1's load tool twice over the same customer file — duplicates must report as updates, not fail the run — and create a customer through S1's own endpoint with an existing document number, which must return S1's validation error rather than a raw constraint violation reaching the surface.
- **S0's `tenants.settings`.** S2 writes the `sync` group through S0's per-group helper. Write the pull interval, then read the `tenant` group back and confirm it is unchanged (criterion 22, rule 5). A read-modify-write on that column takes out S0's group and is only noticed on the screen it belongs to, weeks later.
- **S0's tenant pinning.** The push endpoint is rule 6's fourth context and S2 is the first code to use it. Confirm `devices` and `sync_conflicts` both report row security enabled **and** forced, that a select against either outside a pinned transaction returns zero rows (criterion 24), and that S0's own check still holds — a management command run without its tenant argument refuses to start.
- **S0's audit path.** Claim, relabel, sede change and revoke on the seeded tenant must produce four `audit_log` rows with actor, entity and before/after, appended through S0's path and not by hand.
- **S0's service worker.** S2 registers no route in it (§9). With the network off, the shell must still load and the network panel must show **no** API response served from cache — a cached response is the second truth §9 forbids, and it presents as a catalog that is stale in one place and current in another.
- **S0's sign-in.** The claim card must appear for a `cashier` on an unclaimed browser and for nobody else. Sign in as `owner` and as `admin` on clean profiles: no claim card, no `/api/sync/pull` in the network panel, and the office surfaces server-authoritative exactly as before (A4).

  **≈ 8,3 MB of documents is ≈ 17 MB on disk at the twice-the-document-bytes factor this stage budgets against, and criterion 4's figure is under 12 MB.** The reference half — every amendment except S4's event collections — is ≈ 5,4 MB and ≈ 11 MB on disk, inside the budget with nothing spare; S4's seven-day sales window is the whole of the overrun. Criterion 4 measures a first sync of registry v1 and holds as written, but the **whole-registry** ceiling is not this stage's to set alone and is carried to *Gated on*. The levers, in the order the owning stages name them, are S4's retention window, S8's `cross_sell_rules_per_item` and S3's other-location cap — and **`item_warnings` is never one of them**, because a blackout must remove the network and not the safety layer (A8).
- **`GET /api/sync/pull`** — a stateless per-collection delta on a `(updated_at, id)` cursor below a safety horizon, with the client holding its own checkpoint. A stage adding a collection adds a predicate and an index, not an endpoint.
- **`POST /api/sync/push` and the idempotent client-write service** — one pinned transaction, per-row outcomes, dedupe on `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` for rule-8 tables and on a declared natural key otherwise, whole-batch rejection for a foreign tenant or location. **S3, S4, S5 and S8 call it and none dedupes by hand** (rule 8, cross-stage services).
- **The delta cursor indexes**, in both shapes: `(tenant_id, location_id, updated_at, id)` for a location-scoped collection and `(tenant_id, updated_at, id)` for a tenant-wide one. A stage adding a collection adds the matching index and says which shape it took (rule 4).
- **The client-write contract for every table a till writes** — `client_uuid` (uuid v7), `device_id`, `occurred_at` and `recorded_at`, created by each table's owner and enforced by this helper (rule 8, A5). S3 and S4 create the columns; the behaviour is already built.
- **`SyncStatus`, its five states, its six named degraded reasons and the sync panel** — rendered by every surface that reads the local store, re-implemented by none. **The panel's numbering slot is absent rather than zero**, and nothing at v1 fills it (A6, §8).
- **The local store's rules**: what is replicated, what is local-only, what is compacted and when, and the guarantee that nothing unpushed is ever compacted. S3's moves and S4's sales inherit them.
- **The outbox** — one local-only collection every later stage queues into. S3 and S4 add a kind to it and a line to the sync panel's breakdown (`Ventas 2 · Movimientos 9 · Conteos 1`, §B.9.3); neither builds a second queue.
- **The poll schedule, its backoff and its push-before-pull ordering**, so a later stage adding a collection inherits a cadence rather than choosing one.
- **The `core/sync/` boundary** on the server and one client sync module, with no domain code calling the replication API directly (§5). If §4's measurements ever justify a sync engine, it replaces that module and nothing else.
- **The `sync` settings key group** and its home in the settings dialog.
- **The `blocked` state and its banner geometry**, built and unproduced, with its copy deliberately unwritten (§B.9.1). **Nothing produces it at v1** (A6, §8) — the exhausted numbering lease it was written for is deferred with the leases themselves. A stage that later finds a condition under which a till genuinely must stop raises this state rather than inventing a sixth.
- **The offline answer template** every later stage fills in: which of its surfaces are local, which are stale-and-marked, which are online-only, and what an online-only surface shows instead (§B.9.2's three tiers).

## Gated on

- **§11.5 — the pilot: which network, how many sedes, how many tills per sede, what the connectivity actually is, and which browser is on those machines.** This is the gate on the whole stage, because **every number in *Acceptance* is a measurement and none of them is measurable anywhere else.** Three specifics matter more than the rest. **The browser decides the persistence story**: Chrome grants `navigator.storage.persist()` heuristically and usually silently, Firefox prompts, and a Safari-based till evicts unused site data on a timer no application can override — which would turn §B.9.4's degraded line into the normal state and force `storage_persistence_policy = required` plus a device that syncs daily to stay alive. **The connection decides the first-sync criterion**: 4,6 MB is thirty seconds on one link and six minutes on another, and criterion 4 has no threshold until someone names the link. **The hardware decides the 30 ms and 2,5 s budgets**, which are the two this stage can already fail and the two that are cheapest to fix now.
- **The whole-registry disk ceiling.** Criterion 4's **under 12 MB** is registry v1's own measurement and holds for it, but the full registry with S3's, S4's, S5's and S8's amendments applied is ≈ 8,3 MB of documents and ≈ 17 MB on disk (*Hands off*), so the shared ceiling has to be restated against a pilot till's own `navigator.storage.estimate()` before S4 ships, using the levers the owning stages name.
- **Three names coined here**, because the architecture supplies none of them: the enum `device_status` (`active` \| `revoked`), the enum `sync_conflict_type` **declared here in full** — S2's six protocol values plus `negative_stock`, `stale_price` and `catalog_divergence`, which S3 and S4 write and neither declares (ledger) — and the enum `sync_conflict_status` (`open` \| `resolved` \| `dismissed`). `devices` also carries seven columns beyond the five §3 names — `status`, `last_pushed_at`, `clock_skew_ms`, `storage_persisted`, `app_version`, `enrolled_by_user_id`/`enrolled_at` and `revoked_at`/`revoked_by_user_id` — coined under rule 1, since §3 states it lists only the columns that carry a decision.
- **The one degraded reason added to §B.9.1's four** — `este equipo fue dado de baja`, for a revoked device. §B.9.1 requires every degraded reason to be named and lists four; revocation is not among them and is the reason a till will most often see after an office action.

Nothing else. **§11.1, §11.2, §11.3, §11.4 and §11.6 do not block this stage**, and neither does anything in S1's catalog beyond its tables existing — the stages README is explicit that S0, S1 and S2 can be built today, and the only thing S2 cannot do without the pilot is prove it.
