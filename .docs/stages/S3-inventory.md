# S3 — Inventory

## Outcome

Stock stops being a number somebody typed and becomes a record of what happened. Merchandise is received at a sede by scanning a barcode and entering a lot code, an expiry date and a cost; the units land on a shelf and on a ledger at the same moment. An administrator opens **Existencias** and sees the network's 4.284 rows at product, laboratorio, sede, lote and vencimiento grain, filtered to the 312 that need a decision, with each row's `Estado` derived from that row's own quantity, its lot's expiry and the threshold a pharmacist set for it. Merchandise moves between sedes as a document with two ends and two moments, so the box that left Chapinero on Tuesday and has not reached Suba by Thursday is visible as exactly that rather than as a discrepancy nobody can name. A cycle count reconciles the shelf to the record by writing the difference down, not by erasing it.

The structural claim underneath all of it is one sentence: **no code updates a quantity in place** (A3). `stock_moves` is append-only, `stock_on_hand` is a projection maintained inside the same transaction as the moves that change it, and the projection can be dropped and rebuilt from the ledger with no loss. Every other stage that needs stock to move calls this stage's service and never touches either table (ledger rule 7). At the end of S3 there is one code path in the product that can change a quantity, it is auditable, it is idempotent, and it is the same path whether the event arrived from a browser online, from a device that was offline for six hours, or from a management command.

Two capabilities exist here that nothing later can retrofit. A lot is traceable: given a lot code, the system answers who has it, who bought it, on which device, in which shift, and at what moment — which is what an INVIMA withdrawal asks and what an inspection asks. And a sale can drive stock below zero without anyone at a counter being told no: the negative is an exception raised to the office, on the office's own screen, while the customer walks out with the box they were already holding (§5 rule 2).

What does not exist yet is anything that learns. `stock_policies` holds thresholds a person set, marked `source = manual`. There is no forecast, so the **Sobrestock** state has no day figure and the **Punto de reorden** badge fires off a number a pharmacist chose. S6 replaces those numbers and marks them `model`; the column exists now so the screen is complete three stages early, and `source` is what stops S6 quietly overwriting a threshold somebody set on purpose (ledger, disputed columns).

## Scope

### In

1. **The stock ledger service** — the single implementation every stage calls to move stock (ledger rule 7, cross-stage services). It appends one or more `stock_moves` rows and updates `stock_on_hand` in the same transaction, enforces the sign of each `type`, resolves or requires a lot per the item's `tracks_lots`, writes nothing at all for an item with `tracks_stock = false` (A7), stamps both clocks, and delegates deduplication to S2's helper on `(tenant_id, client_uuid)`. It has no public HTTP surface of its own: a move is always the consequence of a document, and the three exceptions are named in deliverable 8.
2. **`stock_moves`, append-only.** No endpoint, job, admin action or migration in this product updates or deletes a row in it. A mistake is corrected by a second row, never by editing the first (§6, A3).
3. **`stock_on_hand` as a projection**, keyed `(tenant_id, location_id, item_id, lot_id)` with `NULLS NOT DISTINCT`, maintained only by the service, and **rebuildable from `stock_moves` alone** — with the rebuild and its verification shipped as working operations, not as a claim (deliverable 15).
4. **`lots` and the lot grain.** Expiry, supplier, acquisition cost and the lot's own sanitary registration attach to the lot, not to the item (§6). `UNIQUE (tenant_id, item_id, lot_code)`.
5. **FEFO with a recorded override.** The service exposes lot selection for a consuming move: given location, item and quantity, it returns lots in first-expired-first-out order, skipping expired lots unless one is named explicitly. A caller may name a different lot and the move is written with `fefo_override = true` (§6). The physical box in a person's hand outranks the algorithm, and the disagreement is recorded rather than prevented.
6. **The lot trace**, as a deliverable rather than as an emergent property of having the data: given a lot, every move on it in order, each with its document, its device, its user, `occurred_at` and `recorded_at`, and the running balance after it — plus the reverse lookup from a lot code to every location holding it. This is the query an INVIMA withdrawal is answered with.
7. **`stock_policies`** with `min_quantity`, `max_quantity`, `reorder_point`, `target_coverage_days` and `source`. S3 writes `manual` rows, per item and per location, plus network-wide rows with a null `location_id` (ledger). This is what makes the **Punto de reorden** badge real before a forecast exists.
8. **Direct movements — the only three a human writes by hand:** `adjustment`, `shrinkage` and `expiry`, each carrying a reason from a fixed vocabulary and an optional note. Every other `type` is written by the service on behalf of a document (ledger, the `stock_moves.type` table).
9. **Receiving — `Cargar mercancía`.** Scan a barcode, enter lot code, expiry, quantity and unit cost, repeat, confirm. Creates or matches `lots` and appends one move per line. **This covers opening stock and standalone receipts only**; a receipt against a purchase order is S6's `goods_receipts` and writes `type = receipt` (ledger). See the Data section for why S3's receipts are `adjustment` rows.
10. **Opening stock at scale** — a management command taking an explicit tenant and location (ledger rule 6), a file of item / lot / expiry / quantity / cost lines, and writing one move per line under one shared `document_id`, with an `audit_log` row per run.
11. **Transfers** — `transfers` + `transfer_lines` with the state machine `draft → dispatched → received | partial` (§3). Two moves at two moments: `transfer_out` at dispatch from the origin, `transfer_in` at receipt at the destination. Between them the units are on no shelf and are reported as **En tránsito**, which is a figure on the transfer and on the item's record panel, never a state in the `Estado` column.
12. **The shortfall resolution.** A transfer at `partial` stays on a work list until a person closes it by choosing one of two answers, each of which writes a move: the remainder arrived late (a second `transfer_in`), or it did not arrive (a `shrinkage` move at the **origin**, reason `loss`). A shortfall that quietly nets out of the ledger is merchandise nobody ever has to explain.
13. **Cycle counts** — `stock_counts` + `stock_count_lines`, scoped to a location and optionally to a category or an item list. A line stamps the expected quantity **at the moment the line is entered**, not at close. Closing writes one `count` move per line whose counted quantity differs from that stamp. A count never overwrites a quantity (§6).
14. **Negative stock as an exception, never a refusal** (§5 rule 2). When an append drives `stock_on_hand.quantity` below zero, the service writes or updates one open `sync_conflicts` row per `(location, item, lot)` naming the sede, the item, the lot, the resulting quantity and the documents that crossed zero. The row is resolved by closing a stock count that covers that item and lot, in the same transaction as the adjusting move (§5).
15. **Projection rebuild and verification**, as two operations and one nightly job. Rebuild recomputes `stock_on_hand` for a tenant and location as the sum of `stock_moves` and replaces it; verify recomputes and reports the difference without writing. Drift is never expected — a non-zero result means a code path wrote outside the service, which is a defect (ledger rule 7).
16. **Expiry horizons**, three of them, owned by the `inventory` settings group and read by the `Estado` derivation, by the expiry filter chip, by the daily digest and by the Panel's `Inventario por vencer · 90 días` tile that S9 renders.
17. **The `inventory` settings group** — `GET` and `PATCH /api/settings/inventory` on the pinned tenant, written through S0's helper (ledger rule 5).
18. **Existencias**, server-authoritative and server-paginated as drawn: seven columns at the drawn widths, the in-cell stock bar, four filter chips, the search field, the footer's range and its `312 requieren acción` annotation, the row-size select and the page group (handoff Pantalla 2, design-system §A.17, §A.18.1, §B.4).
19. **The `Estado` derivation**, specified exactly — its precedence, the input each state reads, and what each shows before S6 exists (design-system §B.7.4 fixes the families, the dots and the labels; this document fixes the rules).
20. **Traslados, Conteos and Cargar mercancía** as three further routes under the same `Inventario` nav item, reached from the breadcrumb's module menu, so the nav stays at seven items (design-system §B.8.1).
21. **The sync registry amendment** (ledger rule 9) — `stock_on_hand`, `lots` and `stock_policies` on the device, with predicates and row-count estimates, amended into S2's document.

- **`daily_metrics` and the Panel** — **S9**. S3 ships `GET /api/stock/expiring` and the per-location summary the `Inventario por vencer · 90 días` tile and the **Sedes** cards read; it renders neither (design-system §B.8.4·2, ledger).
- **The Sedes route** — **S9**, per design-system §B.8.4·2, which names it `S3/S9`. The stock figures on those cards — quiebres and días de stock — come from S3's summary endpoint; the route, the cards and the per-sede table are one screen and belong to the stage that owns the rollup behind the other two figures on them.
- **`sync_conflicts` as a resource** — **S2** creates the table, declares `sync_conflict_type` in full with `negative_stock` already in it, writes the protocol-level rows and exposes the endpoint and the surface. S3 writes negative-stock rows into it and resolves them from the counting screen, and adds neither an endpoint under S2's path nor a value to S2's enum (ledger, enum register).
- **The `imports` run record** — **S1** creates it and **S1 and S6** write it (ledger). S3's opening-stock command records its run in `audit_log` and in the moves' shared `document_id`, not in `imports`.
- **Item pricing and margin.** `item_prices`, `regulated_max_price`, and every retail figure — **S1** and **S7**. Inventory valuation in this stage is at `lots.unit_cost` and never at sale price.
- **`items.invima_registration` and its expiry.** Filtered and displayed here, written by **S1**, checked against nothing (§12, ledger).
- **Hardware.** No label printer, no scale, no ESC/POS. A barcode scanner in HID keyboard mode needs no integration and is assumed (§12).
- **Reservations and allocations.** Nothing in v1 holds stock aside for a customer, an order or a transfer that has not been dispatched. A `draft` transfer moves no units and its lines are a request, not a hold. Adding a reservation later is a new document type and a fourth input to the `Estado` derivation, not a change to the ledger.
- **Serial numbers and unit-level tracking.** The grain is the lot, and a pharmaceutical unit is identified by item and lot (§6). Nothing here tracks an individual box.
- **The libro de control and the controlled-medicine consumption report.** `items.controlled` is captured by S1 and shown here; Botica holds the records that make the report possible and computes and files nothing (§12). A stock ledger that is queryable per controlled item is the whole contribution, and it is already a by-product of A3.
- **Barcode and shelf-label printing.** No hardware in v1 (§12).
- **A distributor module.** `locations.type` already admits `distribution_center` and a transfer is already a document between two locations, so nothing here has to be unpicked later; no distributor feature ships (A10).

**Why this is a ledger and not a counter, argued rather than asserted.** A3 is one line in architecture.md §14 and it is the decision the rest of this stage is shaped around, so here is what it buys.

*Appends commute, and that is what makes offline safe.* Addition does not care what order it happens in. Two tills at one sede, each offline, each selling the last box, produce two rows; whichever arrives first, and however many times it is retried, the sum is the same and it is right. Deduplication is a unique index on `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` and nothing more (A5). The alternative — `UPDATE stock_on_hand SET quantity = quantity - 1` — needs the writes to arrive, to arrive once, and to arrive in an order nobody controls; on a network that drops several times a week the first of those three fails weekly and the other two fail silently. A lost update in an inventory system is not an error message, it is a number that is quietly wrong for months.

*Every unit traces to the document, the person, the device and the moment.* A move names its `document_type` and `document_id`, its `user_id`, its `device_id`, and both clocks — `occurred_at` from the till and `recorded_at` from the server (§5 rule 4). That is what makes a discrepancy attributable to a shift rather than to a sede, what makes an INVIMA withdrawal answerable in one query, and what a droguería running on a fifteen-year-old desktop program and the owner's memory cannot produce today. It is also why cost of goods is computable at all: the move carries the cost at which those units actually moved, so a cost change next month does not retroactively rewrite last month's margin.

*A count writes the discrepancy down instead of erasing it.* Overwriting a quantity to match a shelf destroys the only interesting output of counting — the difference, its size, its direction and where it keeps happening. A `count` move with a reason keeps it, and a sede that is short 40.000 pesos every month in the same category is a fact somebody can act on rather than a feeling.

**The projection is not a cache and is never repaired by hand.** `stock_on_hand` exists because summing a ledger on every grid page is not a 400ms query (§4), and for no other reason. It is written by one module, it is never read as truth in an argument about what happened, and the rebuild in *Jobs* is the standing proof that dropping it costs nothing. A migration or a job that corrects a quantity in place is a defect and not a shortcut (ledger rule 7) — and the verify job exists so that such a defect is found by the system rather than by a pharmacist counting boxes.

**Why S3's receipts are `adjustment` rows and not `receipt` rows.** The ledger fixes `receipt` as *caused by S6, when a goods receipt against a purchase order is confirmed*, and the ledger wins on ownership. So opening stock and standalone receipts — merchandise that arrives with no order behind it — are written as `adjustment` with `reason` in (`opening_stock`, `standalone_receipt`), positive. The consequence to hold onto: **cost of goods must never be computed by filtering `type = 'receipt'`.** It reads `stock_moves.unit_cost` and the move's `lot_id`, across every positive type. If a later reviewer prefers a distinct type for a receipt with no order, that is one enum value and one ledger row, and this paragraph is the diff to apply — but a cost report written against the move type would already be wrong the day it was written.

**`quantity` is always in the item's own `unit`, never in packs.** S1 fixes `unit`, `splittable` and `units_per_pack` per item (§3), and the ledger counts the unit that gets sold: tablets for a splittable blister, boxes for one that is not. **Receiving asks in packs and converts on the way in**, showing both figures on the line before it is confirmed — `12 cajas · 360 unidades`. This is stated because the alternative is the classic inventory bug that survives a year: merchandise received in packs, sold in units, and a projection that reads a thirtieth of the truth. A move whose quantity was recorded in the wrong unit is only correctable by another move, which is exactly the cost of getting it wrong here.

**Valuation is per lot, so there is no cost method to choose.** A location's inventory value is `Σ quantity × lots.unit_cost` over its `stock_on_hand` rows, and `GET /api/stock/expiring` values at that cost and never at sale price — an inventory-at-risk figure priced at retail overstates the loss by the whole margin and is the number an owner would repeat to their accountant. FIFO, weighted average and standard cost are all answers to the question *which cost applies to these units*, and lot grain answers it directly: these units came in that lot, at that cost (§6).

**The `reason` vocabulary**, fixed here as a constrained text column rather than a Postgres enum so a later stage adds a value without a migration: `opening_stock`, `standalone_receipt`, `correction`, `damage`, `theft`, `loss`, `expired`, `count_adjustment`, `negative_resolution`. It is null on every type except `adjustment`, `shrinkage`, `expiry` and `count`.

## API surface

Every path carries the `/api/` prefix and is English; every response is rendered in Spanish by the client (§3). Every endpoint runs behind S0's single permission dependency and inside the pinned transaction (§2, A1), and every list endpoint applies S0's location-scoping helper rather than its own filter (A2). Server-paginated surfaces honour the grid contract (§9), and the settings pair follows the one convention every stage uses — `GET` and `PATCH /api/settings/{group}` against the pinned tenant, with no tenant id in the path (ledger rule 5).

**Two shape rules bind this stage in particular.** There is **no endpoint that writes `stock_on_hand`**, at any path, for any role — the projection has no API (ledger rule 7). And **`POST /api/stock-moves` accepts three types and no others**, because every other type in the enum is the consequence of a document that has its own endpoint; a general "write me a move" endpoint would be the exact hole through which a future stage bypasses the service.

## Jobs

Three, all queued on Procrastinate with `tenant_id` in the payload and pinning before touching anything (ledger rule 6).

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `inventory.expiry_digest` | Daily, per tenant per location, at the tenant's local morning | `(tenant_id, location_id, date)` | Retries with backoff. **Writes no `stock_moves` row, ever** — an expired lot is raised onto a work list and never written off on a schedule. §12 fixes that Botica surfaces the state and records the pharmacy's decision; a job that writes off stock is a job that destroys the record of a decision nobody made. If email is unreachable the digest is retried; the state renders on Existencias regardless |
| `inventory.projection_verify` | Nightly, per tenant per location | `(tenant_id, location_id, date)` | Recomputes `stock_on_hand` from `stock_moves` and compares without writing. Zero drift writes one `audit_log` row saying so; non-zero drift writes the differing keys into that row's `before`/`after` and surfaces on S10's operator view. Drift is a defect signal, not a correction event — a job that silently repairs the projection also silently hides the code path that broke it (ledger rule 7) |
| `inventory.projection_rebuild` | On demand, per tenant per location, from the management command | `(tenant_id, location_id, requested_at)` | Recomputes and replaces the projection inside one transaction holding an advisory lock the ledger service also takes for that location, so appends during the rebuild are serialised rather than lost. Naturally idempotent: running it twice produces the same rows. Writes one `audit_log` row with the count of rows changed |

**The rebuild procedure, stated because a projection nobody has ever rebuilt is a projection nobody trusts.** For one tenant and one location, inside one pinned transaction: take the location's advisory lock; compute `SUM(quantity) GROUP BY item_id, lot_id` over `stock_moves` for that location; upsert every resulting key into `stock_on_hand` and delete every key the ledger no longer produces; write the `audit_log` row; release. The lock is the whole correctness argument — the ledger service takes it on every append, so a rebuild and a sale cannot interleave into a lost update. It runs per location rather than per tenant so a 20-sede network is 20 short locks and not one long one, and so a single sede can be rebuilt during business hours without the other nineteen pausing.

#### The `Estado` derivation

**`Estado` is not a column on any table.** It is computed per row from that row's quantity, its lot's expiry, its policy and — when S6 exists — its forecast. Design-system §B.7.4 fixes the seven states, their families, their dots and their labels, and calls itself their only definition; what follows fixes the rules, which it leaves open. **First match wins, top to bottom.**

| | State | Family · dot | Label | The input it reads | What it does before S6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `expired` | Critical · solid | **Vencido** | `lots.expires_at < today` and `quantity > 0` | unchanged |
| 2 | `stockout` | Critical · solid | **Quiebre**, or **Quiebre · hay 96 en Suba** | `stock_on_hand.quantity <= 0` | unchanged. The clause reads `GET /api/stock/availability` |
| 3 | `expiring_urgent` | Critical · **hollow** | **Vence en 6 meses** | `expires_at` inside `expiry_alert_days` | unchanged |
| 4 | `expiring` | Warning · **hollow** | **Vence en 5 meses** | `expires_at` inside `expiry_notice_days` and outside the alert window | unchanged |
| 5 | `reorder_point` | Warning · solid | **Punto de reorden** | `quantity <= stock_policies.reorder_point` | the manual threshold. S6 overwrites the number and sets `source = model`; the rule does not change |
| 6 | `overstock` | Informative · solid | **Sobrestock · 94 días** | `demand_forecasts.coverage_days` above 90, **or** `quantity > stock_policies.max_quantity` | `max_quantity` only, and **the label carries no day figure** — see below |
| 7 | `sufficient` | Positive · solid | **Suficiente** | none of the above | unchanged |

**Why the precedence is in that order.** `expired` outranks everything because units on a shelf past their date are a legal problem, not a supply problem. `stockout` outranks expiry because finding the quiebres is what a droguería opens this screen to do. **Expiry outranks the reorder point**, and the handoff settles it: the Salbutamol row is at 7 units against a 4-day cover and still renders `Vence en 6 meses` rather than `Punto de reorden`. A short-dated lot changes what you buy, and a reorder badge that hides an expiry sends someone to order more of something that will not sell before its date.

**A row with no `stock_policies` row cannot reach states 5 or 6** and falls through to `sufficient` unless expiry or a zero applies. The record panel says `Sin política definida` so that a **Suficiente** badge on an unmanaged reference is never mistaken for a judgement about it.

**`Sobrestock` before S6.** §B.7.4 makes the cover figure part of the label, and there is no forecast at S3 — and no sales at all, since S4 has not shipped. So the badge reads **Sobrestock** alone, and §B.9.2 tier 3 is why: a fabricated day count is worse than no day count, and the alternative of a zero would be the most expensive kind of lie this screen can tell. When `demand_forecasts.coverage_days` arrives the clause appears with no other change to the rule (ledger, disputed columns).

**Sorting `Estado`** is by the ordinal in the first column above, descending by default — most urgent first — and is computed server-side like every other sort (§9). It is never alphabetical on the label: in Spanish that would place `Punto de reorden` above `Quiebre`, which inverts the only ordering the column has.

**`Estado · Requiere acción`**, the chip the handoff draws active, is states 1, 2, 3 and 5 — expired, quiebre, urgent expiry and reorder point. It is not `expiring` (a lot eleven months out is not a decision anyone takes today) and not `overstock` (that is capital, and it is Compras' screen). Those four are what the footer's `312 requieren acción` counts. The chip also offers each of the seven individually.

**The other chips.** `Sede` is multi-select and drawn as `Sede · Todas · 6`; its selection is intersected with S0's location-scoping helper and never replaces it (A2). `Categoría` reads S1's tree. `Vencimiento` offers `Vencidos`, `Vence en 90 días`, `Vence en 6 meses`, `Vence en 12 meses` and `Sin fecha`, mapping to the valuation window and the two alert horizons rather than to hand-typed numbers. **The availability clause names one sede only** — the location holding the most units, ties broken by name so the string does not change between page loads. The handoff draws one, and a badge listing four sedes is a badge nobody reads.

- **Loading:** fifteen skeleton rows at the real 48px with a bar per cell at that column's real width; the range is a skeleton bar at its resting width and the page group is not rendered until `rowCount` arrives (§B.10.1, §B.4.5). A filter, sort or page change keeps the previous rows at `opacity:0.6` with the 2px progress line under the filter bar — it never blanks a populated table.
- **Empty — never populated:** `Todavía no hay existencias` / `La mercancía entra por Cargar mercancía o por un traslado desde otra sede.` / primary `Cargar mercancía` (§B.10.2).
- **Empty — filtered to nothing:** `Ningún producto coincide con estos filtros` / the active filters echoed verbatim / secondary `Quitar filtros` (§B.10.2). Never the primary — the intent was to filter.
- **Error:** route scope — the empty-state geometry, a retry and a selectable correlation id (§B.10.3). `No pudimos cargar las existencias.` and the reason. Never `Ocurrió un error`.
- **Denied:** a `cashier` reaches this route read-only across every location, with their home sede pre-selected in the `Sede` chip — §2 grants exactly that, so this is not an open question. The two header actions and the panel's threshold editor are **not rendered** for them, and a direct call to `PUT /api/stock-policies` is refused.
- **Below 1440px:** no column is dropped. The table frame scrolls horizontally inside its own `overflow-x:auto` container at a stated 1080px minimum, and `Producto` is sticky-left (§B.4.4). Every one of the seven columns is a filter target, and a column that disappears at a viewport width is a column a user cannot find. If a pilot on a 1366px laptop finds the horizontal scroll to `Estado` intolerable, the fix is a column reorder, not a redesign — see *Gated on*.
- **Nav counter:** none. §B.8.2 fixes the counter as work waiting in a module, and `312 requieren acción` is the state of the shelves rather than a queue of decisions. A three-digit badge that is never zero on a nav item a cashier sees all day is a badge nobody reads.

### Cargar mercancía — `/inventory/receive`

Breadcrumb `Inventario / Cargar mercancía`, title `Cargar mercancía`. A capture field at the top holding focus permanently, a line list below it, and a footer with the line count and the primary `Confirmar entrada`. A scan resolves through `item_barcodes`; an unrecognised code offers `Crear producto` for an `admin` and says so plainly for a `cashier`. Each line asks lot code, expiry `MM/AAAA`, quantity and unit cost, with the previous cost for that item and supplier pre-filled and editable. Counter density, 56px rows, and **no single-letter shortcuts anywhere on this surface** — a scanner is a keyboard (§B.11, §B.13.3). Focus returns to the capture field after every action.

- **Loading:** optimistic — a scanned line renders immediately with a defined revert path, and there is no loading state on an add (§B.10.1).
- **Empty:** `Escanee el primer producto` / `El cursor ya está en el campo de captura.` / no action, because the action is the scanner.
- **Error:** field scope for a lot code whose stored expiry disagrees with the one entered — `El lote A-2291 ya existe con vencimiento 03/2027.` The line is refused rather than accepted with either date, because an expiry mismatch on the same lot code is a typo the person holding the box fixes in three seconds, and silently keeping either value puts a wrong date at the head of the FEFO queue.
- **Denied:** `cashier` may not confirm an entry.

### Conteos — `/inventory/counts`

Breadcrumb `Inventario / Conteos`. The list flags a location whose `count_cadence_days` has elapsed. Opening a count gives the counting surface: Counter density, capture field, one line per scan, the expected quantity **hidden until the line is entered** so the count is a count and not a confirmation. Closing shows the differences, their value at `lots.unit_cost`, and the sentence `Al cerrar se registran N movimientos de ajuste. Las diferencias quedan en el historial.` A count covering an item with an open negative-stock exception shows that exception inline — this screen is where §5 says it is resolved.

### The direct-movement dialog

Reached from the record panel. One type (`Ajuste`, `Merma`, `Vencimiento`), one quantity, one reason from the fixed vocabulary rendered in Spanish (`Corrección`, `Daño`, `Robo`, `Pérdida`, `Vencido`), and an optional note. The confirm button states the consequence: `Restar 12 unidades del lote A-2291 en Chapinero`. Destructive is the confirm and never a form default (§B.6.2).

- **Loading:** no skeleton — the dialog opens over data the record panel already holds. The confirm button carries the only spinner on this surface, and the dialog stays open with its values until the move is appended (§B.10.1). A dialog that closes optimistically on an append is a dialog that loses the reason somebody typed.
- **Empty:** not reachable, and stated so nobody builds one — the dialog is opened from a row that exists, and the reason list is the fixed vocabulary in *Data*, which is never empty (§B.10.2).
- **Error:** field scope on the two the endpoint requires — a quantity that is not a positive number in base units, and a missing `reason`; dialog scope on a failed append, naming the operation, the lot and the recovery, with a selectable correlation id (§B.10.3). Never `Ocurrió un error`, and never a dismissal that discards the line silently.
- **Denied:** `Ajuste` is not rendered in the type selector for a `cashier`, and `POST /api/stock-moves` refuses it if called directly — see below.

**A `cashier` may write `shrinkage` and `expiry` and may not write `adjustment`.** This closes design-system §B.17·3's open question about a manual movement. The reasoning: a negative movement a cashier can point at on a shelf — a broken bottle, an expired blister — is something the person who found it should record while it is in their hand, and requiring a regente for it means it never gets recorded. A positive adjustment is the one movement in the product that creates value out of nothing, and it is the exact shape of a loss being covered. What breaks if this is wrong: a sede whose administrator is not on site daily cannot correct a mis-scan without waiting, and the recorded fix is to grant that person the `admin` role, which §2 already supports — not a new permission surface.

## Offline

**Existencias is online-only**, and this is deliberate. §4 fixes it as a server-authoritative, server-paginated office surface reading the whole network, and A4 forbids syncing 20 sedes into a browser. With no network the route renders the §B.10.3 route-scope treatment — `No pudimos cargar las existencias. El equipo está sin conexión.` with a retry — and **never a cached table**, because a stale 4.284-row grid a person is about to make a purchasing decision from is exactly the confident-number-that-is-wrong §5 rule 1 exists to prevent. The service worker caches the application shell and never an API response (§9).

**Traslados and Conteos' list views are online-only** for the same reason. **The counting surface itself is offline-capable**: a count is walked around a back room where the wifi is worst, its lines carry the client-write quartet (ledger rule 8) and queue through S2's push. **Closing a count is online-only** — it writes adjusting moves against a projection the device cannot see whole.

**Cargar mercancía is offline-capable**, because merchandise arrives whether or not the internet is up, and a box that cannot be received is a box that gets sold from while being invisible. Lines queue with `client_uuid`, `device_id` and `occurred_at`; the server creates or matches the `lots` row inside the pinned push transaction, since `lots` is not a till-written table (ledger rule 8) and the push carries the lot's natural key rather than a row. Offline the surface shows a single line under the header: `Sin conexión · no podemos comprobar si hay traslados en camino a esta sede.` That is the one real risk of receiving offline — the same box booked in twice, once as a receipt and once as a transfer — and naming it is cheaper than a check that needs the server anyway.

**Transfers are online-only in all three verbs.** A dispatch commits stock at another location's expense and a transfer created twice offline is merchandise that leaves twice. A receipt is an append against a transfer the device has never seen, because transfers are not in the sync registry. Offline, the transfer screens say so and the units stay `En tránsito`, where the office can see them. The cost of this decision: a sede offline for a day cannot book in an arriving box, so its shelf figure understates by that box until the connection returns — and the network total stays right the whole time, because the units are in the ledger as dispatched and not yet received.

**What this stage puts on the device**, amended into S2's registry under ledger rule 9, with predicates and estimates. The estimates use the handoff's own figures: 1.184 active references per sede (Compras KPI) and roughly 1,6 open lots per referencia.

| Table | Predicate | Rows per location | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stock_on_hand` | `location_id = <device location>` | **≈ 1.900** | The till's own sede's stock, at lot grain. Tier 1 authoritative, no staleness marker (§B.9.2) |
| `stock_on_hand` | rows at **other** locations, for items the device's own location holds at or below `reorder_point` or at zero | **≈ 420** at 6 sedes, **≈ 1.600** at 20, hard-capped at 2.000 | **Provisioned for S4's counter lookup — no S3 surface renders them.** Existencias is online-only and server-authoritative, so nothing in this stage reads these rows in a browser; they are here so the till's `hay 96 en Suba` clause answers at zero latency the day S4 ships, and so the registry is amended and measured once rather than twice. The set scales with the number of the device's own problems, not with the size of the network, which is what keeps A4 true. Every figure S4 draws from them carries the §B.9.2 marker and the reading `Suba · hace 3 min`; above the cap the clause is absent rather than stale, and the badge reads `Quiebre` alone |
| `lots` | lots referenced by either `stock_on_hand` set above | **≈ 2.300** | Expiry and cost for the FEFO queue and the counter's expiry display. Joined through `stock_on_hand` rather than scoped by location, because `lots` has no `location_id` |
| `stock_policies` | `location_id = <device location> OR location_id IS NULL` | **≈ 1.200** | The reorder threshold behind the counter's low-stock signal |

Roughly 5.800 rows and a few hundred kilobytes per till, against S2's budget. The delta-pull budget of **< 20ms p95 server time** (§4) is an acceptance criterion of this stage precisely because these are the first three tables added to the registry after S2 shipped it.

**Where a figure is not synced, it is not guessed.** Another sede's stock for an item that is not in trouble at this sede is unknown at the till and renders as an em dash with the reading `sin conexión`, never as a last-known number and never as a zero (§B.9.2 tier 3). A zero standing in for "we don't know" is the single most expensive lie an inventory system can tell, and it is the one a cashier acts on immediately.

## Demo seed

**S1 owns the command; S3 registers fixtures with it** (§1 *Cold start*, ledger — the demo seed row). One command builds Droguerías La 45 and its six sedes, and each stage contributes the rows for its own tables rather than shipping a seeding path of its own, because two seeds diverge within a month and the one nobody runs is the one that stops building. This stage contributes `lots`, `stock_moves` and `stock_policies`, plus the transfers and counts that keep the module's other three routes off their empty states. It contributes no `stock_on_hand` rows at all, because `stock_on_hand` is a projection and nothing writes it directly.

**The seed moves stock through the ledger service, exactly as production does** (rule 7, A3). It appends moves and lets the projection follow in the same transaction; it never inserts a `stock_on_hand` row and never sets a quantity. What breaks if this is wrong: acceptance 3 — drop the projection, rebuild it, compare — would pass against fixtures that never went through the ledger, and the seed would be the one code path in the product that changes a quantity in place, which is the single thing this stage exists to make impossible.

**Existencias is why the fixtures have to be good.** It is the most data-dense screen in the product, the handoff draws it fully populated at the network's real scale, and it is the surface where an absent denominator, a state nobody produced or a clause with nothing to name is visible at a glance. So this stage's fixtures are specified by what the screen must render rather than by what the tables must contain.

| The seed produces | So that |
|---|---|
| **4.284 rows at `(item, location, lot)` grain across all six sedes**, of which exactly **312 match `Estado · Requiere acción`** — the seeded network's whole grid, which is a different measure from S2's per-sede production sizing and coincides on the number only because the seed does not stock every item at every sede | the footer reads `1-15 de 4.284` with `312 requieren acción`, and acceptance 12 and 13 are measured at the drawn scale rather than against a toy table |
| **All seven `Estado` states**, a dozen rows deep each — the five the handoff draws (**Suficiente**, **Punto de reorden**, **Sobrestock**, **Quiebre · hay N en `<sede>`**, **Vence en N meses**) plus **Vencido** and the second expiry tier, which it does not | acceptance 14 is satisfied from the seed instead of from rows somebody built by hand for the test; both dot styles, all four badge families and the precedence of the derivation are visible on one screen |
| **Lots spread across both horizons and past them** — expired, inside `expiry_alert_days`, inside `expiry_notice_days`, and well outside both — with every offset computed **from the tenant's own configured horizons and relative to the run date** | a pilot that moves a window does not empty a tier, and the seed does not decay: hard-coded dates would turn a `Vence en 5 meses` row into a `Vencido` one six months from now and quietly change what the screen demonstrates |
| **The same item held unevenly across the six sedes**, with a set of rows at zero in one sede while another holds a clear plurality — **no ties** | `Quiebre · hay 96 en Suba` has a sede to name, the clause is stable between page loads, and the `Sede · Todas · 6` chip filters onto six populated sedes rather than onto four |

| **`stock_moves` history behind the rows** — one lot past the panel's 50-move cap, most with a handful, every move carrying its document, device, user and both clocks | a record panel opened on whichever row a reviewer happens to click is never the empty state, and the cap is exercised rather than assumed |

**`Sobrestock` carries no day figure from the seed, and must not.** The state is produced here by `quantity > stock_policies.max_quantity`, which is the only input that exists at S3; the cover clause needs `demand_forecasts.coverage_days`, and that is S6's fixture to register against the same command. Acceptance 16 already forbids `Sobrestock · 0 días`, and a seed that invented a day count so the badge would match the drawing would be the first place in this product where a screenshot was worth more than a true number.

**The seed is synthetic and self-evidently so**, and it is never loaded into a real tenant (§1). The command takes S1's explicit tenant argument and pins it before doing any work (rule 6); this stage's fixtures refuse to append into a tenant that already carries a `stock_moves` row. What breaks if this is wrong: 4.284 fabricated stock rows inside a customer's tenant is not something anyone cleans up afterwards, because the ledger is append-only by design and the correction is itself a movement.

**Every random draw is seeded from a fixed constant**, so two runs produce the same screen. A demo whose numbers move between runs cannot be compared against a drawing, and a defect naming a row nobody can reproduce is a defect nobody acts on.

## Acceptance

Every statement below passes or fails while watching someone use the product.

1. **No in-place update exists.** Grep the application for an `UPDATE` or `DELETE` against `stock_moves` and find none; attempt one through any endpoint and be refused. `stock_on_hand` is written from exactly one module (ledger rule 7, A3).
2. Receiving 40 units of a new lot at Chapinero appends exactly one `stock_moves` row and moves `stock_on_hand` by exactly 40, in one transaction — killing the process between them leaves neither.
3. **Drop `stock_on_hand` entirely, run the rebuild for the tenant, and every quantity on Existencias is identical to what it was before**, on a database with transfers, counts, adjustments and shrinkage in it. This is the criterion A3 exists for.
4. The verify job on a healthy database reports zero drift and writes one `audit_log` row saying so. Insert a row into `stock_moves` by hand, re-run it, and it names the exact `(location, item, lot)` key that drifted.
5. Two devices at one sede each sell the last box of the same item while both are offline. Both sales are accepted, both appear in the ledger, `stock_on_hand` reads −1, no error is shown at either counter, and one `sync_conflicts` row at `type = negative_stock` names the sede, the item, the lot and both sales (§5 rule 2).
6. Replaying that same push batch three times changes nothing: three attempts, one set of moves, one conflict row (A5, ledger rule 8).
7. Closing a cycle count that covers that item and lot writes the adjusting `count` move and marks the conflict resolved in the same transaction — with no second endpoint under S2's `sync_conflicts` path, and no `ALTER TYPE` on `sync_conflict_type` anywhere in this stage's migrations.
8. A count entered at 10:00 and closed at 10:40, with two sales in between, ends with `stock_on_hand` equal to the counted figure **minus those two sales** — not equal to the counted figure. The adjusting move is the discrepancy measured at entry, and the sales apply on top. Getting this backwards double-counts every sale made during a count.
9. Dispatching a transfer of 60 units and receiving 48 leaves the transfer at **Recibido parcial**, the origin down 60, the destination up 48, and 12 units belonging to no shelf and visible as `En tránsito`. Resolving as `No llegó` writes a `shrinkage` at the origin and the network total falls by 12 — once.
10. A lot code entered on the trace screen returns every location holding it and every sale that took a unit from it, each with its device, its user and both clocks, and exports as CSV. This is the INVIMA answer, demonstrated rather than asserted.
11. Selling against a lot other than the FEFO head is accepted, and the resulting move carries `fefo_override = true` (§6).
12. **Existencias serves any filter combination in under 400ms p95 server time**, on a dataset at the handoff's scale — 4.284 rows, 6 sedes — with page, size, sort and every filter in the URL, so the view is a link (§4, §9).
13. The footer reads `1-15 de 4.284` with `312 requieren acción` beside it, and the `Estado · Requiere acción` chip narrows the grid to exactly those 312 rows.
14. All seven `Estado` states render on one screen with the correct family, dot and label from §B.7.4 — including a hollow dot on **Vence en 5 meses** and a solid one on **Vencido** — and no state is readable from colour alone.
15. A row at zero on hand reads `Quiebre · hay 96 en Suba` on Existencias and the bar draws no fill, with the clause naming the single location holding the most units. That is the office screen's assertion and the whole of it — how the same figure renders on a till is S4's, over the other-location rows this stage provisions in *Offline*.
16. Before S6 exists, an overstocked row reads **Sobrestock** with no day figure, and the record panel says why. It never reads `Sobrestock · 0 días`.
17. Setting `reorder_point` by hand writes `source = manual`; the badge changes on the next page load, and the value survives everything in this stage.
18. **After the registry amendment, a delta pull with nothing pending still answers in under 20ms p95 server time**, and a fresh till at a 6-sede network reaches sellable in under 2.5s cold (§4).
19. Pull the network cable: Existencias shows a named route-scope error with a retry and no table; `Cargar mercancía` keeps accepting scans and the sync line reads `Sin conexión · 12 por enviar`; the transfer screens say plainly that they need the connection. Restore it and every queued line lands exactly once.
20. A `cashier` reaches Existencias read-only with their sede pre-selected, sees no `Cargar mercancía` and no `Nuevo traslado`, can record a **Merma**, cannot record an **Ajuste**, and a direct API call attempting one is refused (§2).
21. Every mutation in this stage lands an `audit_log` row with actor, entity and before/after — direct movement, dispatch, receipt, resolution, count close, policy write, settings PATCH, opening-stock run and rebuild. A mutating endpoint that writes no row fails the check.
22. Receiving 12 packs of a splittable blister of 30 shows `12 cajas · 360 unidades` before confirmation and writes a move of 360. Selling one tablet moves the projection by one.
23. `GET /api/stock/expiring` at the 90-day horizon returns a value and a lot count that reconcile to the sum of `quantity × lots.unit_cost` over the lots inside that window — the two figures behind `$18,9 M` and `142 lotes` — and changing `expiry_valuation_days` changes both without a deploy. The same response carries that location's and the network's total inventory value on the same `lots.unit_cost` basis, reconciling to `Σ quantity × lots.unit_cost` over every `stock_on_hand` row in scope; the expiring value over that total is the `4,6% del inventario valorizado` S9 renders, and a percentage whose numerator and denominator came from two cost bases is a number nobody can defend to an accountant.
24. An `Estado` sort puts **Vencido**, **Quiebre**, **Vence en 5 meses**, **Vence en 8 meses**, **Punto de reorden**, **Sobrestock** and **Suficiente** in that order — the ordinals of the derivation table above, where both expiry states outrank the reorder point — and the ordering survives paging. The two expiry labels are that way round because the default windows put them there: at `expiry_alert_days` 180 a five-month lot is `expiring_urgent` and at `expiry_notice_days` 365 an eight-month one is `expiring`. A sort that ranks `Punto de reorden` above an expiry state contradicts the derivation and sends someone to reorder a lot that will not sell before its date.
25. Every list surface answers `j`, `k`, `Enter`, `x` and `Esc`; every interactive element renders the single focus ring; no surface renders a spinner; no empty region reads `Sin datos` (§B.13.2, §B.5.1, §B.10.1, §B.10.2).
26. **Existencias rendered from the demo seed is indistinguishable in structure from the handoff's drawing.** Run the seed command on an empty database, open `/inventory` with no filter touched, and put it beside Pantalla 2: seven columns at their drawn widths, 48px rows, the in-cell bar present on the rows that carry a policy and absent on the rows that do not, the footer reading `1-15 de 4.284` with `312 requieren acción`, the `Sede · Todas · 6` chip, and **all five drawn states present** — **Suficiente**, **Punto de reorden**, **Sobrestock**, **Quiebre · hay N en `<sede>`** and **Vence en N meses** — with **Vencido** and the second expiry tier one press of the `Estado` sort away. This is the right test because it is the only one here that fails on a *full* screen: 1–25 check states that were designed, and a surface whose empty state was specified but whose populated state was never looked at is a surface nobody has actually reviewed — the tile whose denominator is zero, the `Quiebre · hay 96 en Suba` clause that overruns a 20% column, the badge nothing in the fixtures ever produced. **The stage is not finished until this passes** (§1), which is a sharper line than a green suite.

## Hands off

- **The stock ledger service**, with a fixed contract: append one or more moves and maintain the projection in the same transaction; enforce the sign per type and the lot rule per `items.tracks_lots`; write nothing for `tracks_stock = false` (A7); stamp `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, `device_id`, `user_id` and `client_uuid`; dedupe through S2's helper; **refuse no move on stock grounds**. S4, S6 and every later stage calls it and never writes `stock_moves` or `stock_on_hand` (ledger rule 7).
- **Refusal is a policy at the endpoint, never in the service.** The transfer-dispatch and supplier-return endpoints may refuse to exceed the projection under `negative_stock_block_outbound`; the sale path never asks. Putting the check inside the service would make the offline sale path depend on a rule that must not apply to it (§5 rule 2).
- **FEFO lot selection** as a callable: location, item, quantity in; lots in first-expired-first-out order out, expired lots excluded unless named. S4 calls it for every consuming line, and stamps `fefo_override` when a cashier names another lot.
- `stock_on_hand` per `(location, item, lot)`, and `GET /api/stock/availability` for one item across every location — the query behind S4's counter lookup and behind `hay N en <sede>`.
- **The negative-stock exception path**, already wired: S4 sells through the service, the conflict row appears, and S4 builds no handling of its own.
- `lots` with expiry, supplier, cost and sanitary registration, and `UNIQUE (tenant_id, item_id, lot_code)`. **S6 creates lots on receiving and writes `unit_cost`** through the same service (ledger).
- `stock_policies` with `source`. **S6 writes `model` rows and must preserve `manual` ones**, which is what the column is for (ledger, disputed columns).
- The `stock_move_type` enum, complete. S4 uses `sale` and `customer_return`; S6 uses `receipt` and `supplier_return`; nobody adds a value without a ledger row saying who causes it.
- The `inventory` settings group and its three expiry horizons, which S9's `Inventario por vencer · 90 días` tile reads through `GET /api/stock/expiring` rather than recomputing — an endpoint that returns the expiring value, its lot count **and** the total inventory value it is a percentage of, all at `lots.unit_cost`.
- Three registry entries on the till — `stock_on_hand` (own location, plus the capped other-location set for items in trouble), `lots` and `stock_policies` — so S4's counter has stock, expiry and thresholds locally at zero latency (A4).
- The lot trace, the projection rebuild, and the verify job, all runnable per location on a live system.
- The `Inventario` module's four routes and its breadcrumb menu, into which S6 adds nothing and S9 adds nothing.

## Gated on

**Nothing.** No question in architecture.md §11 blocks this stage: §11.1 is S5's, §11.3 is S8's, §11.4 is S7's, **§11.2 is explicitly not a blocker** — what a client's legacy system can export changes how fast S6's forecast sharpens, not whether this screen fills, because the demo seed fills it with no export at all (§1, *Cold start*) — and §11.5 changes what a pilot measures rather than what this stage builds. §11.6 — which sede goes first and who owns the catalog cleanup — is a scheduling risk on every stage and is not a technical dependency of this one.

Three names are **coined here** because neither the architecture nor the ledger supplies them, and they are flagged so a reviewer can rename or reject them: `stock_moves.reason` (a constrained text column over the vocabulary listed in *Data* — the ledger's `adjustment` row requires "a stated reason" and gives it no column), `stock_moves.note`, and `stock_moves.fefo_override`. The last one must be stamped rather than derived: whether the chosen lot was the FEFO head is unrecoverable once the projection moves on, which is the same argument the ledger already makes for `sale_lines.unit_cost`.

Four decisions were **taken here rather than escalated**, each stated with what breaks if it is wrong. Design-system §B.17·3 asked whether a `cashier` may log a manual movement — answered in the UI section: yes for `shrinkage` and `expiry`, no for `adjustment`. §B.17·2 asked which Existencias columns are expendable below 1440px — answered: none, the frame scrolls at a 1080px minimum with `Producto` sticky-left. The ledger's assignment of `receipt` to S6 means S3's standalone receipts and opening stock are `adjustment` rows, and cost of goods must therefore never be computed by filtering on the move type. And the expiry windows are fixed at 180 and 365 days, which is the one place this document knowingly departs from the prototype: **under these defaults the handoff's `Vence en 5 meses` row renders on the critical tint rather than the warning one.** The prototype's three expiry rows — 5 months warning, 6 months critical, 8 months critical — do not order by remaining life in either direction, so no month-count function reproduces them, and §B.7.4 fixes the rule as a window rather than as a month count. The window is a settings value a pilot moves in seconds; the alternative is a rule nobody can state. One leftover this correction leaves standing: the `Estado` derivation table above still carries the prototype's own example labels — `Vence en 6 meses` on `expiring_urgent` and `Vence en 5 meses` on `expiring` — which are the wrong way round under these windows, and the labels are §B.7.4's to restate rather than this document's, the rule being the window either way.
