# S4 — Counter

## Outcome

A cashier opens the browser at a counter, opens a **turno** with the cash in the drawer, and sells. A scan lands a line on the ticket; a typed fragment of a product name filters a local list as fast as the keys arrive; a quantity is adjusted with a thumb. **Cobrar** takes the payment — one method or several, cash with change — and the ticket closes with a receipt on screen. None of that touches the network. The cable can be out of the wall for the whole shift and the only thing the cashier notices is a line in the header that reads `Sin conexión · 12 por enviar` instead of `Sincronizado hace 4 s`, and a number that keeps climbing until the connection comes back and drains it without anyone being asked what to do about it (§5).

At the end of the shift the cashier counts the drawer and types what they counted. Botica stores the count, the expected figure, and the difference between them — under its own name, `variance`, and it stores it whether it is positive, negative or zero. A till that quietly reconciles its own shortfalls is a till nobody can audit, and the whole reason a droguería's owner buys this product is that today nobody can audit theirs (§6).

A customer comes back the next morning with an unopened box. The cashier finds yesterday's sale — still in the local store, still readable offline — takes back two of the four units, and the stock goes back on the shelf against the same lot it left on. The credit note that a return legally requires is not issued by Botica at all — the client's own invoicing system issues it (§8). This stage records the return and calls S5's sale handoff service, which renders it as a credit note referencing the original sale inside the canonical document and queues it; the record S4 hands over is complete enough that nobody re-derives a single peso from a price list.

What the office sees is the other half of the same data: every sale in the network, filterable by sede and by shift, every turno with its variance, every return, each one traceable to the device it happened on and the person who was signed in. What the office does **not** get is a way to make the counter stop selling. There is no setting in this stage that can refuse a sale, and that is deliberate: §5 rule 2 is binding, and a till that argues with a customer holding a box is a till that gets replaced by a paper notebook.

## Scope

### In

1. **`shifts`** — the **turno**: open with an `opening_float`, sell, close with a `declared_total`, and store the `variance` rather than suppressing it (§3, ledger disputed columns). One open shift per device, opened by the user who is signed in, sold into by whoever is signed in at the time.
2. **`sales`, `sale_lines`, `payments`** and the `sale_status` and `sale_source` enums (ledger). `sales.number` is the internal, per-location, locally allocated sale number and **is not the fiscal number** (ledger, disputed columns) — at v1 Botica allocates no fiscal number at all (A6, deferred), and `sales.number` is the key the client's invoicing system reconciles against (§8).
3. **`sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines`** — a return against a **closed** sale, whole or partial, moving stock back through S3's ledger service as `customer_return` (ledger).
4. **The keyboard-and-scanner capture path** — one always-focused capture field per till surface, the scan-versus-typing heuristic, the focus discipline that makes a scan land on the ticket regardless of what had focus, and the local product search behind it (§4, §12, design-system.md §B.13.3).
5. **The ticket** — indexed lines, quantity subtext, line totals, `Subtotal / Descuento / Total`, the average-ticket note and the 40px `Cobrar` action, in the 380px panel the handoff draws and the 420px panel counter density widens it to (handoff Pantalla 3, §A.19.4, §B.11).
6. **Split-pack selling, lot choice with a FEFO default, the line discount and the price override**, each with a stated rule and a stated role (§6).
7. **The `Cobro` flow** — payment split across `payment_method` values, cash tendered and change, the customer attach and the offline-capable customer registration behind it, the prescription acknowledgement, and the on-screen receipt (design-system.md §B.17 item 5 names this as the largest undrawn gap and assigns it here).
8. **Turno opening and closing at the counter**, both offline-capable, both client writes, with the close report showing declared, expected and variance side by side.
9. **The office's read of the counter** — a server-paginated list of sales, turnos and devoluciones on the `Mostrador` route for `owner` and `admin`, on the grid contract (§9), with a record panel per row.
10. **Void** — `sales.status = voided`, by the cashier inside their own open shift, or by an `owner`/`admin` at any time through an endpoint (ledger, disputed columns).
11. **Offline selling end to end** — the registry amendment that puts this stage's tables on the till, the push unit, the queue's behaviour across a browser restart, and what the customer walks away with when nothing has yet been handed to an invoicing system (§5).
12. **One job** — the stale-shift notice. Named in *Jobs* with its trigger, its key and its failure behaviour.
13. **The `Mostrador` nav counter** — `3` meaning ventas abiertas, from the local store for a `cashier` and from the server for an office role (design-system.md §B.8.2).
14. **This stage's demo-seed fixtures** — synthetic closed sales, shifts, payments and returns across the six sedes, five of them over 180 days and Usme deliberately cold, registered with S0's demo seed command, and the basis S6, S7, S8 and S9 are demonstrated from when the client has no history at all (§1, *Cold start*; ledger, cross-stage services).

### Out

- **The canonical sale document, its delivery to whatever invoicing system the client already runs, and the `fiscal_documents` row that records the attempt** — **S5** (§8, A9). S4 closes sales and records returns and calls S5's sale handoff service; it builds no payload, maps no field and reaches no target. What is *not* deferred to S5 is everything Botica has stopped doing: no CUDE, no signed XML, no DIAN transmission and no proveedor tecnológico contract, none of which is anyone's stage because none of it is in the product (§12); and no numbering lease and no `dian_resolutions` at v1 (A6, deferred), because the system that issues the fiscal document is the system that numbers it. The receipt this stage renders reserves the region S5 fills where a receiving system returns a number of its own, and claims nothing about the DIAN in the meantime.
- **The assistant** — the transcript card, the recommendation, the suggestion cards, the advisory notice, `modo local`, and every write to `sale_lines.from_suggestion` and `assistant_suggestions` — **S8** (§7, A8, ledger). S4 creates `from_suggestion` empty and never writes it (ledger, disputed columns). What occupies the left column until S8 arrives is fixed below under *UI*.
- **`sales.source = imported`** and the sales-history loader — **S6** (ledger, disputed columns). An imported sale was invoiced in the client's previous system long before Botica saw it, so it must never appear in a shift, in a cash reconciliation or in `fiscal_documents` — handing a month of history to an invoicing system is a month of duplicate invoices (§8). The constraint that enforces the first of those is created here.
- **Every `stock_moves` row** — written by **S3**'s ledger service, which S4 calls (rule 7, A3). S4 owns the *cause* of the `sale` and `customer_return` values, not the write.
- **The `sync_conflicts` table, its two enums and the office surface that reads them** — **S2**; and **the negative-stock exception a sale causes** — **S3** (ledger). S4's sale is what drives a lot below zero; S3 writes that row. What S4 does write into S2's table is its own two values, `stale_price` and `catalog_divergence` (*Data*).
- **Prices** — `item_prices` is S1's and S7's (ledger). S4 reads the effective price and stamps it; it never writes a price row, and a counter price override changes `sale_lines.unit_price` on one line and nothing in the price list.
- **`daily_metrics`, the Panel, the network's average ticket and the acceptance tile** — **S9**. The average-ticket note on the counter panel is computed locally from this location's own recent sales, stated below.
- **Thermal printing, a cash-drawer kick, a serial scale, a PDF receipt and a printed QR** — not built (§12). A scanner in HID keyboard mode needs no integration and is assumed (§12).
- **EPS and convenio dispensing, loyalty, a patient record, domicilios** — not built (§12). `customers` identifies an acquirer and recognises a returning customer; it is not a clinical history (§12).
- **A `tenants.settings` group for the counter.** The ledger's key register assigns S4 none, and S4 does not invent one — see *Gated on*.

**Money is stored tax-inclusive, and this is the single most likely arithmetic error in the stage.** A Colombian shelf price includes IVA, and the handoff's own ticket confirms it: `Subtotal $15.600 · Descuento $0 · Total $15.600`, with no tax line and no tax added to the total. So `sale_lines.unit_price` is what the customer pays per base unit, IVA included; `tax_amount` is the IVA **contained** in the line, derived from the line's `vat_class` (§3); `sales.subtotal` is the sum of line gross amounts before discount, `sales.discount` the sum of the discounts, `sales.total = subtotal − discount`, and `sales.tax` is the sum of `tax_amount` and is inside the total, never added to it. A build that adds `tax` to `total` produces a ticket 19% too expensive on cosmetics and exactly right on medicine, which is the kind of defect a pilot finds three weeks in.

**`sale_lines.unit_cost` is stamped from the lot at the moment of sale** (ledger, disputed columns). Margin joined later to a lot's *current* cost is wrong the first time a cost changes, and every margin figure on S9's Panel rests on this being stamped. For an item with no lot — a service, or an item whose `tracks_lots` is false — `unit_cost` is stamped from the item's last known cost and is zero where none exists. **`vat_class` is stamped for the same reason**: a tax class edited next month must not restate what was charged today.

**`quantity` is always in base units.** `stock_moves.quantity` is "signed in base units" (§3), and a sale line that meant something else would make the ledger and the ticket disagree. For a `splittable` item this means `item_prices.price` is the price of one base unit and `units_per_pack` is how many of them a pack holds — so selling a box is a line of `units_per_pack`, not a second unit of measure. **If the pilot's legacy export prices splittable items per pack, S1's loader must divide at load time**; if it does not, every split-pack line is priced by a factor of `units_per_pack` and the error is invisible until someone sells a single tablet.

**Two of §5's three reconciliations are S4's, and both are raised as an offline sale arrives** — inside the push transaction that applies it, one row per offending line, never as a refusal. **`stale_price`** is raised when a line's stamped `unit_price` differs from the `item_prices` row effective at `recorded_at`, which is what a price changed while the till was offline looks like on arrival: the sale stands at the price actually charged, `sale_lines.unit_price` is the record, and the row reports the difference to the office rather than correcting it (§5). **`catalog_divergence`** is raised when a line names an item deactivated while the till was offline: the sale stands and the item is flagged on arrival, because a cashier who sold a box that was on the shelf is right about the world and the catalog is late. In both cases `detail` carries the collection, the sale, the line, the item and the two figures or states — never the payload verbatim, which is S2's rule and applies to S4's rows too. The third reconciliation, negative stock, is S3's and is raised by the ledger service in the same transaction (ledger). **The two enum values ship in their own migration**, ahead of the code that writes them: Postgres will not let a value be used in the transaction that adds it, and Django's migrations are atomic.

## API surface

Every path carries the `/api/` prefix and is English (§3, ledger naming). Every endpoint runs behind S0's single permission dependency inside the pinned transaction (§2, A1, rule 6).

**No endpoint in this stage creates a counter sale.** Every sale, line, payment, shift and return originates on a device and arrives through S2's `POST /api/sync/push` (A5, §5 rule 5). A second write path would be a second way to allocate `sales.number` and a second way to move stock, and rule 7 has exactly one of the latter.

| Method | Path | Purpose | Who can call it |
|---|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/sales` | The sale list the office reads and the counter mirrors locally (§3 names this path for the Mostrador surface). Server-paginated on the grid contract (§9): sede, turno, `status`, `source`, period, cashier, and a free-text match on `number` | `owner`, `admin` across the network; `cashier` scoped to their own sede by S0's location helper (A2) |
| GET | `/api/sales/{id}` | One sale with its lines, payments, returns and the device and user that made it. What the record panel renders | Same |
| POST | `/api/sales/{id}/void` | Void a sale already received by the server, writing the reversing moves through S3's service and an `audit_log` row (§3, ledger). The cashier's own same-shift void is a client write and does not call this | `owner`, `admin` |
| GET | `/api/sale-returns` | The return list, same contract and same filters | `owner`, `admin`; `cashier` scoped to their sede |
| GET | `/api/sale-returns/{id}` | One return with its lines and the sale it reverses. **S5 reads this to build the return's credit note inside the canonical document** | Same |
| GET | `/api/shifts` | The turno list: sede, device, who opened, opened and closed, `opening_float`, `declared_total`, the recomputed expected total, and `variance` | `owner`, `admin`; `cashier` sees their own device's shifts |

`GET /api/sales` and `GET /api/sale-returns` are office reads. The counter never calls them: it reads the same rows from the local store at zero latency, which is the whole of §4's two-read-models boundary and the reason none of §4's counter budgets contains a request.

## Jobs

One, and the count is the point: **everything on the counter's critical path is synchronous by construction, and everything asynchronous is off that path entirely.** Applying a pushed sale — deduping it, appending its `sale` moves through S3's service and maintaining the projection — happens inside the push transaction, not in a job, because rule 7 requires the projection to be maintained in the same transaction as the moves.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stale_shift_notice` | Cron, daily, per tenant. Lists shifts still `open` more than 24 hours after `opened_at` and notifies the tenant's `owner` and `admin` through S0's transactional email | `(tenant_id, location_id, date)` — a re-run on the same day produces the same notice and sends nothing twice | Procrastinate retries with backoff. A missed run costs one day's notice and loses nothing; the shifts are still open and still listed on the Turnos surface |

**The job does not close anything.** Closing a cash session without a count destroys the count, which is the one number the session exists to produce. A shift left open is a fact an owner needs told, not a row a job should tidy.

- **Denied:** an `owner` or `admin` reaching this route lands on the office list instead, described below.

**The ticket** is §A.19.4 verbatim at counter density: header `VENTA EN CURSO` with the counter `3 ítems`, lines carrying a 20px index at `#909090` tabular, the name at `t-16`, the subtext at `t-12` (`2 × $3.900`), the amount at `t-16` tabular; totals pinned to the foot with `Subtotal`, `Descuento` and `Total` at `t-36`; the footer on L0 with the full-width 52px primary `Cobrar` and the centred `t-11` note `Ticket promedio del punto: $28.700`. A line whose item carries a flag shows it as a dot and label under the subtext: `Requiere receta` on warning, `Registro vencido` on critical, `Lote L-7730 · vence 08/2027` in neutral where the lot was overridden.

**The average-ticket note is computed locally** from this location's closed sales in the local retention window — the trailing seven days, which is what the till holds. Below 20 tickets in the window it renders `Ticket promedio del punto: — · sin datos suficientes`, per §B.9.2 tier 3. **Never a zero**: a zero standing in for "we do not know" is the most expensive lie an inventory system tells.

### The capture field, the scan and the focus discipline

**One capture field per till surface, always focused.** A document-level `keydown` handler redirects any printable character or `Enter` into it whenever focus is on the body, a ticket row, or any non-text control, and gives it focus in the same tick. A cashier will not click into a field before scanning, and a till whose focus is somewhere else is a till where the next scan goes into the void — discovered at a counter with a queue (§B.13.3). Focus returns to the capture field after every action: after adding a line, after adjusting a quantity, after closing a dialog (§B.13.3).

**The scan-versus-typing heuristic.** A scanner in HID keyboard mode is assumed and needs no integration (§12). A run of **six or more characters whose median inter-keystroke gap is under 30 ms, terminated by `Enter` within 400 ms of the first character**, is a scan: the whole buffer is resolved as one exact match against `item_barcodes.code` on a unique local index, the line is added, the field clears and focus stays. Anything slower is typing and drives the incremental local filter. **The heuristic is a latency optimisation and not a correctness gate**, and that is what makes it safe to be wrong: a typed string that exactly matches a barcode on `Enter` also adds a line, and a scan misclassified as typing still lands on `Enter` — the only cost of a misclassification is the keystroke-level filtering work that ran on the way. *If the pilot's scanners emit slower than 30 ms per character*, the threshold is one constant and the fallback path already produces the right answer.

**There is no debounce on the local search.** Debouncing exists to spare a server, and this path has no server on it; a debounce would add to the very budget §4 sets at 30 ms.

**A scan while `Cobro` is open does nothing.** The redirect handler is suspended for the duration of the dialog and the counter's capture field is disabled. A barcode arriving during payment is a cashier scanning the next customer's item, and adding it to a ticket that is being paid for is the worst outcome available.

### Adding a line — the rules

**Lot choice.** For an item with `tracks_lots`, the line takes the **FEFO** lot: the earliest `expires_at` among lots at this location with positive quantity in the local projection, ties broken by the oldest lot. The cashier overrides through the line's lot control, which lists each lot with its expiry (`MM/AAAA`, §A.11) and its quantity, and the override is recorded because `sale_lines.lot_id` names the lot that actually went out the door (§6). **Whether a line deviated from FEFO is stamped, not recomputed.** The line calls S3's FEFO selector, and where the cashier names another lot the move S3's service appends carries `fefo_override = true`, with the reason where one is given (§6, S3's *Data*). A stamp that could disagree with the ledger would be a second truth — and it cannot, because it is written by the same service, in the same transaction, as the move it describes, so there is one. Recomputing the deviation instead means replaying the ledger to the instant of that sale to reconstruct which lot FEFO would have chosen, which is expensive and fragile and answers a question nobody asks in that form: an audit asks for the stamp. S3's FEFO override policy in `/api/settings/inventory` governs whether the override needs a stated reason; it can never make the override impossible, because a cashier holding a box whose label disagrees with the screen is right and the screen is wrong.

**No lot available.** If every lot is at zero, the line takes the earliest-expiring lot anyway and S3's ledger raises the negative-stock exception to the office (§5 rule 2, ledger). If the location holds no lot of that item at all, `lot_id` is null and the same exception is raised. **A sale is never refused for want of a lot**, and no setting in this stage can make it so.

**Split packs.** Quantity is in base units, always. A `splittable` item's ticket line carries a `+ 1 caja (20)` affordance that steps the quantity by `units_per_pack`, and the `−` / `+` controls step by one; the subtext reads `20 × $650`. Nothing about the line, the move or the document handed to the invoicing system distinguishes a pack from twenty units, because nothing should.

**Line discount.** `sale_lines.discount` and `sales.discount` exist, default to zero, and **a `cashier` cannot write either.** §2 gives the role no pricing authority, and the ledger's settings register gives S4 no group in which a discount cap could live. The control is not rendered for a `cashier` — not rendered disabled (§B.8.3). An `owner` or `admin` signed in at the till gets it. This is why the handoff's drawn ticket reads `Descuento $0`.

**Price override.** `sale_lines.unit_price` is stamped from the effective `item_prices` row when the line is added, and a `cashier` cannot type a different one, for the same reason. An `owner` or `admin` signed in at the till can, on one line, and the override changes that line and nothing in the price list. *If the pilot needs a supervisor to authorise an override without a session switch*, that is a PIN elevation flow with its own audit trail, and it is not in v1 — the interim answer is that the administrator signs in.

**`requires_prescription` versus `invima_status = expired`, and they are not the same kind of thing.** A `requires_prescription` line is added like any other and carries the warning badge `Requiere receta`; `Cobrar` then shows **one** acknowledgement naming the lines that need one, with a single `Confirmar` — an acknowledgement that the cashier has seen the prescription, not an approval, and there is no field for a prescription number in v1. It never blocks: the item can be sold, by a person who has seen the prescription (§7). An item whose `invima_status` is `expired` gets the critical badge `Registro vencido` on its line and **nothing else** — no acknowledgement, no confirmation, no disabled row. Botica surfaces the state and records the pharmacy's own decision; it does not decide the consequence and does not validate against INVIMA's register (§3, §12, §B.7.4). The difference in treatment is the difference in kind: one is a control on how a sale is made, the other is a state of a product the pharmacy has already decided to keep selling. *If the regente needs the prescription itself recorded* — number, prescriber, date — that is a column on `sale_lines` and a field in `Cobro`, and it is a change to this stage's own table.

### `Cobro` — the payment flow

A 720px focus-trapped modal at counter density (§B.8.5, §B.11), scrimmed, restoring focus to the capture field on close.

The total at `t-36`. A method selector as a radio group over `payment_method`, rendered as `Efectivo`, `Débito`, `Crédito`, `Transferencia`, `Otro`. **`Efectivo` is preselected with the exact amount prefilled**, so the whole cash path is `F2` then `Enter` — two keystrokes, and that is what makes a till fast. `Recibe` and `Cambio` at `t-20` for cash. `Agregar otro medio` splits the payment: each method is a `payments` row with its own `amount`, `Débito`, `Crédito` and `Transferencia` carry a `reference`, and the dialog will not commit until the applied amounts sum to `total` — with cash permitted to exceed it, since the excess is change. **`payments.amount` for cash is the amount applied to the sale, not the amount tendered**; tendered and change are display figures and are not stored.

Below the methods: `Cliente` — an optional attach, defaulting to `Consumidor final`, searching the local slice by document or name and offering `Registrar cliente` when the person is not on it, which asks for `Tipo de documento`, `Documento` and `Nombre` and works with the cable out of the wall (*Offline*) — and the prescription acknowledgement when the ticket needs one. Footer: `[Cancelar: secondary] [Cobrar $15.600: primary, lg]` (§B.6.1, §B.6.2).

- **Loading:** none. Committing is a local write and the button's busy state (§B.6.2) is the only indicator that can appear at all.
- **Error:** field scope on an unbalanced split (`Faltan $2.400 por cubrir`), region scope on a rejected local write, naming the operation and offering a retry (§B.10.3). **No error here blocks the sale** — the sale can always be committed with cash for the full amount (§B.16 item 14).
- **Denied:** not applicable; every role that reaches the till may take payment.

### `Recibo` — the receipt

Rendered immediately on commit, from the local sale. Sede, **`sales.number`**, date and time from `occurred_at`, cashier, the lines with quantities and amounts, `Subtotal / Descuento / Total`, the payment breakdown and the change. A browser print view, and nothing else: there is no thermal printing, no cash-drawer kick and no PDF in v1 (§12).

**`sales.number` is on every receipt, including one rung up entirely offline, because it is the key both systems reconcile on** (§8). It is what the client's invoicing system stores against its own document and what an accountant matches a day of sales to a day of invoices with. A receipt without it makes that reconciliation manual, and it will be done at month end by someone who did not make the sale.

**The fiscal block is an empty region S5 fills, and S4 says nothing about the DIAN.** Claiming a document that does not exist is worse than claiming nothing — and with no invoicing target configured, which is the default and the state a demo runs in, there is no document to claim and the region is simply not rendered. `Vender otra` returns to a fresh ticket with the capture field focused.

### `Apertura de turno` and `Cierre de turno`

Both 560px modals at counter density, both offline-capable client writes.

**Opening** asks one thing: `Efectivo inicial en caja`, a currency field. `Abrir turno` writes the `shifts` row locally. **The till cannot sell without an open turno** — the route renders the opening dialog over an empty ticket, because a sale outside a cash session cannot be reconciled and the `CHECK` constraint above makes it impossible anyway. This is not a network block: opening is local.

**Closing** shows the shift's own arithmetic before it asks for anything: `Efectivo inicial`, `Ventas en efectivo`, `Devoluciones en efectivo`, and `Efectivo esperado`. Then one field, `Efectivo contado`, and the difference computed live as `Diferencia` — `#171717` at zero, and the §B.7 warning or critical family with its label above a threshold the surface states rather than guesses. `Cerrar turno` writes `declared_total`, `variance` and `closed_at`. **The variance is never suppressed, never rounded away and never hidden behind a confirmation that offers to make it zero** (ledger, disputed columns).

If operations are still queued, the dialog says so — `12 operaciones sin enviar. El turno se cierra igual y se envían cuando vuelva la conexión.` — and closes anyway. A cash count is not a sync gate.

- **Empty / loading:** not applicable; both dialogs are computed from local rows.
- **Error:** field scope on a non-numeric count; region scope on a failed local write.
- **Denied:** a `cashier` closes their own device's turno. Only an `owner` or `admin` reaches `force-close`, and it is not rendered at the counter at all.

### `Devolución`

Reached from a closed sale, at the counter or from the office record panel. Find the sale by `number` or from the last seven days' list; the lines render with a quantity stepper capped at what remains returnable after earlier returns; `Motivo` as a required textarea (§B.5.3); `Medio de reembolso` as the same `payment_method` radio group, defaulting to the method the sale was paid with. Confirming writes `sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines` and calls S3's ledger service for one `customer_return` move per line whose item has `tracks_stock`, **against the lot the line originally sold** — a returned box goes back to the lot it came from, or a recall becomes unanswerable (§6). It then calls S5's sale handoff service on the same path a sale close does (ledger, cross-stage services): S5 renders the return as a credit note referencing the original sale and queues it for delivery. S4 builds no payload and calls no target, here or anywhere (§8).

A return line stamps `unit_price`, `discount`, `vat_class`, `tax_amount` and `unit_cost` **from the original sale line**, not from today's price list. A credit note must reverse what was charged; a price that changed in between is exactly the case §5 says the sale's own record settles.

The sale stays `closed`. A fully-returned sale is a closed sale with returns against it, not a voided one.

- **Empty:** `Esta venta no tiene unidades por devolver.` with no action (§B.10.2).
- **Error:** region scope, naming the sale and the operation (§B.10.3).
- **Denied:** a `cashier` may return (§2). A return against a sale from another sede is refused in the content region naming the sede that owns it.

### Void

A `cashier` may void a sale **within their own open turno, and only while no return stands against it** — a mis-keyed ticket at 10:14 is corrected at 10:15 by the person who made it. Once the turno closes, only an `owner` or `admin` may void, through `POST /api/sales/{id}/void`. A permissive void is how a till is robbed; a strictly-office void means a mis-key at 20:00 waits for Monday. The same-shift boundary is the narrowest rule that fixes the common case, and it is the one this stage takes. A void writes the reversing moves through S3's service and sets `status = voided`; no row is ever deleted. Once S5 exists, a sale whose canonical document already reached the client's invoicing system is corrected in that system, by whatever instrument it issues — Botica records the void and issues no fiscal correction of its own (ledger, §8).

### `Mostrador` for the office

`owner` and `admin` land on a server-paginated list rather than a till, because the nav has seven items and §B.8.1 fixes that ceiling — an eighth item called `Ventas` would cost a cashier attention every day to save an administrator a click a month. The route header carries a three-segment control (§A.15.3) — `Ventas · Turnos · Devoluciones` — and each segment is the same filter bar + table + record panel shape (§B.8.5). *If design rejects a segmented control used as navigation*, the alternative is three rail items in the settings dialog; it is not a nav item, and this is where that decision lives.

**Ventas** — Standard density, columns `Venta 12 · Sede 12 · Turno 10 · Fecha 14 · Ítems 8 (der.) · Total 14 (der.) · Medio 14 · Estado 16`. `Estado` is the badge column (`Abierta` informative · `Cerrada` positive · `Anulada` neutral solid, §B.7.4). **Turnos** — `Sede 14 · Equipo 14 · Abrió 16 · Apertura 12 (der.) · Declarado 12 (der.) · Esperado 12 (der.) · Diferencia 10 (der.) · Estado 10`, with `Diferencia` taking the warning or critical colour and always restating itself in the record panel, so colour is never the only signal (§B.12.3). **Devoluciones** — `Devolución 12 · Venta 12 · Sede 12 · Fecha 14 · Ítems 8 (der.) · Total 14 (der.) · Motivo 28`.

The filter bar's right slot carries device provenance rather than a sync line, because this surface does not read a local store: `Equipos sincronizados hace 4 s · 1 sin conectar desde ayer 18:40`, read from `devices.last_synced_at` (§B.8.5, §B.9.1). That is the office's form of the same question a cashier asks of the chip.

- **Loading:** skeleton rows at the real heights on first paint; a re-fetch dims to `opacity:0.6` with the 2px progress line and never blanks (§B.10.1).
- **Empty:** never-populated on a fresh tenant — `Todavía no hay ventas` with `Abrir Mostrador` where the reader has a sede; filtered-to-nothing echoes the filters with a secondary `Quitar filtros` (§B.10.2).
- **Error:** route scope with a retry and a selectable correlation id (§B.10.3).
- **Denied:** a `cashier` never sees this shape; the same route gives them the till.

## Offline

This is the stage the offline contract exists for, and every counter surface above is offline-capable without qualification. Nothing in *UI* degrades when the connection drops except the two things named at the end of this section.

**What the till holds.** S4 amends S2's sync registry (rule 9) with six tables, each scoped by `location_id` and by a retention window, and each with a stated per-location estimate against §4's own load figure of 600 tickets per sede per day. The amendment lands in S2's document; the numbers are S4's to justify.

| Table | Predicate | Rows per location | Why the till needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shifts` | this location, `opened_at` within 30 days | ~60 | The open turno, and the recent closed ones the shift list shows |
| `sales` | this location, `occurred_at` within **7 days**, plus every `open` sale | ~4.200 | Returns against a recent sale, and the average-ticket note |
| `sale_lines` | via the same sales | ~12.600 | A return needs the lines and their stamped money |
| `payments` | via the same sales | ~4.700 | A return's default refund method, and the shift's cash arithmetic |
| `sale_returns` · `sale_return_lines` | this location, 7 days | ~200 · ~400 | What remains returnable on a line |

Seven days, not thirty: thirty is 18.000 sales and roughly 54.000 lines, which is no longer "a few megabytes" (§4) and pushes cold start against its 2.5s budget. Seven covers the return window a droguería actually sees and keeps the store small. **The predicate is the location, not the device** — a customer returns to the counter, not to a machine, so every till at a sede can serve a return rung up on any of them.

One further slice S4 needs on the device, scoped the same way: **`customers` seen at this location within 180 days** (~2.000–6.000 rows), for the attach lookup — and it is the one collection the till reads *and* writes without carrying the quartet, under the natural key instead (rule 8, second paragraph). The catalog, prices, lots and the stock projection are read-only on the device and are already local through S1's and S3's registry entries.

**What is authoritative, what is stale, what is unknown** (§B.9.2). The ticket's arithmetic, this sede's stock and prices, the open turno and this location's own recent sales are **tier 1** and carry no marker — marking the numbers that are right is how a marker stops meaning anything. **Money the customer is about to pay never carries a staleness marker**: `sale_lines.unit_price` records what was actually charged, and what can be behind is the price *list*, whose freshness is stated once in the sync panel. Another sede's stock, shown when a search result is out at this one, is **tier 2** and carries the 4px hollow grey dot with its reading `Suba · hace 3 min`. A figure that cannot be computed — the average-ticket note under 20 tickets — is **tier 3** and renders as `—` with its reason, never as a zero.

**What it queues, and as what.** Pushes carry events, never row states (§5 rule 5). The unit is a document: a closed sale pushes as one batch containing the `sales` row, its `sale_lines`, its `payments` — applied in one pinned transaction on the server, where S3's ledger service appends the `sale` moves and maintains the projection (rule 6, rule 7). **That same transaction is where the sale meets S5**: where an invoicing target is configured, it calls S5's sale handoff service, which builds the canonical document and enqueues its delivery and returns (ledger, cross-stage services). S4 assembles no payload, knows no target's field names and waits for no target; the delivery itself is S5's job and is never on the counter's path. Where no target is configured, that call is not made and nothing is enqueued (§8). A shift open and a shift close are each one row. A return is its header plus its lines. Every row carries its own `client_uuid`, so a push that times out after the server committed is retried and is a no-op (A5). **A customer registered at the counter is the one row S4 queues without a `client_uuid`**: it is master data, not an event, so it travels in the same batch as the sale that needed it and dedupes on `(tenant_id, document_type, document)`, the push answering `applied`, `duplicate` or `merged` rather than failing (rule 8, second paragraph). Two tills registering the same person during one blackout converge on one row instead of two, which is why the till's registration form requires the document type and the document — a row with neither has no natural key, and S2's unique index is partial on `document IS NOT NULL`.

**An `open` sale is pushed too**, on the ordinary delta cadence rather than per line. It moves no stock and is never handed to an invoicing system — only a closed sale is (§8); it exists on the server so that `Mostrador 3` is answerable to an owner and so a stranded ticket on a till that stopped reporting is visible (§B.8.2). Nothing on the counter waits for it.

**What the cashier sees.** The header's `SyncStatus`, in the words §B.9.1 fixes and no others: `Sincronizado hace 4 s` at rest with no dot; `Sincronizando · 3 pendientes` on informative; `Sin conexión · 12 por enviar` on warning with a **hollow** dot, because the system is waiting on something outside itself; `Sincronización con problemas · sesión vencida` on warning solid, with the reason always named and never `Error de sincronización`. It never spins, never animates, counts operations rather than a percentage, and must dwell two seconds before it changes so a flickering connection does not make a flickering chip. **Nothing in this stage can produce `blocked`, and at v1 nothing in any stage can.** `blocked` means stopping is safer than continuing, and the one condition that qualified — a device exhausting its fiscal numbering lease while offline — no longer exists: Botica allocates no fiscal numbers, and numbering leases are not built at v1 (§5, §8, A6, deferred). The state stays defined in the component with no producer behind it, because the day a till must genuinely stop selling is not the day to design that message from scratch. Storage persistence denied — the one condition S4 raises that needs an operator — is `degraded` with its reason named (§B.9.4), and `degraded` is a line in the chrome, not an interruption.

**Across a browser restart with an unsent sale.** The local store is IndexedDB with persistence requested at first run, and the replication persists its own checkpoint (§5). On restart the queue is intact, the checkpoint resumes, and the chip shows the same count it showed before the tab closed. **A ticket in progress survives too**: a `sales` row is written locally with `status = open` the moment its first line lands, which is what that enum value is for — a crash mid-ticket costs nothing, and the cashier finds the ticket where they left it. Two tabs on one till share one store and one replication through `multiInstance` (§5), so the second tab shows the same ticket rather than a second one.

**Storage persistence denied renders `degraded`, never `blocked`**, and §B.9.4 fixes the treatment: a one-time dialog at device claim, which explains what to change and which the operator dismisses, and after that a persistent `degraded` chip carrying the reason — `el navegador puede borrar los datos sin enviar` — for as long as the browser refuses. **The till keeps selling.** The data is at risk of eviction, which is worth telling an operator about and worth keeping in front of them; it is not worth refusing to sell over, because a shop that cannot sell has a certain loss where it had a possible one (§5). A design that stops a sale to protect a sale is not a safety measure (§B.16 item 14).

**What the customer walks away with, and what happens behind them.** At S4 they walk away with the receipt described above and no claim about the DIAN, because at S4 nothing has been handed to anyone. From S5 onward the sale still closes and the receipt still prints, and the handoff runs behind it: closing a sale queues a canonical document alongside the sale itself, the queue drains when the connection returns, and `fiscal_documents.status` walks `pending` → `sent` → `acknowledged`, with `failed` where the target rejected the delivery. Those states describe **Botica's handoff, not the DIAN's filing** — there is no `contingencia` and nothing is `accepted` or `rejected` by us — and a `failed` delivery lands on an administrator's work list, never on the cashier (§8, §B.7.4). **With no invoicing target configured the handoff does not exist at all**: nothing is queued, no `fiscal_documents` row is written, and no till surface, no chip and no Panel tile mentions it. That is the default, it is the state every demo runs in, and it is not an error — an integration nobody has configured is off, not failing (§8). **What S4 guarantees in either case is that the closed sale, its lines with their stamped `vat_class` and `tax_amount`, its payments and its customer — or the absence of one — are complete and immutable at the moment `Cobrar` commits**, so the canonical document is a derivation of a record nobody has to correct, whether it is built one second later or the month after a target is finally connected.

**Registering a customer works offline, and that is what makes an offline sale complete enough to hand over.** `customers` carries no `client_uuid` — it is S1's master-data table, not an event log — and it does not need one: it is pushed under the declared natural key `(tenant_id, document_type, document)`, and the arrival is `applied`, `duplicate` or `merged` rather than a failure (rule 8, second paragraph). Offline, the attach control searches the local slice and, when the person is not on it, `Registrar cliente` takes the document type, the document and the name, writes the row locally, and queues it with the sale that needed it. **The consequence belongs to S5**: the canonical document identifies the acquirer by document type, number and name (§8), so a till that could not name a customer during a blackout would hand over a document with a hole in it — and a field we did not send is a field a cashier re-types at the other system. It can name one. `Consumidor final` stays exactly what it was: what a ticket closes as when the customer declines to identify themselves, which is the customer's choice and not a limit of the till.

**One thing in this stage is online-only**, stated plainly rather than buried: **the office's Mostrador list, the force-close and the server-side void** are office surfaces and are unavailable offline, which is the correct answer for the office read model (§4) — they show the route-scope error with a retry, not a stale table.

## Demo seed

**S4 registers its fixtures with S0's demo seed command** (ledger, cross-stage services), and they carry more than this stage. §1 makes a client's sales history an accelerant and never a precondition, so where the export does not exist — the demo, the pilot's first week, the client whose fifteen-year-old system exports nothing usable — **synthetic `sales` and `sale_lines` are the only history the product has**. S6's demand forecast, S7's elasticity, S8's `cross_sell_rules` and every tile on S9's Panel read those two tables; without S4's fixture four stages render empty and nothing can be shown until somebody finishes a data migration, which is exactly the outcome §1 forbids.

What the fixture generates **under `default`**, and why each shape rather than a simpler one — the other four profiles are under the table:

| What | Shape | Why this shape |
|---|---|---|
| Closed sales | **180 days ending on the seed date** for the five established sedes, roughly 60–120 tickets per sede per day — about 82.000 `sales` and 240.000 `sale_lines`, Usme's three weeks included | **S9's widest period compares 90 days against the previous 90, and its comparator renders only where that previous window is fully covered**, so a seed of 120 days makes the Panel withhold its own comparison on `90 días` — on the screen the seed exists to demonstrate. The 30-bar histogram and S6's parametric floor are satisfied by far less; the comparator is what fixes the number at 180 |
| The cold sede | **Usme opened roughly three weeks before the seed date and carries only those three weeks of sales**, against the other five sedes' 180 days. It is Usme rather than an invented sede because it is the smallest in the handoff's own data — `$29 M` against Chapinero's `$112 M` — so a recently opened sede is plausible there rather than arbitrary | **it is the only thing in the seed that demonstrates the cold-start decision** (§1). With every sede on 180 days every sede is `learned`: S6's `Confianza del modelo` chip filters nothing, S8's card C is populated everywhere, and the parametric path — the thing that makes the product demonstrable on a client with no history at all — is never seen in a demo |
| Daily rhythm | a weekday shape with a Saturday peak and a thin Sunday, the quincena bumps on the 15th and the last day of the month, a morning and a late-afternoon peak within the day, and per-sede volume differing by roughly a factor of three **across the five established sedes, ranked as the handoff draws them** — Chapinero at the head, then Kennedy, Suba, Restrepo, Bosa, with Usme below all five on three weeks of trading | **a flat series makes every model look broken.** Uniform noise gives a forecast nothing to fit, gives the per-sede ranking a six-way tie, and gives a demo audience a histogram that looks like a rendering bug |
| Item mix | a long tail — a few dozen movers with enough observations to reach `learned`, and a majority too thin to leave `parametric` | this is what makes `Confianza del modelo` show more than one value on the Compras screen (§1). A seed where every item is equally well known hides the one thing the chip exists to say |
| Stamped money | `unit_price`, `vat_class` and `unit_cost` taken from the price and the lot effective **on the sale's own day**, not from today's row | margin has a trend instead of a flat line, and where S1's or S7's fixture moved a price inside the window there is real co-movement for S7 to read. Where no price moved, S7 withholds, which is the honest result and is the one §1 protects |
| Payment methods | cash-dominant — roughly half in `cash`, the rest across `debit_card`, `credit_card`, `transfer` and a little `other` | the turno's cash arithmetic is only meaningful when cash is a share rather than the whole, and `GET /api/shifts/{id}` has a breakdown to render |
| `from_suggestion` | **left false by this fixture** | S4 creates the column and never writes it (ledger, disputed columns — Writes is S8). What S4 seeds is the *tickets* the assistant will later credit; **S8's fixture sets the flag**, on exactly the lines its own seeded `assistant_suggestions` rows became, one line credited once under `UNIQUE (tenant_id, sale_line_id)`. A share set here would flag lines no suggestion row credits, break that invariant, and corrupt S9's `Ticket con sugerencia` split — and it would not help the donut either, which S9 computes wholly from `assistant_suggestions` and not from this column. Until S8 lands, the split reads `—`, which is tier 3 behaving correctly |
| Shifts | one per till per trading day, opened with a float and closed with a declared count; variances mostly zero or small in both directions, a few material shortfalls | **never uniformly zero.** A seed where every drawer reconciles perfectly makes `variance` look like decoration and hides the arithmetic error that would produce that same column of zeros in production |
| Returns | a few per sede across the window, partial, against the original lot, refunded at the money stamped on the original line | the devoluciones list, the return path and S9's return figures all have rows, and the return-against-original-lot rule is visible in seeded data rather than only in a test |
| The acquirer on a ticket, and **the customer S1 reserved for this stage** | a customer is named on a **minority** of tickets, drawn from S1's forty clientes — an acquirer is optional at a counter and a seed where every ticket carries one is a seed that never renders the ordinary case. Two of S1's three reserved rows are excluded from that draw **by name**: the one no fixture in any stage may reference, and the one seeded already erased. The third — **the row S1 reserves for this fixture to hang sales on** — is given a small, known set of closed sales at Chapinero, enough for a sale count worth printing and few enough to compare row by row | S1 settles the Ley 1581 deletion rule and can prove only its hard-delete branch, because at S1 nothing references `customers` and every delete takes it. **The erasure branch becomes reachable the moment `sales` exists, which is here**, and the row it runs against is seeded by name rather than attached on the spot — a check that begins *first, hang a sale on a customer* is a check nobody runs twice (S1, *The demo seed*, *Verification* check 9). Drawing the reserved rows into the ordinary customer draw would break both halves at once: the never-referenced row would gain a reference and the erased row would gain a name |

**The cold sede is S4's fixture to generate, and S6 and S8 consume it.** S4 owns `sales` and `sale_lines`; S6 may write only `source = imported` rows and S8 writes neither table (ledger, disputed columns), so neither of them can arrange an uneven sales history and neither describes one as something it sets up. **S6 reads Usme as the one sede that stays `parametric`**, which is what gives `Confianza del modelo` more than one value to filter on the Compras screen; **S8 reads it as the one sede with no mined `cross_sell_rules`**, which is what makes card C's cold path visible in a demo — three cards at Chapinero, two at Usme. Two stages depend on a shape only this fixture can produce, which is why the per-sede window is a stated parameter of the seed and not whatever range the generator's loop happened to run over.

**The profile is an argument to S0's command and this fixture answers all five** (§1). **Two of the five are defined entirely by what this fixture does**: §1 fixes `young` as twelve days of history and `cold` as no sales at all, and history is `sales` and `sale_lines`, which are this stage's and no other's (ledger). A fixture that answered only `default` would leave S7 fitting an engine on a `cold` tenant that still has sales, S9 checking a withheld comparator against 180 days of `young`, and S8 mining rules on the one profile whose point is that there are none — and it would not get that far, because S0's registry fails a run outright when a fixture does not answer a profile, and a case omitted and a case that builds nothing are the same row count and a different defect (S0, *The demo seed*).

| Profile | What this stage's fixture builds |
|---|---|
| `default` | the table above in full: 180 days ending on the seed date at Chapinero, Kennedy, Suba, Restrepo and Bosa and about three weeks at Usme, ≈82.000 `sales` and ≈240.000 `sale_lines`, one turno per till per trading day with real variances, returns at every sede, and a customer named on a minority of tickets |
| `young` | **the same six sedes with twelve days of history, network-wide.** The young profile is a young *network*, not a young sede: **Usme is not a cold sede inside it** and all six windows are the same twelve days. Same generator, same weekday and Saturday shape, the window placed so one quincena bump falls inside it, the same payment mix, a turno per till per trading day and a return or two so the list is not empty. **Nothing else shrinks** — the catalog is still S1's 4.284 and the stock is still S3's, because a network that opened three weeks ago stocks a full shop. *What breaks if this is wrong:* twelve days is the figure S9's withheld comparators are checked against and the floor S7's estimator is checked against — a `young` carrying thirty days lets a 30-day comparison compute, and `Sin periodo previo · 12 días de historia`, the clause that whole section exists to prove, never renders |
| `cold` | **no rows at all** — zero `sales`, zero `sale_lines`, zero `payments`, zero `shifts`, zero `sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines`, and not one `stock_moves` row of type `sale`. S1's catalog is there and S3's stock is there; **nothing has been sold.** The fixture returns empty explicitly rather than omitting the profile. *What breaks if this is wrong:* a single seeded sale here makes S7's sales-free path, S8's empty rule set and S6's parametric order all unreachable — the three states this profile exists for — and each of the three is then reached by deleting rows by hand, which is the check §1 forbids |
| `scale` | **the same shape across twenty locations**: the same 180-day window and the same daily rhythm over the nineteen `store` sedes, ranked with a head and a tail rather than twenty sedes of equal size, and **nothing at all at the network's one `warehouse`** — no turno, no sale, no return — because a bodega rings none, and it is the location a per-sede list has to render without dividing by zero. Roughly three times `default`'s volume, ≈260.000 `sales` and ≈780.000 `sale_lines`, **and that size is the point**: §4's Panel budget at twenty sedes and S9's and S10's row-count checks cannot be measured on six. It is the slowest of the five to build, and where the build time is the obstacle the fix is the batched entry point on S3's service named below, never a direct insert |
| `minimal` | one sede, one till, and the single `owner` at the counter because the profile has no `cashier` — **a few days of sales, a handful of tickets a day, one turno a day and one return.** Small, and never zero: this is the tenant the isolation checks are isolated *from*, and a cross-tenant `sales` count of zero proves nothing when the other tenant has no sales either. Building a second network's 180 days to prove a pin would be a slow way to prove it |

**The fixture reads the tenant it was handed rather than assuming `default`'s shape.** It generates over whatever locations exist, over whatever tills S2's fixture claimed at each, and attributes each sale to the sede's own `cashier` where there is one and to the network's single `owner` under `minimal` — and it takes its window from the profile rather than from wherever a loop stopped. A fixture that hard-coded six sedes, six cashiers and 180 days passes on one profile out of five and fails on the other four inside somebody else's session, on a run they did not change.

**Every row this fixture writes carries `source = counter`, and every one of them is synthetic.** They are not S6's `imported` history, which is a different thing with a different origin: an imported sale was rung up and invoiced in the client's previous system long before Botica existed, and it must never appear in a shift, a cash reconciliation or a fiscal handoff (*Out*, ledger). The two share the tables and nothing else. **Conflating them puts fabricated sales into a real tenant's books** — and, from S5 onward, into whatever system that tenant invoices with, which is a month of invented invoices and the worst failure available to this stage. The seed builds its own tenant and is never loaded into a client's (§1, ledger).

**Seeded stock moves through S3's ledger service, never around it** (rule 7, A3). Every seeded sale consumes stock the way a real one does, so `stock_on_hand` agrees with the sales that caused it; a fixture that inserted rows and left the projection alone would produce an Existencias screen contradicting the Panel, and the first person to notice would be a prospect standing in front of both. The fixture runs after S3's, consumes only what the lots at that sede actually hold on the day, and leaves the deliberate negative-stock exceptions to S3's fixture, which owns that surface. Where 240.000 ledger appends make the command slow, **the fix is a batched entry point on S3's service, never a direct insert into `stock_moves`.**

**The counter's own invariants hold inside the seed**, because a fixture that violates them is a fixture that hides a bug: every seeded counter sale sits inside a turno (the `CHECK`), `sales.number` is composed `{device code}-{per-device sequence}` so a seeded ticket reads like the handoff's `Venta C3-4821`, each sale carries the `device_id` of a till S2's fixture claimed at that sede, and `occurred_at` precedes `recorded_at` by a plausible interval — with a handful of sales carrying a multi-hour gap, so the offline path and rule 8's *account on `recorded_at`* are visible in the data and not only in prose. **No `fiscal_documents` row exists in the seed tenant**: no invoicing target is configured, nothing is handed anywhere, and that is the default state rather than an error (§8).

**The demo seed is not the load fixture, and `scale` does not make it one.** §4's 600 tickets per sede per day is what the performance harness runs against when the budgets in *Acceptance* are measured; the demo seed is sized to look right and to build in a reasonable time, and `scale` is sized so a measurement across twenty sedes is meaningful rather than so one till is loaded to its peak. The four counter budgets are measured on `default` at one sede against the harness's volumes (*Verification*). Merging the two gives a seed too slow to run before a demo and a budget nobody measured.

**The completion test is §1's, and it is sharper than the suite**: this stage is not finished until the Mostrador renders convincingly from the seed. A till opened on any of the six sedes finds its turno and sells; the office's list fills with sales, turnos carrying real variances and devoluciones across all six; the average-ticket note computes instead of reading `—`. It catches what tests do not — the empty state nobody designed, and the tile whose denominator is zero. It does not replace the empty states: a fresh tenant is still how those are exercised, and criterion 28 still holds. **And it is a test per profile rather than once**: `default` is the one that must look like the handoff, `young` is what a client's first fortnight looks like and must not read as a Panel that is broken, and `cold` must render a till that has never sold anything without one tile dividing by zero — a profile whose screens nobody opened is a profile whose states nobody reviewed (§1).

## Acceptance

Every budget below is measured on the pilot's hardware, not a developer's machine (§4, §11.5).

1. **Keystroke to filtered product list is under 30 ms p95, with the network interface disabled**, over at least 4.000 catalog rows at the location. A network request appearing anywhere on that path fails the criterion regardless of the timing (§4).
2. **A barcode scan to the line appearing on the ticket is under 50 ms p95**, offline, measured from the scanner's terminating `Enter` (§4).
3. **From the commit inside `Cobro` to the receipt painted is under 200 ms p95, offline included**, and opening `Cobro` from the ticket's `Cobrar` is under 100 ms. *The architecture phrases this budget as "`Cobrar` pressed to the ticket closed"; the dialog between the two is human time and cannot sit in a machine budget, so it is measured in two halves and both are stated.* The sum of machine time from the first press to the receipt stays under 300 ms (§4).
4. **Cold start to sellable is under 2.5 s** on a warm cache, and **it does not wait for a sync**: the till is sellable as soon as the catalog, the prices and the device's open turno are readable from the local store (§4).
5. Nothing on any of paths 1–4 makes a network call. Demonstrated by completing a full sale — search, three scanned lines, a split payment, a receipt — with the interface disabled from before the route loaded.
6. A scan lands on the ticket when focus is on a ticket row, on the `Cobrar` button, and on the page body with nothing focused at all, without the cashier clicking anything first.
7. A code matching no barcode leaves the code visible in the field with `Código no encontrado · {code}` beneath it, and raises no toast and no dialog.
8. A scan arriving while `Cobro` is open adds nothing to the ticket being paid for.
9. Selling an item with `tracks_lots` takes the earliest-expiring lot at that location without the cashier choosing; overriding to a later lot is possible in two actions, and the sold lot appears on `sale_lines.lot_id` and on the resulting `stock_moves` row, which carries `fefo_override = true` and the reason where the policy asks for one.
10. A sale of an item whose local stock is zero **closes normally**, and the negative-stock exception appears on the office's side after the push. No configuration makes the till refuse it (§5 rule 2).
11. A `splittable` item sold as one pack and as `units_per_pack` single units produces identical `quantity`, identical `total` and identical stock movement.
12. A `cashier` sees no discount control and no price-override control on the ticket; an `admin` signed in on the same device sees both. Neither is rendered disabled (§B.8.3).
13. A ticket containing a `requires_prescription` item shows one acknowledgement at `Cobrar` and closes on a single confirm. A ticket containing an item whose `invima_status` is `expired` shows a badge on the line and asks nothing.
14. A ticket's `Subtotal`, `Descuento` and `Total` satisfy `total = subtotal − discount`, and `sales.tax` is contained within `total` rather than added to it — checked on a mixed ticket carrying `excluded` and `rate_19` lines.
15. A turno opens with a float, takes eleven sales across three payment methods, and closes with a declared count that is **short by $3.000**; the shift record shows `Efectivo esperado`, `Efectivo contado` and `Diferencia −$3.000`, and `variance` is stored with that value. Nothing in the flow offers to make it zero.
16. A turno closes with 12 operations still queued, and those operations arrive later and are attributed to that turno.
17. A return against a closed sale takes two of four units back, the stock returns to the **original lot**, and the return's money is stamped from the original line — demonstrated by changing the item's price between the sale and the return and showing the refund unchanged.
18. `sales.number` is present on every sale including one rung up entirely offline, is unique within the location across two devices selling simultaneously, and is visibly **not** a fiscal number — Botica allocates none (A6) — and there is no `fiscal_documents` row anywhere in this stage.
19. An offline sale, a browser quit, a browser relaunch, and then the network restored: the sale is still queued after the relaunch, drains without anyone pressing anything, and appears once — not twice — in `GET /api/sales`.
20. A ticket with two lines survives a browser crash and is found intact, with both lines, on relaunch.
21. Two tabs open on one till show one ticket, not two.
22. With persistence denied, the till shows the one-time dialog at claim and then the `degraded` chip with its reason named, **and keeps selling**. No path in this stage puts the till in `blocked`, and at v1 no path in any stage does — the state exists in the component with no producer at all (§B.9.4, §5).
23. `SyncStatus` renders at rest as text with no dot, shows `Sin conexión · 12 por enviar` with a hollow warning dot when the cable is out, names its reason when degraded, and does not flicker when the connection drops and returns four times in a second.
24. A `cashier` reaching `/api/sales` for another sede gets nothing for that sede; an `owner` reading the same endpoint sees every sede in one query (A2).
25. Every mutation an `owner` or `admin` makes through this stage's endpoints — a void, a force-close — lands an `audit_log` row with the actor, the entity and both values (§3, ledger).
26. The till renders at 1280 × 720 with no horizontal scroll: the 64px rail, the 28px inset, the 420px panel and the working column (§B.11).
27. No till surface binds a single-letter shortcut, `F2` cobra, `F4` focuses the capture field, `F8` opens the sync panel, and `Esc` clears the capture field **without ever cancelling the sale** (§B.13.3).
28. No surface in this stage renders a spinner outside a button already pressed; every empty state carries an action or says why it does not; no error message reads `Ocurrió un error` (§B.10).
29. **A customer is registered at a till with the network interface disabled**, the sale closes with that customer attached, and on reconnection both arrive in one batch: the customer `applied`, or `merged` onto the existing row when a second till registered the same document during the same blackout, and never a duplicate. Nothing on that path requires the network to name an acquirer.
30. An offline sale whose line price no longer matches the effective price on arrival, and an offline sale of an item deactivated while the till was offline, each close normally, each stand at what was charged, and each raise one `sync_conflicts` row — `stale_price` and `catalog_divergence` — visible on S2's office surface with the sede, the sale and the item named.
31. **The Mostrador renders convincingly from the demo seed with no client history loaded** — measured without reference to `from_suggestion`, which this stage leaves false and S8's fixture sets — (§1, *Cold start*), which is this stage's completion test and not merely a criterion. After S0's seed command runs: a till opened on any of the six sedes finds its open turno and completes a sale; the office list shows sales, turnos with non-zero variances and devoluciones across all six sedes; the daily volumes have a weekday and quincena shape rather than a flat line; the average-ticket note computes rather than rendering `—`; a share of `sale_lines` carries `from_suggestion = true`; every seeded sale carries `source = counter`, sits inside a shift and has no `fiscal_documents` row; and `stock_on_hand` for every seeded lot equals the sum of its `stock_moves`, because the fixture appended them through S3's service.
32. **The seed's shape is checkable rather than eyeballed, because two later stages are built on it.** Grouped by location, the seeded sales span **180 days ending on the seed date at Chapinero, Kennedy, Suba, Restrepo and Bosa** and **about three weeks at Usme**; the six per-sede totals are ordered as the handoff's `Venta por sede` list draws them, with Usme last; and **every row in the set carries `source = counter`**, with no `imported` row anywhere in the seed tenant. Two consequences are checked on the screens rather than in the fixture: S9's Panel at `90 días` renders a previous-period comparison on each of the three cards that draw one, instead of the `Sin periodo previo` clause; and Usme is the one sede whose references stay `parametric`. A seed of 120 days, or one with six warm sedes, passes every other criterion on this list and still fails the demo.

## Verification

Everything below runs on the demo seed and on nothing else: **`seed_demo_tenant --profile default`** against an empty tenant, a till claimed at **Chapinero** and a second at **Suba**, the seeded `cashier` at the counter and the seeded `owner` on the office surfaces (§1). **The profile is always named, because there is no bare invocation to make** — S0's command refuses one and prints the five names, so a check written as `seed_demo_tenant` alone fails at the first command of the session (S0, *The demo seed*). **Every check below names its profile**, and it is `default` unless the check says otherwise: the four budgets, the offline run, the seed-shape queries and the Ley 1581 erasure are all `default`, and the one check that reads the other four says which and why. **An unusual state is reached by asking for a profile, never by editing rows** — a check that begins *first, delete some sales* is a check nobody runs twice (§1). No client export, no hand-made rows — that is the state every session starts in, and a check that needs anything else is a check nobody will run again. Where a step says *offline*, it means the machine's network interface is switched off rather than the browser's throttle, so a request that should not exist fails loudly instead of being answered from somewhere.

### Checks

**The four budgets are measured, not felt.** On the pilot's own till machine, at its own screen with 1280 × 720 as the floor (§B.11), in the browser those machines run, on the **`default`** profile, with its Chapinero catalog, prices and stock in the local store — all four are `default` and none of them is measured on `scale`, whose twenty sedes change what an office read costs and change nothing a till does. Each figure is the **p95 of at least 200 samples** taken from the browser's own performance timeline — a mark at the input event, a mark at the frame that paints the result — and p50 is reported beside it, because a p50 of 4 ms under a p95 of 29 ms is a different machine from one sitting flat at 25 ms and only the second will still pass next month. A stopwatch, a single run, an average, or a developer's laptop is not a measurement of any of these. **§11.5 is still open**: until the pilot names its machines these figures are provisional and recorded as such, and the stage is not passed on them (*Gated on*).

**The offline run, on `default`, in one sitting, with the interface off before the route loads.** This is the stage, and it is one sequence rather than eight criteria: (1) open Mostrador at Chapinero and open a turno with a float; (2) sell — type a fragment and add from the list, scan three seeded codes, step one quantity, `Cobrar`, split across `Efectivo` and `Débito`, commit — and read the receipt: `sales.number`, the lines, `Subtotal / Descuento / Total`, the payment breakdown, **no fiscal region and no mention of the DIAN** (§8), with the chip on `Sin conexión · n por enviar` and a hollow dot; (3) start a second ticket, add two lines, and quit the browser from the operating system rather than closing the tab; (4) relaunch, still offline — the queue count is what it was and the second ticket is intact with both lines; (5) open a second tab — one ticket, not two; (6) close the turno declaring **$3.000 short** — the dialog names the queued operations, closes anyway, and stores `variance = −3000` with nothing offering to zero it; (7) switch the interface back on and **touch nothing** — within one pull interval the chip returns to `Sincronizado hace …` and the count reaches zero; (8) on the server, `GET /api/sales` filtered to Chapinero and today shows the sale **once**, the shift shows declared, expected and difference, and re-sending the same push batch — the retry an unreliable network performs on its own — creates no second row and changes no count (A5). **"Renders convincingly from the seed" is a set of readings, not a feeling** (§1, acceptance 31), and it is read on **`default`**. On the office's Mostrador: `Ventas` reports a five-figure `rowCount` with all six sedes present on the first page at any sort; `Turnos` shows one shift per till per trading day with `Diferencia` non-zero on a visible minority and zero on most — never a column of zeros, which is what the same bug in the arithmetic would produce; `Devoluciones` is non-empty at every sede. On a till at each of the six: the route finds an open turno and completes a sale, and the panel's note reads a peso figure rather than `— · sin datos suficientes`.

**Two `default` seed shapes are queried rather than looked at, because S6, S8 and S9 are built on them** (acceptance 32). Group `sales` by `location_id`: `min(occurred_at)` is about 180 days back at Chapinero, Kennedy, Suba, Restrepo and Bosa and about three weeks back at **Usme**, and `sum(total)` orders the six exactly as the handoff's `Venta por sede` list draws them with Usme last. Then, across the tenant: `count(*) WHERE source = 'imported'` is 0, `count(*) WHERE shift_id IS NULL AND source = 'counter'` is 0, `fiscal_documents` holds no row, and summing `stock_moves.quantity` per `(location_id, item_id, lot_id)` against `stock_on_hand.quantity` yields **zero disagreeing rows** — that last one is what proves the fixture appended through S3's service rather than around it.

**The other four profiles are queried too, because two of them are this fixture's whole definition** (*Demo seed*). Seed each into its own tenant and ask one question of each. On **`young`**: the span from `min(occurred_at)` to `max(occurred_at)` is about twelve days, **and it is twelve days at all six sedes** — group by `location_id` and read six windows of the same length, not twelve days at one sede and 180 at the rest — with no sale anywhere older than the window. On **`cold`**: `count(*)` is **0** on `sales`, `sale_lines`, `payments`, `shifts`, `sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines`, and `count(*) FROM stock_moves WHERE type = 'sale'` is 0, **while `items` and `stock_on_hand` both come back non-empty under the same pin** — that second half is what separates a seeded `cold` from a run that failed silently, and without it a broken seed reads as a passing check. On **`scale`**: twenty locations, sales at nineteen of them, **zero rows of every kind at the `warehouse`**, and per-sede totals with a head and a tail rather than a twenty-way tie. On **`minimal`**: the single sede has sales, every one of them sits inside one of its turnos, and the same counts run under `default`'s pin return **none of them** — a zero that means something only because `minimal` has rows to be missing (S0, isolation). *What breaks if these are not run:* each is a state another stage's checks stand on, and each fails three sessions later, in a session that never touched this fixture.

**The Ley 1581 erasure, which is the half S1 could not run** (S1 acceptance 25 and *Verification* check 9; profile `default`). S1 decides the rule and can prove only its first branch: at S1 nothing references `customers`, so every delete is a hard delete. **The other branch becomes reachable the moment `sales` exists, which is this session, and it is the only legally load-bearing mutation in the catalog** — it runs here or it is run by nobody. Against **the customer S1's fixture reserves for this stage** (*Demo seed*), as `owner`: first read its sales — how many, and each one's `id`, `total`, `customer_id` and `updated_at` — then `DELETE /api/customers/{id}`. It passes when the response **names the erasure branch and the true sale count** rather than a zero; `GET /api/customers/{id}` **still returns the row**, because a 404 here is the failure and not the pass; `document_type`, `document`, `name`, `phone`, `email`, `address`, `notes`, `data_consent` and `data_consent_at` all read back **empty**; every one of those sales still carries the same `customer_id`, `total` and `updated_at`, all four unchanged, and the sale still opens on the office's Mostrador with its lines intact; `Clientes` renders **`Cliente eliminado`** in `#909090` (§B.9.2, tier 3) **derived from the absent name and document**, with no boolean, timestamp or status on `customers` behind it and none added by this stage; and one `audit_log` row carries the actor with before and after. A 404, a `customer_id` gone null, a cascaded sale, a touched `updated_at` or the string `Cliente eliminado` found stored in a column is the failure — and the first three are data loss rather than a wrong screen, on the one button a droguería is legally obliged to be able to press.

### What this stage would break

The counter is the first heavy writer in the product, and it writes through three earlier stages' services rather than its own. Each row below is one earlier behaviour this stage's work can plausibly break and the single check that catches it, run at the end of every session that touched this stage.

| What could break | The check |
|---|---|
| **S3's ledger and its projection** (rule 7, A3) — a sale path that inserts a move directly, or maintains `stock_on_hand` outside the move's transaction | Rebuild the projection from `stock_moves` and compare it to the live table: zero differing rows before and after a session of counter sales. A drift of exactly one ticket's worth names the culprit |
| **S3's FEFO selector** (§6) — an S4 path that picks a lot itself to save a call | Sell one `tracks_lots` item with no choice made and confirm the lot is the earliest-expiring with stock; a move with no `fefo_override` value at all means S4 wrote it |
| **S2's delta cursor** (rule 4, §4) — six new synced tables, and a child that has to join its parent to answer the pull predicate | Delta pull with nothing pending stays under 20 ms p95 server time and returns zero rows, measured with this stage's tables on the registry |
| **S2's dedupe and single-transaction push** (A5, rule 8) — a batch that partially applies, or a `client_uuid` not carried on one of the six tables | Re-send one closed sale's batch and confirm no count anywhere changes; then send a batch whose last row is invalid and confirm nothing from it landed |
| **S2's store size and the cold-start budget** — a retention window quietly widened past seven days | After the seed, read the till's store size and re-measure cold start: over 2.5 s means the predicate grew |
| **S1's price rows** (A11, ledger) — an override at the till reaching `item_prices` | Count and checksum `item_prices` before and after a session containing a till price override: unchanged. The override lives on one `sale_lines.unit_price` and nowhere else |
| **S1's `customers`** — S4 becoming the only writer that works, breaking the office's own `POST /api/customers` | Create a customer from the office, then register the same document at a till offline and drain: one row, `merged`, no rejection of either path |
| **S1's Ley 1581 erasure branch** (S1, *The Ley 1581 deletion*) — `sales.customer_id` declared `ON DELETE CASCADE` or `ON DELETE SET NULL`, which reads as referential tidiness and is data loss the first time an administrator presses a legally required button | Read the constraint on `sales.customer_id`: it is neither, and the erasure check above passes with every sale intact. A cascade deletes the sales; a `SET NULL` keeps them and loses the acquirer S5 has to name on the canonical document |
| **S0's tenant pin and role scoping** (A1, A2, rule 6) — a new endpoint or the daily job reaching the database unpinned | The `stale_shift_notice` job run without its tenant argument refuses to start, and the cashier scope check above returns zero rows for another sede |
| **S1's and S3's own demo screens** — this fixture adds the largest data volume in the seed and runs after theirs | After a full `seed_demo_tenant --profile default`, the catalog grid still reads `1-15 de 4.284` and the Existencias screen still shows S3's own drawn rows and its deliberate negative-stock exceptions |

## Hands off

- **`shifts`, `sales`, `sale_lines`, `payments`, `sale_returns`, `sale_return_lines`**, with the `sale_status`, `sale_source`, `shift_status` and `payment_method` enums, all under the client-write quartet and S2's dedupe (rule 8, A5).
- **A closed sale is complete and immutable at commit**: its lines carry `unit_price`, `discount`, `vat_class`, `tax_amount` and `unit_cost` stamped at the moment of sale, and its payments carry method, amount and reference. **S5's sale handoff service builds the canonical sale document from this record and never re-derives a peso from a price list or a lot's current cost.**
- **`sales.number` is the internal per-location number, it is printed on every receipt, and it is the key both systems reconcile on** (§8). Botica allocates no fiscal number at v1 — numbering leases and `dian_resolutions` are not built (A6, deferred) — so the fiscal number is whatever the receiving system issued, landing on `fiscal_documents.external_number` where a target returns one and null where it does not (ledger). Two numbers, two tables, never one column: conflating them is the most likely modelling error in the product (ledger).
- **`sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines` carry everything a credit note needs** — the sale it reverses, the lines it reverses, and the money as it was originally charged. S5 renders the return as a credit note referencing the original sale inside the same canonical document and delivers it to the same target under the same `document_key` discipline; the credit note itself is issued by the receiving system, not by us (§8).
- **`sales.source = counter` on every row this stage writes**, and the `CHECK` that keeps an imported sale out of a shift. **S6** writes `imported` rows against the same tables and inherits that constraint (ledger, disputed columns).
- **A synthetic sales history exists before any client history does.** S4's demo-seed fixtures are what **S6**, **S7**, **S8** and **S9** are demonstrated from where the client's export is missing or unusable (§1, *Cold start*): the forecast leaves the parametric floor on the seeded movers and stays on it for the thin tail, elasticity appears only for items whose seeded price actually moved, the cross-sell miner has baskets, and the Panel's tiles have denominators. Every one of those rows is `source = counter` and synthetic, and none of it is confusable with S6's `imported` history (ledger). **One sede is deliberately cold**: Usme carries about three weeks against the other five sedes' 180 days, which is the fixture **S6** reads as its one `parametric` sede and **S8** reads as its one sede with no mined `cross_sell_rules`. Neither of them can write it — S6 writes only `imported` rows and S8 writes neither table — so it is produced here or it does not exist.
- **A sales history exists from the day the till goes live** — item, lot, location, quantity, price actually charged, cost actually stamped, and both clocks. This is what **S6**'s demand forecast, **S7**'s elasticity estimates and **S8**'s `cross_sell_rules` learn from, and it is why all three depend on S4 rather than on S3 (§13).
- **`sale_lines.from_suggestion` exists, defaults to false, and is never written here.** **S8** writes it when `Agregar` is pressed on a suggestion card, and it is the single column that makes the Panel's `58,6% de sugerencias aceptadas` answerable (ledger, disputed columns).
- **The left column of Mostrador is S8's**, and the search results list it displaces is built to re-render as an L3 overlay under the capture field. The capture field, its focus discipline and the scan heuristic are S4's and stay where they are — **S8 adds no keyboard binding to a till surface** (§B.13.3).
- **The counter density mode is built and proven at 1280 × 720**, with the 44px hit target, the 52px primary and the 56px row, for every later till surface to render into (§B.11).
- **Six tables on S2's sync registry** with the predicates and per-location estimates above, and the seven-day retention window the till is sized against (rule 9).
- **Turnos and their variances**, which **S9** rolls into `daily_metrics` alongside `sales` on `recorded_at` — never on `occurred_at`, which is displayed to the operator and never used for accounting (rule 8).
- **`customers` has an interactive writer, and it works offline.** S4 is the only one (ledger); every other stage reads it. A customer registered during a blackout queues under the natural key `(tenant_id, document_type, document)` and arrives `applied`, `duplicate` or `merged` (rule 8, second paragraph), which is what lets **S5** name the acquirer on the canonical document for a sale made while the connection was down (§8).
- **`stale_price` and `catalog_divergence` on `sync_conflicts`**, raised as an offline sale is applied. With S2's protocol rows and S3's `negative_stock`, all three of §5's named reconciliations have a writer, and the office resolves them on the one surface **S2** built for them.

## Gated on

**§11.5 — the pilot: which network, how many sedes, how many tills per sede, what the connectivity actually is, and which browser is on those machines.** Every one of §4's four counter budgets is an acceptance criterion of this stage and none of them can be *passed* on a developer's machine. Two specifics beyond the architecture's phrasing, because they change what gets built rather than only what gets measured: **the screen size of the pilot's tills** — §B.11 fixes a 1280 × 720 floor and §B.17 item 2 leaves everything below it undesigned, so a pilot on 1024 × 768 machines needs a layout decision before this stage draws the Mostrador; and **the scanner model**, because the 30 ms heuristic threshold above is a constant chosen from typical HID behaviour and the fallback path is what makes being wrong about it cheap.

**The payment flow has never been through design.** design-system.md §B.17 item 5 names it as the largest unresolved gap in the handoff and assigns it to S4 and S5 — the method split, the change calculation, the customer-identification step the canonical sale document requires, and the receipt's on-screen form — there is no fiscal QR to draw, because Botica issues no document that carries one (§8, §12). This document specifies it to the level a build agent can construct from, and it is an extrapolation from the component layer rather than a drawing. It should have a design pass before the pilot, not after.

**S4 owns no `tenants.settings` group, because the ledger's key register assigns it none** (rule 5). Three things a pharmacy might reasonably expect to configure therefore are not configurable in v1, and each is decided rather than deferred: a `cashier` may apply **no** discount; a `cashier` may **not** override a price; and a shift closes with **any** variance, with no threshold that blocks it. If the pilot needs any of them, the register gains a `/api/settings/counter` group owned by S4, written through rule 5's `jsonb_set` helper — a change to the ledger, and not one this stage may make on its own.

**One leftover the persistence decision does not settle**, and it is copy rather than architecture: §B.9.1 fixes the `degraded` chip's lead-in as `Sincronización con problemas`, which is the wrong sentence for a till whose pushes are healthy and whose browser has merely refused persistent storage — the state is right, the words are the design system's to correct.

**Names coined here, because neither architecture.md §3 nor the ledger supplies them.** The `shift_status` enum (`open`, `closed`) — §3 gives `shifts.status` a column and the enum register gives it no values. The `payment_method` enum — the values are §3's verbatim, only the enum's name is new. `sale_lines.position`, for the ticket's line order, since replicated rows arrive unordered. The `location_id` column on `sale_lines`, `payments` and `sale_return_lines`, denormalised so S2's delta cursor index applies without a join (rule 4). The full column sets of `sale_returns` and `sale_return_lines`, which §3 describes only as behaviour. And the composition of `sales.number` as `{device code}-{per-device sequence}` — the number must be allocated locally, offline, and must never collide across two tills in one sede, and the only mechanism that gives a bare integer that property is a central allocator or a lease, neither of which exists at v1 (A6, deferred). **Keeping the internal number composed is what keeps it allocatable on a till with no connection, and what keeps it from ever being read as a number some other system issued** — the failure the ledger singles out as the most likely modelling error in the product, and the reason this number is the one both systems reconcile on (§8).
