# S6 — Purchasing

## Outcome

An administrator opens **Compras** on a Monday morning and finds an order already waiting: one per supplier per sede, generated overnight, with a quantity and a sentence per line. She reads six columns — what the product is, what is on the shelf, what it sells in a week, how many days that shelf covers, what the model proposes, and why — and the fourth column is coloured so the lines that will run out are the ones her eye lands on. She raises one quantity, cuts two to zero, and presses **Aprobar y enviar**. The order leaves for Coopidrogas and locks. Nothing she edited was overwritten, and the difference between what the model proposed and what she approved is recorded on every line she touched, which is how anyone will ever know whether the model is worth trusting.

Three days later the boxes arrive. She opens the order, types what actually came, enters the lot code and expiry that are printed on the carton, and confirms. Stock moves — through S3's ledger, never by a quantity written in place (rule 7) — the lots exist with the cost that was actually paid rather than the cost that was quoted, the order settles at `received` or `partially_received` depending on what the supplier shorted, and the number of days the supplier actually took becomes the lead time the next forecast plans against. Nobody typed a stock level anywhere.

Behind both of those is the thing the deck sells and that this stage has to make true: a model that has learned each product's rotation per sede. It learns from `sales`, and on a droguería's first day there are none — **and the screen still fills** (§1). The forecast runs in one of three regimes and every row says which one it is in: `parametric` on day one, arithmetic over the sede's own `stock_policies`; `learning` as Botica's own sales accumulate, with the parametric floor still holding under a thin signal; `learned` once an item has earned an estimate worth the name, from history imported or earned. This stage also owns the loader that brings the previous system's sales history in, as `sales` rows marked `imported` — an accelerant that moves most references into `learned` in an afternoon, never a precondition for anything on this screen (§11.2). Those rows teach the forecast and they are invisible everywhere else: they were never issued by us, they belong to no turno, they produce no fiscal document, and no cash count will ever be reconciled against them.

What this stage does **not** produce is a black box. The method is stated, the three regimes and the per-item thresholds between them are stated, what a suggestion means when there is no history at all is stated, and every line carries its basis, its confidence and a computed reason code underneath the model's prose — so when a suggestion is wrong on a Monday, the person looking at it can find out why before lunch rather than filing a ticket.

6. **The Orden sugerida screen**, as drawn: four KPIs, the six-column table with the coloured `Cobertura` and the editable `Sugerido`, four filter chips including **Confianza del modelo**, the training-provenance line, and the footer total (handoff, pantalla 4).

8. **`goods_receipts` and `goods_receipt_lines`** — receiving against an order, partial receipt, over- and under-delivery, lot code and expiry capture, and the write-back of the cost actually paid and the lead time actually observed. Confirmation calls S3's ledger service and writes `receipt` moves (ledger, `stock_moves.type`).
9. **Supplier returns**, which the ledger assigns to S6 as the cause of `supplier_return` moves. Modelled as a `goods_receipts` row at `type = supplier_return` — see *Data* and *Gated on*.
10. **Manual orders** (`source = manual`), so the enum value is not dead: a supplier, a sede and typed lines, with `suggested_quantity` **null** rather than zero.
11. **The sales-history loader, optional by decision** (§1, §11.2) — a management command writing `imports`, `sales` and `sale_lines` with `source = imported`, and the constraint that keeps those rows out of everything fiscal. Where a client can export, it lands most of a tenant in `learned` on day one; where none can, nothing here is blocked and the ramp is slower.
12. **Model-sourced stock policies**: `stock_policies` rows at `source = model` carrying the forecast's `reorder_point` and `target_coverage_days`, written only where no pharmacist's manual row stands (ledger, disputed columns) **and never from a `parametric` forecast**, which would be the pharmacist's own number written back as if the model had learned it.
13. **The `purchasing` settings group** — `GET` and `PATCH /api/settings/purchasing` (rule 5, the settings register).
14. **The indexes this stage's read paths need** (rule 4), each named against the query it serves.
15. **This stage's demo-seed fixtures**, registered with S1's command (§1, ledger cross-stage services) — a suggested order that renders pantalla 4 as drawn, with lines in all three regimes so the confidence chip has something to filter. See *Demo seed*.

- **Transfers between sedes** — S3. `Ibuprofeno … hay 96 en Suba` is a *reason*, not an action: the Orden sugerida screen never dispatches a transfer, it says the stock exists elsewhere and the administrator opens Traslados.

- **`fiscal_documents`** — S5. A purchase order is not a fiscal document and a goods receipt is not one either. `dian_resolutions` and numbering leases are **not built at v1** (A6, deferred): Botica hands the sale to the client's invoicing system and allocates no fiscal number (§8), so neither table exists for this stage to be warned off.
- **`daily_metrics` and the Panel** — S9. S6 hands it `demand_forecasts`, the suggestion-versus-approval deviation and the receipts; S9 decides what is rolled up.
- **The model gateway client** — S8, which owns it as a cross-stage service (ledger, cross-stage services). It is the one OpenRouter path, with the per-tenant spend cap, the kill switch and the cost log; S6 calls it for the reason text and never opens a second path to the vendor. Whichever of the two stages lands first builds it and the owner is S8 either way, which is why the cap and the kill switch live in S8's `assistant` settings group rather than in `purchasing`, where `reason_text_enabled` gates this stage's call and nothing else. A second client would be a second spend cap, which is no cap at all.
- **Supplier invoice matching, accounts payable, three-way match, payment terms enforcement** — not in v1 (§12: no accounting). `suppliers.payment_terms` is displayed and nothing computes against it.
- **EDI, a supplier portal, or any supplier API.** Dispatch is email with an attached order (§10's transactional email). If a supplier needs a stable URL for the order, that is object storage and a decision nobody has taken.
- **A distributor module** — A10. `locations.type` already admits `distribution_center` and a purchase order is already a document between a location and a supplier; nothing here ships a distributor feature.
- **Substitution and equivalence modelling.** No table maps an item to its therapeutic substitute, so `Sustituto del ibuprofeno en quiebre` is prose the model writes from the order's own context, never a computed relation. Stated so nobody looks for the table.
- **Forecast history.** `demand_forecasts` holds one current row per item per sede. What the model said on a given day survives on `purchase_order_lines`, stamped at generation.

**`items.custom` is read and not written.** The ledger permits S6 to write a derived band under its own key; this stage declines. A rotation class persisted into JSONB is a second copy of what `demand_forecasts` already says, and it goes stale the moment the next refresh disagrees with it. If a later build needs a persisted band it lands under a single `purchasing` key and this paragraph is the diff. **What breaks if this is wrong:** nothing at v1 — a consumer wanting the band computes it from `weekly_sales` and `trend`, which is one expression.

**Imported sales are quarantined by construction, not by discipline.** This is the sentence in this document most likely to be violated later and hardest to find once it has been:

> **Every rollup, KPI, cash, shift and fiscal query filters on `sales.source`, and the default is `'counter'`. A query over `sales` that does not name `source` is a defect.**

An imported sale was issued by another system fifteen years ago. It has no shift, no device, no payment, no `stock_moves` row and no fiscal document, and the check constraint above enforces the first two. S5 selects `source = 'counter'` when it builds documents; S9 selects `source = 'counter'` when it rolls up `daily_metrics`; a cash reconciliation selects a shift, and an imported sale belongs to none. The two consumers that read `imported` are S6's forecast and S7's elasticity estimation, and both name it explicitly. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a Panel reading `$412,8 M` that silently contains two years of a previous system's turnover is a number an owner will make a decision on, and it will not reconcile against anything — not a bank statement, not the client's own invoicing system, not last month's own figure once the import is a month old.

**The loader's identity columns.** Rule 8 requires `client_uuid` on `sales` and `sale_lines`. Imported rows carry a **deterministic** uuid derived from the `imports` row and the legacy document's own identifier, not a v7. The unique index is the same either way and the gain is that re-running the same export file is a no-op instead of a duplicated history. **What breaks if this is wrong:** nothing sorts on `client_uuid` today; if something later assumes v7's time ordering, imported rows sort arbitrarily among themselves. `occurred_at` is the legacy sale's own timestamp; `recorded_at` is the moment of import.

**`sales.number` on an imported row is the legacy document's own number, prefixed so it can never collide with a number S4's per-location allocator will issue.** Where the export carries no number the loader synthesises one under the same prefix. **What breaks if this is wrong:** `UNIQUE (tenant_id, location_id, number)` fails on the first counter sale that reaches a number the import already took, and it fails at a counter with a customer waiting.

### What `demand_forecasts` holds — the v1 model, stated so it can be debugged

**Three regimes, decided per item and per sede, never per tenant** (§1). A fast-moving analgesic at Chapinero earns a real forecast weeks before a slow dermocosmetic at Usme does, so the regime is decided for each `(item, location)` pair on that pair's own signal and is recomputed on every refresh. `demand_forecasts.basis` carries the current regime; `purchase_order_lines.basis` carries the copy the line was generated under.

| `basis` | An item is in it when | `suggested_quantity` comes from | `confidence` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `parametric` | no usable week of sales for that item at that sede, own or imported | the sede's own `stock_policies` — stated in full below | fixed: **0,20** where a `manual` policy row stands, **0,10** where only a category assumption does. Always **Baja**, and never more: a parameter a pharmacist typed is a deliberate instruction, not evidence |
| `learning` | at least one usable week, but the estimate has not earned promotion | the exponentially weighted mean below, **floored at the parametric quantity** so a fortnight of quiet weeks cannot talk the model out of restocking | the formula below, **capped at 0,65** — a `learning` item never renders **Alta** |
| `learned` | `learned_min_weeks` usable weeks (default **4**) **and** the relative standard error of `weekly_sales` at or below `learned_max_rse` (default **0,35**) | the full method below, seasonal multiplier included where it exists | the three-input formula below, over its whole range |

**Promotion is a measurement, not a calendar.** The relative standard error is `σ_weekly / (√n × weekly_sales)`, so an analgesic selling forty units a week with ordinary variance clears 0,35 in four or five weeks, while a dermocosmetic selling one unit a fortnight may never clear it and stays in `learning` indefinitely — which is the correct answer for that item rather than a failure of the model. A tenant-wide "eight weeks of history" switch would promote both on the same morning and be wrong about one of them. **Demotion carries hysteresis**: an item falls back to `learning` only above `learned_demote_rse` (default **0,45**), and every regime change is recorded in `model_version`. **What breaks if this is wrong:** the `Por qué` column flickers between a learned claim and a parametric one from Monday to Tuesday, and an administrator learns to distrust both.

**Method — `learning` and `learned` only.** Per `(item, location)`, over the trailing **26 weeks** of `sale_lines` joined to `sales` at `status = 'closed'`, both sources included, **bucketed by `occurred_at`**.

- **`weekly_sales`** — an exponentially weighted mean of weekly units with a **four-week half-life**, computed after winsorising each week at the 95th percentile of that item's own weekly history, so one wholesale-sized ticket does not become the baseline.
- **Stockout weeks are censored, not counted as zero.** A week in which the item stood at zero on-hand for more than two days is dropped from the mean. Selling nothing while having nothing is not demand information, and treating it as a zero is the classic way a forecasting system talks itself out of ever restocking the thing that keeps running out.
- **`trend`** — the last four weeks' mean over the prior eight weeks' mean, minus one, clipped to ±1. It drives reason codes and nothing else; it does not scale the quantity.
- **`safety_stock`** — `z × σ_weekly × √(lead_time_days / 7)`, with `z` from the service level in the `purchasing` settings group (default 95%, `z = 1,65`) and `lead_time_days` from `suppliers`, falling back to the group's default.
- **`reorder_point`** — `weekly_sales × lead_time_days / 7 + safety_stock`.
- **`coverage_days`** — on-hand ÷ (`weekly_sales` / 7). Where `weekly_sales` is zero it is **not a number**: it renders as `—` with `sin ventas en el periodo` in the secondary slot (§B.9.2 tier 3). Where the row is `parametric` there is no `weekly_sales` at all and the cell reads `—` with `sin histórico`. Never a zero, never an infinity.
- **`suggested_quantity`** — `max(0, target − on_hand − on_order)`, where `target = weekly_sales × (target_coverage_days + lead_time_days) / 7 + safety_stock`; rounded up to `supplier_items.min_order_pack`; capped by the group's per-line and per-order caps. `on_order` counts lines on orders at `approved` or `sent` for the same item and sede, so two consecutive mornings do not order the same shortfall twice. In `learning` the result is the **greater** of this quantity and the parametric one, and a line raised by that floor carries `reason_code = learning_floor`.

**What a parametric suggestion actually is.** With no usable week of sales, the suggestion is not a forecast and does not pretend to be one: it is the sede's own replenishment policy, arithmetic over `stock_policies` and current stock, which a pharmacist set deliberately and which beats a guess. Two paths and a refusal, in that order:

1. **A `manual` `stock_policies` row stands at that sede** — `suggested_quantity = max(0, max_quantity − on_hand − on_order)` where the row carries a `max_quantity`, and `max(0, reorder_point + min_order_pack − on_hand − on_order)` where it carries only a reorder point. `reason_code = parametric_policy`, `confidence = 0,20`.
2. **No manual row, but the item's category has a tenant-wide median `weekly_sales`** from at least `category_default_min_items` items already in `learning` or `learned` (default **5**) — that median carries the item at the group's `target_coverage_days`, **stated as an assumption about the category and never as a finding about the item**. `reason_code = parametric_category_default`, `confidence = 0,10`.
3. **Neither** — no policy row, no category to assume from. **The item is not proposed at all and does not appear on the order.** A model with no basis withholds rather than guesses (§1), and a screen full of invented quantities is worse than a screen that says what it is missing.

Parametric quantities are still rounded up to `supplier_items.min_order_pack` and still capped by the group's per-line and per-order caps — the caps are arithmetic and apply in every regime.

**A parametric forecast writes nothing back into `stock_policies`.** The `source = model` row is written only from a `learning` or `learned` forecast. A parametric row's reorder point *is* the pharmacist's reorder point, and writing it back under `source = model` would launder a human parameter into a model output and then read it the next morning as though the model had learned it. **What breaks if this is wrong:** Existencias shows a `Punto de reorden` attributed to a model that has seen no sales, and `source` — the one column that tells a regente which numbers are theirs — stops meaning anything.

**The `Por qué` string in the parametric regime is honest by construction**, and the wording is the load-bearing part. It reads `Sin histórico · sugerido por el punto de reorden de la sede` on path 1 and `Sin histórico · cobertura por defecto de la categoría` on path 2, and it is never written by the model, which sees `learned` lines only. The handoff's `Pico de temporada + quiebre reciente` is a **learned** claim — a seasonal multiplier needs fifty-two weeks that do not exist yet — and it is unreachable in this regime, as is anything naming a trend or a rotation. **What breaks if this is wrong:** the first screen a prospect ever sees claims a pollen season inferred from nothing, a pharmacist who knows the category notices inside a minute, and the demo is over.

**Confidence is a stored number and a rendered band.** `confidence` is 0–1, lives on `demand_forecasts` and is stamped onto every `purchase_order_lines` row at generation. In `learned` it is driven by three things: weeks of usable history after censoring, the coefficient of variation of weekly demand, and the share of the window that is imported rather than Botica's own. In `learning` the same formula is capped at 0,65. In `parametric` it is the fixed pair above. It renders as **Alta** (≥ 0,70), **Media** (0,40–0,69) and **Baja** (< 0,40). An item whose window is majority-imported drops one band, because imported weeks cannot be censored for stockouts — there are no `stock_moves` behind them — and an uncensored stockout week is a mean that is quietly too low.

**Basis and band are two readings, and both are shown.** The basis says what the model computed from; the band says how much it knows. A **Baja** on a `learned` item is thin, noisy history and may improve next week; a **Baja** on a `parametric` one is no history at all and will not improve until the sede sells something. Collapsing them into one number would tell an administrator the two lines need the same review, and they do not.

**Seasonality: v1 fits no seasonal model, and says so.** What it does instead is a **year-ago category multiplier** — the same ISO week last year, for the item's category, across the whole tenant, expressed as a ratio to that category's annual mean — applied to `target` only when at least 52 weeks of history exist and the category has enough observations in that week to be worth reading. This is the mechanism behind both of the handoff's seasonal strings: `Sube con la temporada de polen` is the antihistamine category's multiplier crossing 1,25 on its own, and `Pico de temporada + quiebre reciente` is the same multiplier together with a stockout inside the trailing four weeks. **On a tenant with less than a year of history the multiplier does not exist, and the reason text must not claim a season.** It applies to `learned` rows only, and never to a `parametric` or `learning` one whatever the category's own history says. A model that invents a pollen season out of eleven weeks of data is worse than one that says `Rotación estable`.

**Reason codes, and the split between arithmetic and prose.** Every line gets exactly one `reason_code`, computed and always present. The model call turns the whole order's codes into one Spanish clause per line, which is what lets it write a sentence the arithmetic cannot — `Sustituto del ibuprofeno en quiebre` requires seeing another line in the same order. When there is no prose, the code's fixed string renders. **Only `learned` lines are sent to the model at all**; a `parametric` or `learning` line always renders its fixed string, because on those lines the exact wording *is* the honest part and is not a thing to be improved.

| `reason_code` | Fires when | Basis | Fallback string |
|---|---|---|---|
| `seasonal_peak_recent_stockout` | Category multiplier ≥ 1,25 **and** a stockout in the last four weeks | `learned` | `Pico de temporada + quiebre reciente` |
| `stable_rotation` | \|`trend`\| < 0,10 and confidence ≥ 0,70 | `learned` | `Rotación estable, mantiene 45 días` |
| `stockout_available_elsewhere` | On-hand = 0 and another sede holds at least the shortfall | any | `En quiebre, hay 96 en Suba` |
| `seasonal_peak` | Category multiplier ≥ 1,25 alone | `learned` | `Sube con la temporada de polen` |
| `predictable_chronic` | Coefficient of variation < 0,25 over 26 weeks | `learned` | `Crónico, demanda predecible` |
| `sufficient_coverage` | `coverage_days` ≥ target + lead time; `suggested_quantity` = 0 | `learning` · `learned` | `Cobertura suficiente, no pedir` |
| `lot_expiring` | The on-hand is concentrated in a lot inside S3's expiry alert horizon | any | `Lote actual vence en 6 meses` |
| `overstock` | `coverage_days` > 90; `suggested_quantity` = 0 | `learning` · `learned` | `Sobrestock, liberar capital` |
| `cross_sell_pair` | The item co-occurs with another line above the cross-sell threshold | `learning` · `learned` | `Se vende junto con suero oral` |
| `learning_floor` | The own-sales quantity fell below the parametric floor and the floor was applied | `learning` | `Con pocas semanas de venta · sostenido por el punto de reorden` |
| `parametric_policy` | Parametric path 1 — a `manual` `stock_policies` row stands at that sede | `parametric` | `Sin histórico · sugerido por el punto de reorden de la sede` |
| `parametric_category_default` | Parametric path 2 — no manual row, the category has a median to assume from | `parametric` | `Sin histórico · cobertura por defecto de la categoría` |

**Two codes are facts and not findings, which is why they fire in every regime.** `stockout_available_elsewhere` reads `stock_on_hand` and `lot_expiring` reads a lot's expiry date; both are observable on a tenant's first morning, because S3's ledger exists three stages before any forecast does. Everything else on this list is a claim about demand and is admissible only where demand has been measured. **`no_history` is gone**: under §1 an item with no basis is not on the order at all, so there is no line to carry the code, and the two `parametric_*` codes describe a suggestion that exists rather than one that could not be made.

The `stockout_available_elsewhere` figure is another sede's stock, read from S3's `GET /api/stock/availability` in the same request that builds the order's rows, and it carries **no staleness marker**: §B.9.2's marker marks a figure a till read from its local store at the last sync, and every surface in this stage is server-authoritative and online-only (*Offline*). The marked version of this same string is S3's, on a till.

**Writing `model` into `stock_policies` without erasing a pharmacist.** After each refresh, for every item and sede with a **`learning` or `learned`** forecast, S6 upserts a `stock_policies` row at `source = model` carrying `reorder_point` and `target_coverage_days`. **A row whose `source` is `manual` is left exactly as it stands** — not merged, not partially updated, not overwritten in one column and kept in another. The forecast's own numbers still live in `demand_forecasts` and the suggested order still uses them; what the manual row keeps is the `Punto de reorden` state S3's Existencias screen renders, which is a threshold a regente set on purpose (ledger, disputed columns). **What breaks if this is wrong:** the first refresh after go-live silently replaces every threshold the pharmacy configured during onboarding, nobody notices for a week, and the trust the pilot depends on is spent recovering thresholds nobody wrote down.

**The sales-history loader is a management command, not an endpoint** (rule 6). It takes the tenant as an explicit required argument and pins before doing any work; it takes a location mapping, a file, and a dry-run flag that reports what it would write and writes nothing. It is not exposed over HTTP in v1 because it is run once per tenant, by us, during onboarding, against a file whose shape is different for every legacy system (§11.2).

**`purchasing` settings keys.** The ledger names four — default lead times, coverage targets, order caps, model refresh cadence. The group carries: `default_lead_time_days`, `target_coverage_days`, `order_cap_value`, `order_cap_weeks_per_line`, `refresh_hour`. Eight more are **coined here** and listed in *Gated on*: `service_level`, `learned_min_weeks`, `learned_max_rse`, `learned_demote_rse`, `category_default_min_items`, `seasonal_multiplier_enabled`, `reason_text_enabled`, `write_model_stock_policies`. The four regime keys replace a single `minimum_history_weeks`, which assumed one threshold for a whole tenant and would have promoted an analgesic and a dermocosmetic on the same morning. The last is a safety valve: a tenant that wants the forecast but not the automatic thresholds turns it off, and the manual-row rule above stops needing to be the only protection.

## Jobs

Four, all on Procrastinate's Postgres queue, all pinning the tenant from the job payload before touching anything (rule 6). Times are tenant-local and chosen so the handoff's own provenance line — `actualizado hoy 06:00` — is true when an administrator opens the screen.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `forecast.refresh` | Cron, 04:00 daily, one job per tenant fanned to one job per location; also on demand | `(tenant_id, location_id, run_date, model_version)` | The previous run's rows stand. The screen shows the older `computed_at` and the provenance line says so — it never shows a fresh timestamp over stale numbers. Three consecutive failures raise a region-scope error on the Compras route naming the sede |
| `purchase_order.generate` | Cron, 05:00, after the refresh; also `POST /api/purchase-orders/generate` | `(tenant_id, location_id, supplier_id, run_date)` | A second run on the same day **updates the existing `suggested` order in place and never creates a second one**, and it never touches an order past `suggested` — an administrator's edits from an hour ago are not overwritten by the 05:00 job's own retry. On failure no order exists for that supplier and the nav counter is simply lower; the empty state says the model has not produced one |
| `purchase_order.reason_text` | On generation completing, per order | `(tenant_id, purchase_order_id, model_version)` | Retried with backoff. After the last attempt `reason` stays null on every line and the screen renders each line's `reason_code` string; the filter bar's right slot appends `· sin redacción del modelo`. **The order is fully usable** — quantities are arithmetic and never wait on a model (§10). A retry replaces prose and never a number |
| `supplier.lead_time_refresh` | On each `goods_receipts` confirmation | `(tenant_id, goods_receipt_id)` | Computes `received_at − sent_at` for the order and rewrites `suppliers.lead_time_days` as the rolling median of the last ten observations for that supplier. A **median**, because one 40-day back-order would otherwise move the reorder point of every item that supplier carries. On failure the lead time keeps its previous value and the next receipt recomputes it |

**The refresh writes a row for every stocked item at every sede, in every regime.** There is no such thing as an item the forecast skips because it has no history: a `parametric` row is written with `basis = parametric`, `confidence` fixed, and `weekly_sales`, `trend` and `coverage_days` **null**. Two consumers read those rows and both must handle the nulls: S3's Existencias derives its `Sobrestock` state from `coverage_days` and simply does not fire it where there is none, and S9's tiles must not average a parametric row into anything describing forecast accuracy. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a null coverage read as a zero puts every reference at a new sede into `Sobrestock` on its first morning, which is the exact inversion of the truth.

**The gateway call is bounded by this stage's own switch.** `reason_text_enabled` in the `purchasing` group gates the call. S8's `assistant` group holds the shared gateway kill switch and per-tenant spend cap; when both exist, either being off suppresses the call. S6 does not read S8's group as a precondition, because S6 and S8 run in parallel off S4 (§13) and a tenant that never enabled the assistant would otherwise get no reason text at all.

### Compras · Orden sugerida — the drawn screen

Breadcrumb `Compras`, title `Orden sugerida 248` at `t-28` (§A.13.2). Header actions: secondary `Descartar`, primary `Aprobar y enviar` (§B.6.1 `sm`, §B.6.2 one primary per surface). Content inset `28px 40px` — a route whose `main` is a single working panel (§B.3). Filter bar (§A.13.3, §B.8.5): chips `Proveedor · Coopidrogas`, `Sede · Chapinero`, inactive `Categoría`, inactive `Confianza del modelo`; right slot `Modelo entrenado con 18 meses de venta · actualizado hoy 06:00` at 11px `#727272`, which is this surface's provenance line rather than a sync line, because nothing here reads the local store (§B.8.5).

**The provenance line states the regime the order was mostly generated in, and is not a fixed string** (§1). Three forms, chosen by the basis of the majority of the order's lines: `Modelo entrenado con 18 meses de venta · actualizado hoy 06:00` where that majority is `learned`; `Aprendiendo de 7 semanas de venta propia · actualizado hoy 06:00` where it is `learning`; `Sin histórico cargado · sugerido por parámetros de la sede · actualizado hoy 06:00` where it is `parametric`. The `· sin redacción del modelo` suffix appends to any of the three. **What breaks if this is wrong:** the screen tells a prospect it was trained on eighteen months of their sales on a tenant that has none of them, which is the cheapest possible way to lose a pilot and the hardest to recover from.

**Four KPI tiles** (§A.19.1), `repeat(4,1fr)`, `gap:16px`, strings verbatim:

1. `Referencias sugeridas` · `42` · `de 1.184 activas en la sede` — lines with `suggested_quantity` > 0 over active `tracks_stock` items at that sede.
2. `Valor de la orden` · `$9,4 M` · `cubre 34 días de venta proyectada` — Σ `approved_quantity × unit_cost`, abbreviated because it is a display figure (§A.11); the days figure is the order's value over the sede's projected daily cost of goods.
3. `Quiebres que evita` · `11` · `referencias que se agotan en 7 días` — lines with `coverage_days` ≤ 7 and `approved_quantity` > 0.
4. `Recortes vs. pedido manual` · `−$2,1 M` · `en referencias de rotación lenta` — U+2212, never a hyphen (§A.11). **The counterfactual is defined here so the tile is defensible:** the "pedido manual" is the order a flat-coverage rule produces — the group's `target_coverage_days` applied to every active reference at that sede with no forecast and no censoring — and the tile is this order's value minus that one's, reported only when negative. A KPI whose comparison is undefined is a number nobody can argue with and nobody should believe.

**What the tiles do when there is no projection to divide by.** Three of the four rest on a demand estimate, and in the parametric regime there is none. Each degrades to §B.9.2 tier 3 — the em dash with its reason in the secondary slot — and none of them to a zero. Tile 1 is unaffected: it counts lines, which exist. Tile 2 keeps `Valor de la orden`, which is arithmetic over cost, and its secondary reads `sin proyección de venta` instead of the days figure. Tile 3 renders `—` over `sin proyección de venta`, because `coverage_days` is null on every line. Tile 4 renders `—` over `sin comparación posible`, because the flat-coverage counterfactual and the order in front of you are the same rule — and a difference of zero would read as *the model saved you nothing* rather than *there is nothing here to compare*. **What breaks if this is wrong:** three tiles reading `0` on the first screen of every demo, which is precisely the empty state nobody designed and the tile whose denominator is zero (§1).

**The table** (§A.17, drawn widths): `Producto 26 · Stock 11 (der.) · Venta / sem 14 (der.) · Cobertura 13 (der.) · Sugerido 13 (der.) · Por qué 23`. Rows 48px, `Standard` density (§B.4.1). First column `#171717`, the rest `#555555`, `Por qué` steps to 12px `#727272` (§A.17).

- **`Cobertura` is coloured by urgency** on §B.7.4's thresholds — ≤ 4 días `#b04a3f` · 5–20 `#8c6a33` · 21–90 `#555555` · > 90 `#4c6a86` — as a numeral with no dot, and the `Por qué` cell always restates the reading in words so the colour is never the only signal (§B.12.3).
- **`Sugerido` is the editable cell** (§A.18.2) rendered as §B.5.6's stepper: 34px box, right-aligned, tabular, `−`/`+` ghost glyphs on hover or focus. At zero it recedes to `background:#fbfbfb`, `color:#909090` — drawn on `Cobertura suficiente, no pedir` and `Sobrestock, liberar capital`. Commit on `Enter` or blur, revert on `Esc`, optimistic with no loading state (§B.10.1).
- **`Por qué` carries the basis in words and the band as a prefix.** The reason string names the basis itself wherever it is not `learned` (`Sin histórico · …`, `Con pocas semanas de venta · …`), so nothing about the regime is encoded in a colour or a glyph (§B.12.3). The confidence band is prefixed as §B.7.3's incidental treatment — dot and label at `t-12` `#555555`, no fill, no pill, since this table spends no badge column — **and only where the band is not `Alta`**: `Baja · Sin histórico · sugerido por el punto de reorden de la sede`. Marking the rows that are fine is how a marker stops meaning anything (§B.9.2). The band, the basis and the numbers behind them are always in the record panel.
- **An edit writes `approved_quantity` and never `suggested_quantity`.** This is the single most important line in this document. Two columns, never one; the difference is the only honest measure of whether the model is trusted, and overwriting the proposal destroys that measurement permanently and irrecoverably (§3, ledger). The edit is legible on the screen where it is made: the row's `Por qué` cell gains ` · ajustado de 220` at 12px `#727272` (§B.5.6), the footer total recomputes, and the KPI tiles recompute. **What breaks if this is wrong:** six months into the pilot nobody can answer "does the buyer follow the model", the deck's central claim becomes unmeasurable, and there is no way to reconstruct it because the proposals were overwritten one keystroke at a time.
- Row click opens the line's record panel (§B.8.5, 440px, pushes rather than overlays): the item, its laboratorio and category, its lots and expiries at that sede, `Al generar la orden` showing the stamped `basis`, `coverage_days`, `confidence` and `model_version` — and under them what the confidence was computed from: usable weeks after censoring, the coefficient of variation, the imported share of the window, and in the parametric regime the `stock_policies` row the quantity came from and whether it is the pharmacist's or a category assumption — the supplier's `supplier_code`, `min_order_pack` and cost, and the full reason with its code.
- **Footer** (§A.17): left `Mostrando 11 de 42 referencias sugeridas`; right the label `Total de la orden` at 11px `#727272` and `$9.412.600` at 20px/−0.025em on a shared baseline — a table cell figure, so never abbreviated (§A.11).

**Confianza del modelo is the chip that makes a day-one order reviewable**, and it filters on two readings at once, because on a cold-start tenant they are not the same population. Its menu is multi-select, like `Sede` (§A.13.3), in two labelled groups with a count beside every value: **Base** — `Paramétrica`, `Aprendiendo`, `Aprendida` — and **Confianza** — `Alta`, `Media`, `Baja`. Selecting across the groups intersects; selecting inside one unions. The active chip's value pill (§A.15.1) reads the selection or its count: `Confianza del modelo · Paramétrica`, `Confianza del modelo · 2`. The menu's first entry is the shortcut the affordance exists for — **`Revisar primero`**, which selects `Paramétrica` and `Baja` together and leaves the administrator looking at exactly the lines the model knows least about.

**Per line, the chip's two readings are visible without a seventh column**: the basis is in the `Por qué` prose and the band is its prefix where it is not `Alta`, as above. **Sorting matches the chip**: the `Por qué` header sorts on `(confidence asc, basis)`, so the sort key is the one that column already displays and the thin lines come to the top of a long order. The full band renders in the record panel as a dot and a label rather than a badge, because §B.7.3 allows one badge column per table and this table spends none.

**Approve.** Confirmation modal at 560px (§B.8.5): title `Aprobar y enviar la orden`, body naming supplier, sede, line count and total, primary button `Aprobar y enviar` — the consequence is in the button, not `Aceptar`. - `Recibido` is the §B.5.6 stepper, defaulted to `approved_quantity`, because the common case is the whole order arriving.
- `Lote` and `Vence` are required on every line whose item has `tracks_lots` / `tracks_expiry` (§3) and are a text field and a `MM/AAAA` field (§A.11). A line missing either cannot be confirmed and shows the §B.5.7 field error in place of its help text.
- **Over-delivery is allowed and flagged**, never refused: the row's `Estado` reads `Recibido de más · 20` on the informative family. The supplier sent them, they are on the shelf, and a receiving screen that refuses reality is a screen that gets bypassed with a manual adjustment.
- **Under-delivery settles the order at `partially_received`** and leaves the shortfall on the line as `approved_quantity − received_quantity`; a second receipt against the same order is normal and lands the order at `received` when every line is met or the buyer closes it short.
- `Confirmar recepción` is the primary and is the atomic act: lots created or attached, one call per line into S3's ledger service writing `receipt` moves (rule 7), `lots.unit_cost` and `supplier_items.cost` written from what was paid, `suppliers.lead_time_days` recomputed, the order's status settled. Confirmation is idempotent on the receipt id.
- A **supplier return** is the same surface at `type = supplier_return`: quantities are what goes back, the ledger service writes `supplier_return` moves, and the header reads `Devolución al proveedor`. It needs no order.
- *States* — loading is the same skeleton discipline; empty is unreachable (a receipt is opened against lines); error is region scope naming the operation, the sede and the recovery, and a confirmation that fails moves no stock at all rather than some of it.

### Ajustes · Compras

A section of the settings dialog, not a route (§B.8.4·4): reached at `/purchasing?settings=purchasing`, so the section is a link and `Escape` returns where you were. Fields per the key list above, with `Obligatorio` in the help slot rather than an asterisk, validation on blur and on submit (§B.5.7). `write_model_stock_policies` carries a `t-12` `#727272` line stating exactly what it does: `Cuando está activo, el modelo escribe el punto de reorden. Nunca reemplaza un umbral definido a mano.`

## Offline

**Every surface in this stage is online-only, and it says so plainly.** Compras is the office read model: server-authoritative over the network, fetched per view, never synced to a browser (§4, A4). Nothing in this stage is in S2's sync registry (rule 9), no purchasing table reaches a device, and a `cashier` — the only role that works offline — has no Compras nav item at all (§B.8.3).

With no network, every route in this stage renders a **route-scope state** in the content region using §B.10.3's geometry: title `Sin conexión`, body `Compras necesita conexión para leer el inventario de la red. El mostrador sigue vendiendo con normalidad.`, action `[Reintentar]`. The shell, the sidebar and the header paint normally — the chrome is cached by the application shell's service worker, which **never caches an API response** (§9), so there is no risk of a stale order rendering as a live one. There is no partial mode, no read-only cache of the last order, and no queued approval: an approval sent into a void that lands twenty minutes later against a supplier who has already shipped is worse than an approval that did not happen.

**One operational consequence, stated because it will happen at the pilot.** A delivery that arrives during a blackout cannot be received until the connection returns, so the stock is on the shelf before it is in the system. A cashier who sells one of those units sells it anyway — selling never blocks on stock (§5 rule 2) — the lot goes negative, and S3 raises a `sync_conflicts` row that the counting screen resolves once the receipt is confirmed. That is the offline contract working as designed, not a defect, and the receiving screen must not try to be clever about it: it records what arrived, the ledger sums the events, and the negative resolves itself.

## Demo seed

**S1 owns the command; this stage registers its own fixtures with it**, and S6 is not finished until Compras renders convincingly from a tenant built by that one command and nothing else (§1, ledger cross-stage services). What S6 contributes, on top of S4's synthetic sales:

- **Five sedes in `learned`, one deliberately not.** Chapinero, Suba, Kennedy, Engativá and Usme carry the seed's full sales history and their references promote normally. **Fontibón opened three weeks before the seed's *today*** — it has stock, `stock_policies` rows and three weeks of its own sales, so its order is a mixture of `learning` and `parametric` lines. This one fixture is what makes **Confianza del modelo** worth clicking, and it is a story a prospect recognises rather than a flag somebody set by hand.
- **One `suggested` order per supplier per sede**, Coopidrogas at Chapinero being the drawn one: 42 lines with `suggested_quantity` > 0 out of 1.184 active references, a total near `$9,4 M`, eleven lines at `coverage_days` ≤ 7 so the coloured `Cobertura` reads as drawn, and at least two lines at zero so the recessed stepper state is visible without editing anything.
- **Lines in all three regimes inside that one order** — enough `parametric` and `learning` lines that setting the chip to `Paramétrica`, to `Aprendiendo` or to `Baja` returns rows rather than the filtered-empty state, and enough `learned` lines that clearing the chip looks like pantalla 4.
- **`demand_forecasts` and the orders are not written by hand.** The seed loads sales, stock and policies and then runs `forecast.refresh` and `purchase_order.generate` exactly as the cron does; it tunes its *sales* until the generated order lands near the drawn figures, and it never writes a forecast row or an order line directly. A regime that appears only because a fixture set `basis` is a regime nobody has tested, and the drawn numbers are worth less than the guarantee that the screen shows what the code produces.
- **No gateway call.** `reason` prose for the seed's `learned` lines comes from a fixed table carrying the handoff's own strings, so pantalla 4 reads as drawn without a paid model call on every seed run; `parametric` and `learning` lines carry the deterministic strings they would carry in production anyway.
- **One order at each downstream status** so Órdenes de compra shows all six badges, and **one `sent` order with a partial receipt** so Recepción, `Recibido de más`, `partially_received`, an observed `suppliers.lead_time_days` and a `supplier_items.cost` written from a receipt are all reachable without typing anything.
- **No imported sales in the seed.** The seeded tenant's history is Botica's own, because a demo that only works after an import demonstrates exactly the precondition §1 says must not exist. The loader is exercised by its own fixture file in the test suite instead.
- Every row the seed writes is **self-evidently synthetic** — the tenant, its NIT, its suppliers and its document numbers are the handoff's fiction — and no path in this stage loads it into a tenant that has ever recorded a real sale (§1).

## Hands off

- **`demand_forecasts`**, one current row per item per sede, with `weekly_sales`, `trend`, `coverage_days`, `reorder_point`, `safety_stock`, `computed_at`, `model_version`, **`basis` and `confidence`**. S3's Existencias screen reads `coverage_days` for its `Sobrestock` state; S9 reads the whole row for its tiles (ledger). **Two obligations travel with the row**: `weekly_sales`, `trend` and `coverage_days` are null on a `parametric` row and a null is not a zero, and no consumer may fold a `parametric` row into a figure about how well the model is forecasting — it is not forecasting, and it says so in `basis`.
- **`stock_policies` rows at `source = model`**, alongside whatever manual rows S3 wrote, with the guarantee that no manual row was overwritten.
- **`purchase_orders` and `purchase_order_lines`** carrying `suggested_quantity` and `approved_quantity` as two columns on every model-sourced line — the raw material for the only honest "is the model trusted" measurement the product will ever have. S9 builds the report; the data exists because of this stage.
- **`goods_receipts` and `goods_receipt_lines`**, with the cost actually paid per lot — which is what makes S9's margin figures and S4's stamped `sale_lines.unit_cost` mean anything.
- **`suppliers.lead_time_days` and `supplier_items.cost`**, observed rather than quoted.
- **`sales` and `sale_lines` at `source = imported`** — history for S7's elasticity estimation and S8's cross-sell mining, under one binding rule S9 inherits: **`daily_metrics` rolls up `source = 'counter'` only.** A report that deliberately spans the legacy history names `imported` explicitly and labels itself as spanning two systems. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a period comparison silently spanning two systems with two definitions of a sale, on the screen an owner opens on Monday.
- **The `purchasing` settings group**, populated, behind its `GET`/`PATCH` pair on the pinned tenant with no tenant id in the path (rule 5, the settings register).
- **The reason-code vocabulary**, which is a stable English enum on `purchase_order_lines.reason_code` that S9 can group by without parsing Spanish prose, and **`forecast_basis`** beside it — a second stable enum, on both `demand_forecasts` and `purchase_order_lines`, which is how any later surface says which regime a number came from without inferring it from a null.
- **The `sale_lines (tenant_id, item_id, sale_id)` index**, created here as the item-grain superset and inherited by S7, which lists it under *Inherits* and migrates nothing (ledger rule 4).

## Gated on

**Nothing outside this stage blocks it any more** (§1). What was its one blocker is now a quality and timing variable, and it is restated here in that register rather than deleted, because the thing it changes — how fast the forecast becomes good — is still worth chasing at every client.

**§11.2 — what the legacy system can export, and whether it includes sales history. Not a blocker** (§1). The forecast runs `parametric` on day one from the sede's own `stock_policies`, every screen in this stage fills, the demo seed renders every one of them, and a pilot opens Compras on its first morning without a single imported row. What an export changes is **how fast an item reaches `learned`**: with eighteen months loaded, most references are `learned` on day one and the seasonal multiplier exists immediately; with nothing loaded, fast movers promote in four or five weeks, slow ones in months or never, and no multiplier exists for a year. That is a quality and timing variable — free accuracy, worth chasing early, never worth blocking a pilot for. The three things that help, in order of how much their absence costs: **sales at line grain with a timestamp and a location** (nothing else moves a reference to `learned` on day one); **a supplier-to-item mapping**, which is closer to a real blocker than the sales history is, because an order grouped under the wrong supplier cannot be sent and the `Proveedor` chip becomes decoration; **historical stock levels** (without them imported weeks cannot be censored for stockouts, which is why an imported-majority window drops a confidence band). §11.6 still matters wherever an import happens — the loader maps legacy location codes to `locations` and legacy item codes to `items`, and an unmapped code is a row the loader must refuse rather than guess at — and where no import happens it does not gate this stage at all.

**Found here, and nobody has decided:**

- **Which clock the censoring replay reads.** The inherited `stock_moves` index orders by `recorded_at`, the accounting clock ledger rule 8 fixes, while the forecast's demand weeks bucket `sales` by `occurred_at`, so the two halves of one 26-week window read different clocks and nobody has ruled on whether they should — at v1 they diverge only for events a till recorded while it was offline.
- **`supplier_return` has a cause and no document.** The ledger assigns `supplier_return` moves to S6 and assigns S6 no returns table. This stage models it as a `goods_receipts` row at `type = supplier_return`, which keeps it inside S6's owned tables and reuses the whole receiving path. If the ledger would rather have a `supplier_returns` table, this is the paragraph to overrule.
- **Coined names.** Enums `goods_receipt_type` (`receipt` | `supplier_return`) and `forecast_basis` (`parametric` | `learning` | `learned`). Columns `purchase_orders.number`, `purchase_order_lines.reason_code`, `purchase_order_lines.basis`, `demand_forecasts.basis`, `demand_forecasts.confidence`, `goods_receipts.status`, `goods_receipts.supplier_document_number` — every one of them lands on a table this stage creates, so rule 1 covers them and no ledger amendment is asked for. Settings keys `service_level`, `learned_min_weeks`, `learned_max_rse`, `learned_demote_rse`, `category_default_min_items`, `seasonal_multiplier_enabled`, `reason_text_enabled`, `write_model_stock_policies`. All English, all in register, none of them supplied by architecture.md or the ledger.
- **The parametric regime has one precondition, and it is somebody else's screen.** This stage decides not to invent a policy where none exists, so a sede with no history *and* no `stock_policies` rows gets the withholding empty state rather than an order. That makes onboarding's *set the reorder points* step load-bearing for the day-one Compras demo, and nothing in S3 obliges anyone to do it. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a pilot opens Compras on its first morning, finds the withholding state at every sede, and reads it as the product not working rather than as the product refusing to guess. **The measurement that would change it:** if onboarding consistently leaves policies empty, the fix is a defaulting pass at sede creation — `max_quantity` seeded from the opening count — owned by S3, and not a guess buried inside the forecast.
- **May a `cashier` receive merchandise?** This stage decides **no** — receiving is `owner`/`admin`, because it writes cost and creates lots. design-system.md §B.17 item 3 lists per-action role gating as open and this is one of its cases. **What breaks if this is wrong:** at a six-sede network the person who opens the carton is the auxiliar, and receiving that must wait for the administrator is receiving that happens on a laptop at night from a photograph of a remito — which is exactly the workflow this product was commissioned to replace. **The measurement that would change it:** if pilot receipts are consistently confirmed more than a day after `received_at`, the answer is wrong and the fix is a receive-only permission, not a role.
- **`suppliers.lead_time_days` is network-wide.** A supplier slower to Usme than to Chapinero is averaged into one number, because the column lives on `suppliers` and nothing in the ledger gives lead time a location grain. Accepted for v1; the fix is a column on `supplier_items` or a small table, and neither is assigned.
- **Nothing else.** §11.1, §11.3, §11.4 and §11.5 block other stages and do not block this one.
