# S7 — Pricing

## Outcome

An owner opens **Precios** and sees, for every reference the network sells, what it costs, what it charges, what margin that leaves, and what price this stage would suggest instead — computed from the reference's own measured price response where one exists, and from the tenant's own margin goal where it does not. Each suggestion carries its arithmetic in the open: which of the two engines produced it, the current and projected margin, the estimated monthly impact in pesos, and — where it rests on an elasticity — the coefficient itself, with the number of weeks and distinct price points it was fitted on. **Nothing on this screen changes a price.** Where the owner agrees, the row's action opens the catalog's own price editor with the suggested number already in the field, and what lands in `item_prices` is a `manual` row carrying that person's name and the `proposal_id` of the suggestion that informed it. This stage writes no price, and there is no source under which it could (A11).

**Two engines, because the screen has to be useful on the first morning** (§1, *Cold start*). The **margin rule** needs no history at all: a reference whose margin sits below the tenant's goal is proposed a price that moves toward the goal, bounded by the regulated cap and by the largest single step an owner permits. It works on day one, for every reference that has a cost and a price, which on a freshly loaded catalog is most of them. The **elasticity engine** appears per reference as that reference earns an estimate, and where it has one it decides. Every proposal states which engine produced it and how much demand signal stood behind it, because *this rests on a measured response* and *this rests on arithmetic about the margin you asked for* are two different claims, and an owner deciding which suggestions are worth carrying into the price editor is entitled to tell them apart at a glance.

The deck's claim is that most of a droguería's portfolio is price-inelastic: demand barely moves when the price does, so a small sustained adjustment on the right references lifts the month's margin without selling another unit. This stage is where that claim is either substantiated or exposed, and the screen is built to do both. **An item whose price has never moved has no elasticity, not a small one** — there is no variation to regress on, and waiting does not fix it, because what is missing is not observations but variation. That is not a limitation of the screen: **it is the reason the margin rule exists.** An elasticity does not degrade gracefully the way a demand forecast does (§1), so the estimator withholds rather than offering a weak number, records in one sentence which floor it missed, and hands the reference to the margin rule, which suggests a price from the owner's own goal and says so. The run's summary states how many references were evaluated, how many were estimable, how many were not and why, and how many proposals each engine produced. A pilot in which the estimable count collapses is a pilot in which the deck's claim is not yet substantiated — visibly, in the product, on the first Monday, which is the only useful time to find out — and the screen still had something to say that morning, which is the whole point.

The guardrail is the regulated maximum price. Colombian medicines under CNPMDM control carry a legal ceiling, and whether a maintained source of those ceilings exists is §11.4 and unanswered. This stage takes the only safe reading of a null cap: **unknown, never uncapped.** By default no reference whose cap status is unknown is ever proposed upward by **either** engine; it may be proposed downward, and it may be held. An owner may lift that default for the tenant, deliberately, with the act recorded. An item whose current shelf price already sits above a loaded cap is not a pricing opportunity at all — it is a compliance finding, and it is surfaced as one. **The margin rule sharpens this considerably.** An engine that mostly withholds suggests almost nothing on day one; an engine that needs only a cost and a goal can suggest a rise across a great deal of the catalog on the first morning, with no history behind it. That is why §11.4 is this stage's one substantive open question and §11.2 is not — though A11 changed what an unanswered §11.4 costs, because a suggestion nobody can act on from this screen is a far cheaper mistake than a price the model wrote (see *Gated on*).

The margin goal the whole product measures itself against lives here too, and after the cold-start decision it is no longer only a reference line: it is the margin rule's single input, so the number an owner types into `Ajustes` on the first morning is the number that decides what this screen suggests that week. The Panel's `Margen bruto` tile draws **24,8%** against a **≥ 22%** reference (handoff, Pantalla 1); the `22` is a tenant setting in the `/api/settings/pricing` group this stage owns, S9 reads it rather than hard-coding it, and this stage measures every suggestion against it.

No correctness in this stage rests on that migration having landed — the estimator issues the same queries without it, only slower — which is what keeps the two stages genuinely parallel rather than ordered.

## Scope

### In

1. **`elasticity_estimates`** — one row per item per grain per run, written for **every item evaluated**, including the ones that could not be estimated, with `elasticity` null and the reason recorded (§3, ledger).
2. **The estimator**: a constant-elasticity log-log fit on weekly aggregates over a 26-week window, with the exclusions, the minimum-observation and minimum-variation floors, and the diagnostics that decide whether an estimate may become a suggestion — all stated below rather than left to a build agent.
3. **The margin rule** — the parametric engine, which needs no history at all (§1): a reference whose net-of-IVA margin sits below `margin_goal_pct` is suggested a price that moves toward the goal, bounded by `max_single_step_pct` and by the regulated cap, with a materiality floor stated as a **margin gap** rather than a peso impact, because on day one there may be no volume to price against.
4. **`price_proposals`** — the **analysis** per item, never an instruction (A11): its **basis** (which engine produced it), current and suggested price, current and projected margin, estimated monthly impact, confidence, the estimate it rests on where there is one, and `respects_regulated_cap` (§3, ledger). S7 writes `proposed`, `above_cap` and `superseded`. **S1 writes `taken`, `modified` and `dismissed`**, with `resolved_by` and `resolved_price`, when a person acts on a suggestion in the price editor (ledger, the `price_proposals` row) — see *Data* and *UI*.
5. **The suggestion engine and the precedence between the two**: a bounded single-step evaluation rather than an unconstrained optimisation, the rounding, the two materiality floors, the cooldown, and the rule that decides which engine owns a reference when both have an opinion — all fixed here.
6. **`items.regulated_max_price` and `items.cap_status`** — the cap, which lives on the **item** rather than on a price row: it is a regulatory property of the product, and holding it on `item_prices` would oblige every new price row to carry the previous one's cap forward, with the guardrail failing silently on exactly the reference somebody just repriced. **S7 writes no column of `item_prices` at all** (A11), with the manual entry path and the CSV load that make the guardrail usable before §11.4 is answered (ledger, disputed columns). A cap is a constraint, not a price: it opens no price window and closes none.
7. **A stated answer for every reference that got no suggestion.** An item is never silently absent: it is in the grid, with a badge distinguishing *nothing could evaluate this* from *both engines looked and there is nothing to say*, and a sentence naming which floor or which gate it missed.
8. **`items.custom` under the `pricing` key** — `elasticity_band`, `cap_status`, `last_price_change_at` (ledger, disputed columns).
9. **The `/api/settings/pricing` group** — margin goal, maximum single-step change, whether uncapped items may be raised, plus the two keys the behaviour below requires (ledger rule 5).
10. **The Precios screen as the pricing analytics surface**, built from design-system.md §B.8.4·1, which fixes its layout, its eight columns, its four KPI tiles and its cap treatment. Its two header actions and its editable cell are superseded by A11 and reconciled in *UI*. This stage invents no visual language beside the design system.
11. **The row action that hands a suggestion to S1's price editor**, pre-filled with the suggested price and carrying `proposal_id`. It is the only path from this screen to a price, and it is a navigation rather than a write.
12. **The adoption measurement** — how often suggestions are taken, taken with a different number, or dismissed, **per engine** — which exists at all only because `proposal_id` and `resolved_price` are recorded. See *Measurement*.
13. **The `Precios y topes` settings section**, in the settings dialog's **Operación** group (§B.8.4·4), which also hosts the loaded caps and the CSV load.
14. **Two jobs** — the weekly run and the daily cap check — and an explicit statement that this stage runs no job that writes a price, because there is no such write.
15. **Audit trail** on every elevated-role mutation this stage exposes: cap set, cap import, settings write and run enqueued (ledger). Taking, modifying or dismissing a suggestion is audited by S1, because that write is S1's.
16. **Seed fixtures registered with S0's demo-seed command** — suggestions from both engines, caps in all three states, and resolved suggestions in all three resolved states, so the screen shows the mix rather than one engine's live output (§1, ledger *Cross-stage services*). See *Demo seed*.

This stage has no `INSERT` and no `UPDATE` on `item_prices.price`, there is no `model` value in `price_source` to record one under, and there is no scheduled repricing and no bulk apply. A suggestion becomes a price when a person changes it in the catalog's own price editor, and the row that results is `manual`, carries their name, and carries `proposal_id` where a suggestion informed it. - **Shelf labels and price tags.** No hardware in v1 (§12); a price change produces no printable artefact in this stage.

Naming it here so it is a product decision later rather than a gap discovered at a pilot review. It is also the single change that would most improve this stage's second version — and under A11 it would have to be built as a *catalog* capability, not a pricing-model one.
- **A model gateway.** S7 calls **no** LLM. §10 puts OpenRouter on S6 and S8 only; every sentence this screen shows is composed from a `reason_code` and figures the engine computed, so a suggestion cannot be explained by text that was not derived from its own arithmetic.
- **A pricing run table.** The ledger assigns S7 two tables and no more. A run is identified by `(computed_at, model_version)` on the rows it wrote; nothing else is created (ledger rule 1, and the instruction not to invent one).
- **An `imports` row per cap load.** `imports` is written by S1 and S6 (ledger); S7 records a cap load in `audit_log` instead. See *Gated on*.

**Why `elasticity.elasticity` must be nullable.** An item whose price has never moved does not have a small elasticity; it has no elasticity, because there is no variation to regress on. A zero in that column would be a claim of *perfect inelasticity* — the single most dangerous number this table can hold, because zero elasticity says "raise the price as far as you like and sell exactly as many". So the estimator writes a row with `elasticity = NULL`, `status = insufficient_variation`, and the counts that show why. Nothing downstream is allowed to coalesce that null to a number. If a build agent defaults it to `0`, every unmoved reference in the catalog — which is most of them — becomes a maximum-step rise suggested with a straight face, and a person working down a screen of them is how this stage does damage.

**`item_prices.price` is the price the customer pays.** Architecture.md does not state it; this stage does, because margin is not computable without it. The shelf price is gross of IVA (handoff, `$15.600`), so **margin is computed net of IVA**: `net = price / (1 + rate(vat_class))`, `margin = (net − cost) / net`, with `excluded` and `exempt` at rate 0, `rate_5` at 0,05 and `rate_19` at 0,19 (§3). **Elasticity is estimated on the gross price**, because that is the number the customer saw. Two prices, two uses. *If this is wrong* — if S1 defines `price` as net — every margin figure in this stage, and therefore the Panel's `24,8%`, is overstated by up to 19% on the roughly one-third of a droguería's basket that is not IVA-excluded, and every `projected_margin` is unachievable. It is a one-line change here and a rebuild of S9's tile, so it is worth settling before either is built.

**The cost basis is current, never historical.** `cost_basis` is the quantity-weighted `lots.unit_cost` of the item's on-hand lots across the network at the moment the suggestion is computed, falling back to `supplier_items.cost` where there is no stock and to null where there is neither — an item with no cost gets no suggestion, because a margin computed against a missing cost is a fiction. `sale_lines.unit_cost` is deliberately **not** used: it is stamped per sale from the lot (ledger, disputed columns) and is exactly right for historical margin, and exactly wrong for projecting forward from today's replacement cost. The projection holds cost constant and the record panel says so — a suggestion is an analysis of a price, not a forecast of a cost.

### The estimator, and its limits

The columns above are meaningless without the method that fills them, so it is fixed here rather than left to a build agent. A pricing model does its damage through its assumptions, and an assumption nobody wrote down is an assumption nobody can review.

**Grain and window.** Weekly aggregates over the trailing **26 weeks**, per item, **pooled across sedes** with a location fixed effect where two or more sedes contributed. `window` records `26w`. The network grain (`location_id = NULL`) is the one a proposal reads, because a proposal is network-wide. A per-sede estimate is written only where that sede clears every floor on its own, and it is used to flag heterogeneity in the record panel — never to price a sede differently, which v1 cannot express.

**The estimator.** Ordinary least squares on `ln(units) = α + β·ln(price) + γ·t + month effects + location effects + ε`, with `β` reported as `elasticity`. The linear trend `t` absorbs slow drift; month effects are included only when the surviving weeks span three or more distinct months. `model_version` is `elasticity-loglog-v1`; changing the specification bumps it, and a run never mixes versions.

**The observed price is what was paid**, `unit_price − discount` per unit from `sale_lines`, weighted by units — not the list price, because the customer responded to what they were charged.

**Exclusions, in the order they are applied.**

| Excluded | Why, and what it would do if it were not |
|---|---|
| Any week in which the item had zero sellable on-hand at a contributing location, replayed from `stock_moves` (§6) | **A stock-out reads as a demand collapse at an unchanged price.** That biases β *toward zero* — the item looks more inelastic than it is — and this engine raises the price of inelastic items. Left in, the model's confident recommendation is to raise the price of the product the sede could not supply. This is the most damaging failure mode in the stage and it is why S3's append-only ledger is a dependency rather than a nicety |
| Any week in which more than 30% of the item's units carried a discount | A promotion is a different demand regime, and mixing it in overstates responsiveness |
| Sales with `status = voided`, and returns | Not demand |
| Weeks with no `item_prices` row covering them | The price is unknown; an imputed price is an invented observation |
| Items with `active = false`, or `tracks_stock = false` where no cost exists | A service with no cost has no margin to project |

`weeks_excluded_stockout` and `weeks_excluded_promo` are stored and shown, so a reader can see how much of the window survived.

**Floors. An estimate is written at all only when** at least **8** weeks survive, **and** the window contains at least **2 distinct prices** with at least **3 surviving weeks at each of the two most-used**, **and** the standard deviation of `ln(price)` is at least **0,02**. Below any of these, the row is written with a null `elasticity` and the matching `status`. Most of a droguería's catalog lands here on day one, the screen says so rather than hiding it, and the margin rule is what keeps that screen worth opening while it is true.

**An elasticity proposal is offered only when**, in addition: `observations ≥ 12`, `r2 ≥ 0,30`, `confidence` is `medium` or better, the reference's price has not changed within `min_days_between_changes`, and the projected monthly impact clears the materiality floor. `confidence` is **`high`** at `observations ≥ 20`, `r2 ≥ 0,50` and a 90% interval on β no wider than 0,6; **`medium`** at the offer floor; **`low`** otherwise — and a `low` estimate never becomes an **elasticity** proposal, so an elasticity-backed proposal is only ever `high` or `medium`. **A `margin_rule` proposal carries `confidence = low` by construction**, and that is not a demerit in it: its arithmetic is exact, and what is unknown is how demand will respond, which is precisely what `confidence` measures on this table. An owner who filters the grid to `Confianza · Alta` is asking to see only what was measured, and gets exactly that.

**The engine does not solve for an optimum, and that is deliberate.** Under constant elasticity the margin-maximising price is `c·β/(1+β)`, which for an inelastic item (`|β| < 1`) is negative or unbounded — the formula's answer for most of this catalog is "infinity", which is obviously false and is the trap this method sets. So the engine evaluates a **bounded step** instead: candidate prices on a grid from `−max_single_step_pct` to `+max_single_step_pct`, each rounded to `rounding_unit`, each scored as `Δprofit ≈ q̂₃₀ · [ (net(p₁) − c)·(p₁/p₀)^β − (net(p₀) − c) ]`, and the highest-scoring candidate wins. `q̂₃₀` is the item's trailing 30-day units across the network after the same exclusions. **For an inelastic item the winner is always the largest permitted step**, which means the step cap *is* the pricing policy and it belongs to a human — which is why `max_single_step_pct` is a setting an owner sets and not a number the model chooses. *If this bounded-step decision is wrong*, the model leaves money on an inelastic reference where a larger move would have earned more; the remedy is an owner raising the cap after a first step has been measured, not a model extrapolating a local fit to a price nobody has ever charged.

**Rounding is toward the current price, never away from it.** A candidate is rounded to the nearest `rounding_unit` that does not carry the step past `max_single_step_pct` — so a 3% cap on `$12.500` at a `$50` unit yields `$12.850` (+2,8%), not `$12.900` (+3,2%). A rounding rule that can exceed a stated policy limit is a rounding rule that quietly rewrites the policy.

**The arithmetic, worked once, because a build agent should not have to infer it.** Acetaminofén 500 mg × 100, Genfar. `vat_class = excluded`, so gross and net are the same figure. Cost basis `$8.400` from the weighted `lots.unit_cost`; current price `$12.500`; trailing 30-day units across the network `1.180`; the fit returns `β = −0,34`, `r2 = 0,41`, `observations = 22` — `high` confidence. With `max_single_step_pct = 3,0` and `rounding_unit = 50`, the winning candidate is `$12.850`, a step of +2,8%.

| | Now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Price | `$12.500` | `$12.850` |
| Margin, net of IVA | `(12.500 − 8.400) / 12.500` = **32,8%** | `(12.850 − 8.400) / 12.850` = **34,6%** |
| Monthly units | `1.180` | `1.180 × (12.850/12.500)^−0,34` ≈ **1.169** |
| Monthly contribution | `1.180 × 4.100` = `$4.838.000` | `1.169 × 4.450` = `$5.202.050` |

`estimated_monthly_impact` = **`+$364.050`**, `current_margin` = 32,8%, `projected_margin` = 34,6%, `step_pct` = 2,8, `reason_code` = `inelastic_raise`. Eleven fewer units a month, and the margin moves **+1,8 pp** — which is the deck's claim, in one reference, with every input on screen. It is also the shape of the Panel's `+1,9 pp desde el ajuste de precios` footnote, and the reason S9 can compute that footnote at all.

**Materiality.** An elasticity proposal is offered only when `|estimated_monthly_impact| ≥ $50.000` and the price moves by at least one `rounding_unit`. This is a constant in the engine and **deliberately not a setting**, because a setting that controls how many proposals appear is a dial someone will turn until the screen looks productive. A screen of 1.200 rows each worth six hundred pesos is a screen nobody reviews, and a screen nobody reviews is a model nobody uses, which is the failure this whole surface is shaped to prevent.

**The margin rule's materiality is a margin gap, not a peso impact**, and the reason is structural rather than stylistic: on day one there is no trailing volume, so there is no peso figure to compare against a floor, and a rule that demanded one would fire on nothing precisely when the screen most needs to fire. A margin-rule proposal is offered when the reference sits at least **1,0 pp** below `margin_goal_pct` **and** the price moves by at least one `rounding_unit`. Where trailing volume exists the peso impact is computed at unchanged volume and shown — labelled as the assumption it is — and it orders the grid; it never gates it.

**Seasonality is partly handled and says so.** A week-of-year term is not estimable from 26 weeks and this document does not pretend otherwise: the fit carries a trend and month effects, and models no annual cycle. The bias it leaves points at *withholding*: a price rise that coincided with a seasonal fall reads as elastic, which suppresses a raise rather than producing a bad one. The residual risk is the mirror case — an elastic reading that produces a **decrease** on a seasonal item — and it is bounded by the same step cap, the same materiality floor and a human pressing a button on every line. A second year of history is what actually fixes this, and nothing in the schema needs to change when it arrives.

**Competitor effects are not handled at all, because there is no source.** β is a reduced-form own-price response that already contains whatever competitors did in the same weeks. For a price the pharmacy controls that is the right object to estimate. Where it fails is a market-wide move everybody followed, which is attributed to us; the guard is that `computed_at`, `window` and `model_version` are on every estimate, so a run made in a month the whole market moved is identifiable afterwards rather than untraceable.

**Imported history is used and labelled.** Where S6's loader has run, `sales.source = imported` rows enter the fit — they carry a quantity and a price, which is all β needs — and `imported_share` records what fraction of the window they are. A proposal computed on a majority-imported window is capped at `confidence = medium`, because those rows record a price the previous system charged and a cost we did not pay. Where S6 has not run, the window is short, `observations` is low, few references clear the floors, and the screen says exactly that (§11.2).

**The regulated cap, as a rule, and it binds both engines.** `regulated_max_price` null means **unknown**, never uncapped (ledger, disputed columns). The three cap states live in `items.custom.pricing.cap_status`: `capped` (a cap is loaded), `not_regulated` (a source states the item is not under control), `unknown` (nobody has said). Every item starts `unknown`. Nothing below distinguishes a proposal by its basis, because a legal ceiling does not care which arithmetic reached it. Then:

- `respects_regulated_cap` is `true` only when a cap is known **and** the proposed price is at or below it. **An unknown cap yields `false`**, because a boolean named *respects the regulated cap* cannot be true against a cap nobody holds.
- An item at `unknown` may be proposed **downward** or held, and may be proposed **upward only** when `allow_raise_without_cap` is on. It is **off** by default, so on a tenant where nobody has loaded a cap **the margin rule proposes nothing upward either** — which is the day-one behaviour §11.4 actually governs. Turning it on is an ordinary settings write behind the ordinary permission dependency (ledger), and it writes an `audit_log` row and puts a standing line on the screen while it is on.
- An item at `capped` whose optimal candidate exceeds the cap is proposed **at the cap**, and its reason states that the cap bound it.
- An item whose **current** price already exceeds a loaded cap is not a pricing opportunity. It is written with `status = above_cap`, `respects_regulated_cap = false`, and it is a compliance finding: the till is charging above the legal maximum today. It renders `Sobre el tope regulado` in critical and **carries no row action at all**: the price editor cannot be opened from this screen against a price already above a legal ceiling, and the action is absent rather than disabled — exactly the treatment §B.8.4·1 gives that row's checkbox, applied to what replaced it. design-system.md §B.17·3 asks whether an `admin` may approve above a cap; after A11 nobody approves anything here, so the question dissolves rather than being answered (see *Gated on*).

*If §11.4 comes back "no maintained source exists"*, every item stays `unknown`, and with the default off **this stage offers no upward proposal at all** and reduces to a downward-and-diagnostics tool. That is the intended failure mode and it is not a defect. **It is also a much cheaper failure than it was**: what an unknown cap now suppresses is a number on a screen, not a row in the price table on every till (A11). The escape that makes the stage useful anyway is the manual cap path: a regente can enter caps for the few dozen references they actually sell under control, or load them as a CSV, and those references become priceable immediately.

### The margin rule, and which engine wins

The estimator measures what a reference's demand does. The margin rule measures nothing: it applies the tenant's own margin goal to the tenant's own cost, which is exactly why it works on a catalog loaded yesterday, and exactly why it is labelled as an assumption everywhere it appears (§1, *parametric*).

**What it computes.** For an item that is `active`, carries a cost basis and has an in-force price, the target gross price is `p* = c · (1 + rate(vat_class)) / (1 − margin_goal_pct)` — the price at which the net-of-IVA margin equals the goal. The proposal is `min(p*, p₀ · (1 + max_single_step_pct), regulated_max_price)`, rounded to `rounding_unit` toward the current price by the same rule the estimator uses, so the stated step cap is never exceeded by a rounding. **The margin rule only ever proposes upward.** A reference already at or above the goal is not a defect to correct, and lowering a price in order to *reduce* a margin is not a thing this product does.

**It reaches the goal in steps, not in one move**, and the reason line says so. A reference at 15,2% against a 22,0% goal needs an 8,7% rise and `max_single_step_pct` is 3,0, so the proposal is the capped step, `margin_gap_pp` records what remains, and the reference is proposed again after `min_days_between_changes`. *If this is wrong*, an owner waits three months for a reference to reach goal and the remedy is theirs — raise the step cap, having watched what the first step did to volume. *If the opposite were built*, one 8,7% jump lands on a reference nobody has measured, which is the move that sends a customer to the droguería across the street, and no engine here has the evidence to justify it.

**Worked once, so nobody has to infer it.** Loratadina 10 mg × 10, `vat_class = excluded`, cost basis `$3.900`, current price `$4.600`, goal 22,0%, step cap 3,0%, `rounding_unit` 50. Current margin `(4.600 − 3.900) / 4.600` = **15,2%**. The goal price is `3.900 / 0,78` = `$5.000`, a +8,7% move, past the cap — so the candidate is `4.600 × 1,03` = `$4.738`, rounded toward the current price to **`$4.700`** (+2,2%). `projected_margin` **17,0%**, `margin_gap_pp` **5,0**, `basis = margin_rule`, `confidence = low`, `reason_code = margin_below_goal`. With trailing 30-day volume of 220 units the impact shown is `220 × 100` = **`+$22.000` al mes, si el volumen no cambia**; with no trailing sales at all `estimated_monthly_impact` is **null**, the cell reads `Sin volumen`, and the proposal stands anyway — the margin arithmetic never needed the volume.

**No category defaults, deliberately.** §1 permits a parametric model to use category-level defaults stated as assumptions; this stage uses none. A default margin per therapeutic class would be a claim about what a category ought to earn, made by us, about a market we do not sell in — and it would arrive on the screen wearing the same badge as the owner's own goal. The only parametric inputs here are the tenant's: `margin_goal_pct`, `max_single_step_pct`, `rounding_unit`, and whatever caps a regente has loaded. *If this is wrong*, the screen proposes one goal for a category that could never bear it, the owner dismisses those suggestions in the price editor, and the dismissals are the signal that a per-category goal is worth building — one settings key and no schema change.

**Which engine owns a reference.** The partial unique constraint above permits one live proposal per reference, so precedence is decided here rather than blended:

| The reference's estimate | Who prices it |
|---|---|
| Qualifies — `confidence` `medium` or `high` | **The elasticity engine, whatever it concludes**, including concluding that nothing is worth doing. A measured response beats an assumption in both directions, and a reference the estimator priced or deliberately left alone is not re-proposed upward by the margin rule because a goal says it should earn more |
| Exists, does not qualify, and reads **elastic** (point `β ≤ −1`) | **Neither engine raises it.** `no_proposal_reason = elastic_veto`. The estimate is too weak to price on and strong enough to veto: weak evidence may stop a move and may not start one |
| Exists, does not qualify, and reads inelastic — or `elasticity` is null | **The margin rule**, on the terms above |
| No estimate at all — day one, a new reference, no sales in the window | **The margin rule** |

*If this precedence is wrong* — if a measured response should defer to the margin goal on a reference sitting far below it — the reference is still in the grid with its estimate and its margin both in the record panel, and the owner may set whatever price they judge right in S1's editor, and the proposal records it as `modified` with the number they chose — which is the signal that the precedence is wrong, arriving in the same column the measurement reads. The reverse error is not recoverable in the same way: a margin rule permitted to override a measured elastic response would raise the price of exactly the references whose customers leave, under a badge saying the model recommended it.

**The gates bind both engines.** The cap rules above, `min_days_between_changes`, the one-live-suggestion constraint and the staleness check the grid applies at read time hold whatever produced the row; none of them is an elasticity concern. A `margin_rule` suggestion is an ordinary row in `price_proposals`, rendered, filtered and resolved by the same paths, which is what keeps the second engine a second **input** rather than a second product.

## API surface

Every path carries the `/api/` prefix and is English (§3, ledger). Every endpoint runs behind S0's single permission dependency, inside the pinned transaction (§2, A1). The grid endpoint honours the grid contract: `manualPagination`, `manualSorting`, `manualFiltering` and `rowCount` from the API (§9).

**Read this table for what is missing.** There is no approve, no reject, no override, no apply, no revert and no batch. Not one route in this stage writes `item_prices.price`, and the database role this stage's service runs under needs no privilege on that column at all — which is the difference A11 draws between human approval as a *policy*, which is a setting somebody can change, and a model with no write path, which is a property of the schema and the grants (see *Acceptance*, criterion 11).

**A suggestion goes stale, and now the screen says so instead of a transaction refusing it.** A run computed on Monday is read on Friday against a cost and a price that may both have moved, and `item_prices` is a table this stage no longer writes at all. The previous design put that check inside a batch apply and refused the whole batch; with no apply and no batch, the check moves to where it always belonged — read time, on screen, before anybody types anything. `GET /api/pricing/items` recomputes the current price, the current cost basis and the cap on every read, and marks a suggestion **stale** when:

- **the in-force price no longer equals `current_price`** — somebody changed this price after the run, so the suggestion is arithmetic about a price that no longer exists. This is the collision that follows from a person being the only writer of `item_prices`, and it is now a rendering rule rather than a refusal;
- **the cost basis has moved more than 5%** since `computed_at` — the suggestion's whole case is a projected margin, and that margin is not the one the screen is showing;
- **a cap now binds** that did not when the run happened, or `cap_status` moved to `unknown` while `allow_raise_without_cap` is off.

A stale row keeps its figures, renders them at `#727272` with a line naming what moved and by how much, and **its action opens the price editor with no pre-fill** — the person prices it on today's numbers, or waits for Monday. Strictly safer than the batch refusal it replaces, and strictly less irritating: nothing is all-or-nothing any more, because nothing is a batch.

**Who reads, and who runs.** `owner` and `admin` both read every surface in this stage, and both may load caps; only an `owner` triggers a run or writes the settings group. **Nobody approves anything, at any role**, because this surface has nothing to approve — §2's "price approval" grant is exercised in S1's price editor, under S1's permissions, on a row that carries the person's name. *If restricting the run button to `owner` is wrong*, an `admin` who wants fresh figures asks somebody to press one button, which is a friction worth one line of code to remove. It is a much smaller question than the one it replaces, and making it smaller is what A11 was for.

**Settings group** (ledger rule 5; the first three keys are the register's, the last two are added inside the group S7 owns):

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `margin_goal_pct` | **unset** | The Panel's `≥ 22%` reference, the `Margen proyectado` tile's target marker, and **the margin rule's only input**. **Deliberately shipped with no default**, for the reason this stage gives for rejecting category margin defaults: a number we chose arrives on the screen wearing the same badge as the owner's own goal, and `22,0` looks like a finding about their business rather than a placeholder we picked. Until an owner sets it the margin rule suggests nothing and says why, and S9 renders `Sin meta definida`. **This is the day-one state, not an edge case** — see the first empty state under *UI*. **Setting it is an onboarding step** (S10's provisioning), which takes an owner about ten seconds and is the moment they decide what the number should be. `22,0` is the handoff's illustration and the demo seed's value, never a shipped default. **S9 reads this key** |
| `max_single_step_pct` | `3,0` | The largest move a suggestion from either engine may propose. For an inelastic reference, and for every margin-rule reference, this *is* the shape of the advice |
| `allow_raise_without_cap` | `false` | Whether an item at `cap_status = unknown` may be suggested upward, by either engine |

| `rounding_unit` | `50` | Suggestions round to this many pesos. `$15.637` is not a price a droguería charges |
## Jobs

Both jobs take `tenant_id` in the payload and pin inside their own transaction before touching anything (ledger rule 6). Both hold a Procrastinate lock keyed `pricing:{tenant_id}` (§9), so a run and a cap check never interleave.

**`pricing.run`** — the estimator, the margin rule and the suggestion engine as **one job**, not three.

- **Trigger:** cron, weekly, Monday 03:00 in the tenant's timezone (`/api/settings/tenant`, S0). Also on demand from `POST /api/pricing/runs`.
- **Idempotency key:** `(tenant_id, 'pricing.run', window_end_date, model_version)`. A second enqueue for the same window and version is a no-op, so a retried cron and an owner pressing `Calcular ahora` on the same morning produce one run.
- **Shape:** aggregation and regression happen in memory, the estimator runs first and the suggestion engine then consults its output to decide which engine owns each reference; **the entire run's estimates and suggestions are written in one transaction at the end.** A long write transaction holding four thousand regressions open is the thing not to build, and a half-written run is the thing a screen must never read.
- **The run never no-ops for want of sales.** With zero `sales` rows the estimator writes a `no_sales` row for every evaluated item and the margin rule still produces suggestions from cost and price alone. The day-one path is the ordinary path with one engine silent — not a special case, not a separate command — and it is the path the demo seed exercises.
- **Superseding:** suggestions still `proposed` or `above_cap` from an earlier run move to `superseded` in the same transaction, and the run's summary states how many. **A resolved suggestion is never touched.** `taken`, `modified` and `dismissed` are S1's writes and they are the measurement record; a job that rewrote or superseded them would be erasing the only evidence this stage produces about its own worth.
- **Failure:** three attempts with backoff. All three failing leaves the previous run's estimates and suggestions exactly as they were — nothing is partially replaced — and the screen's freshness line goes stale and shows its age. The screen additionally renders a region-scope error naming the run and its date (§B.10.3). It does not blank the table: last week's suggestions are still the best information that exists, and every current figure beside them is recomputed live on the same read.

**`pricing.check_caps`** — the guardrail, run independently of the model.

- **Trigger:** cron, daily, 05:00 tenant time.
- **What it does:** compares every item's in-force price against its `regulated_max_price` and raises `status = above_cap` for any item now charging above a loaded cap. This exists because a cap can move without us: a CNPMDM circular lowers a ceiling and a price that was legal on Tuesday is not on Wednesday. Tying that check to a weekly model run would leave a pharmacy up to six days over the line. **It is the one thing in this stage that reports a problem rather than proposing an improvement**, which is why it runs daily, on its own schedule, and survives the model being switched off entirely.
- **Idempotency key:** `(tenant_id, 'pricing.check_caps', date)`. Re-running produces the same rows.
- **Failure:** retried; a persistent failure surfaces on the screen as the `Propuestas sobre el tope` tile carrying its own staleness reading (§B.9.2), because a compliance tile that silently stops updating is worse than one that is absent.

**This stage runs no job that writes a price, because there is no such write** (A11). There was an obvious third job available here — a cron that applied approved suggestions on their effective date — and it is precisely the mechanism most likely to become *prices moving on their own*, so it does not exist rather than existing behind a flag that somebody could flip. Every price this stage informs is written by a person in S1's editor, synchronously, one reference at a time, with their name on the row. *If this is wrong*, an owner who agrees with forty suggestions spends forty short interactions instead of one, and the remedy is a better editor. *If the opposite were built and wrong*, one configuration change moves every price at every till, and there is no remedy at all.

## Measurement

**This stage can now be graded, and the grading is why the amendment kept `proposal_id` and `resolved_price` instead of simplifying them away.** A pricing engine that wrote its own prices could report how many prices it changed, which measures its reach and not its worth. An engine that can only suggest is measured by what people did with the suggestions — and every ingredient of that is already recorded: `price_proposals.suggested_price` is what we said, `resolved_price` is what they chose, `basis` is which engine said it, `resolved_by` is who decided, and `item_prices.proposal_id` is S1's proof that the suggestion and the price change are the same event. It is the same measurement `purchase_order_lines.suggested_quantity` versus `approved_quantity` and `sale_lines.from_suggestion` exist to preserve (A11), and it is the closest thing this product has to a verdict on whether the pricing model is worth its cost.

**`GET /api/pricing/adoption`, over a selectable window, split by `basis` and never aggregated across it:**

| Figure | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `taken` count and share | How often a suggestion was good enough to use unchanged |
| `modified` count and share, with the **median signed gap** `(resolved_price − suggested_price) / suggested_price` | How often the number was directionally right and quantitatively wrong, and which way. A gap near zero with a high `modified` share is an engine that is trusted and rounded; a large positive gap is an engine that is systematically timid; a large negative gap is an engine people are correcting downward, which is the reading that should stop a rollout rather than tune a constant |
| `dismissed` count and share, and the share of dismissals whose reference was **repriced by hand anyway** inside the following month | Whether a dismissal means *wrong* or means *not now*. The second figure is computable because a price change is a row in `item_prices` whether a suggestion informed it or not |
| `superseded` share of everything ever suggested | How much of the engine's output nobody ever looked at. A weekly run makes that figure easy to ignore and expensive to ignore |

**Split by `basis`, always**, because the two engines make two different claims and a blended adoption rate hides the case this stage most needs to detect: an elasticity engine nobody trusts, carried by a margin rule everybody uses. The `Adopción` panel renders the table above for `Elasticidad` and for `Margen` side by side, and states the denominator beside every share — a 100% take rate on three suggestions is not a finding.

**What this cannot tell us, stated so nobody reads more into it than it holds.** It measures whether people agreed with the suggestions, not whether the suggestions were right. A margin that rose and a customer who quietly went elsewhere are both invisible here: the first is S9's `daily_metrics` and the second is not observable at all. So the claim this panel supports is *the advice is being taken*, and the claim it does not support is *the model made the pharmacy money* — which is measured against `daily_metrics` on the references that actually changed, joined through `proposal_id`, and only S9 can compute it. Presenting adoption as though it were impact is the specific error this section exists to prevent.
## Offline

**Precios is an online-only office surface, and it says so plainly.** It is `owner`/`admin`, seated, server-authoritative, server-paginated, and it is **not** in S2's sync registry — this stage amends that registry in no way (A4, ledger rule 9). With no network the route renders the route-scope error geometry with a retry and a correlation id (§B.10.3). It does **not** render a cached table: a suggestion is a number somebody is about to type into a price field, and the staleness check that decides whether it is still a sane number cannot run without the server.

**This stage sends a till nothing.** After A11 it writes no price at all, so there is no burst of changed rows, no batch, no effective date to schedule and no new sync path. A price reaches a till the way it did before this stage existed: a person edits it in the catalog, S1 writes one `item_prices` row, and every device picks it up on its next delta pull over S2's existing replication (A4). The only row this stage writes into a synced table is `regulated_max_price`, which no till reads.

**A till that is offline through a price change keeps charging the old price, and that is correct.** §5's stale-price reconciliation is already decided: the sale stands at the price actually charged, `sale_lines.unit_price` is the record, and the difference is reported rather than corrected. **What A11 changed is how much of that condition this stage produces, and the answer is far less than the previous design would have.** A batch of five hundred prices applied on a schedule would have been the largest single burst of changed rows this product ever produced for one device, and it is what drove a 500-reference batch cap chosen against §4's `< 20ms p95` delta-pull budget. There is no batch now: prices change one at a time, at human speed, in the catalog editor, and the burst that cap existed to bound does not occur. **The cap is gone with the mechanism**, and a later stage that writes many rows to a synced table owes §4's budget its own answer rather than inheriting a number from here. What does not change is S9's obligation to read `sale_lines.unit_price` rather than the price list, because an offline sede still charges yesterday's price for an afternoon.

**The settings section is online-only for the same reason and with a sharper edge.** `Precios y topes` lives in the settings dialog, which floats over whatever route is showing (§B.8.4·4); with no network it renders the section's field stack in the region-scope error treatment rather than a form whose `Guardar` will fail. A settings form that accepts input it cannot persist is worse than one that refuses, because the owner walks away believing the margin goal changed.

**Staleness on this surface** is a single per-surface line, not forty dots: the provenance line in the filter bar states the run's `computed_at`, which is §B.9.2's one permitted exception for a surface whose entire content is one snapshot. The per-row stale marking described under *API surface* is a different thing and is not a dot — it is a set of figures greyed with a sentence naming what moved, on the few rows where something did.
## Demo seed

Everything below is synthetic and self-evidently so, and none of it is ever loaded into a real tenant.

**Which profiles this fixture answers to.** The five are fixed in §1 and this stage adds none. `default` and `cold` are the two S7 is graded on; the other three still have to produce a coherent Precios screen, because a fixture that built nothing under a profile another stage asked for would break that stage's seed run.

**The unset-goal state is not a sixth profile, and nobody should go looking for one.** It is `default` with `margin_goal_pct` cleared through `Ajustes → Precios y topes` — the product's own settings write, reached exactly the way acceptance 5 reaches `allow_raise_without_cap`: by using the surface that owns the setting, then putting it back. A profile per settings permutation is how a five-profile contract becomes twenty.

**The window is not fully covered on `default`, and no check may assume it is.** §1 builds 180 days of sales, which is 25,7 weeks against this stage's 26-week window, and the stock-out and promo exclusions take more out of it. `window` still records `26w` — that is the window the estimator asked for — but `observations` is whatever survived, and it is read from the record panel and `GET /api/pricing/summary` rather than computed from the window's nominal length. The seeded fits are built to clear the floors on their own terms: two or three price points, at least three surviving weeks at each of the two most-used, and enough weeks left after the exclusions to reach `medium` or `high`.

**What `default` builds**, and what the other four vary from:

- **The settings group at the seed's own values** — `margin_goal_pct` **22,0, which the seed sets explicitly because no default ships**, `max_single_step_pct` 3,0, `rounding_unit` 50, `min_days_between_changes` 30, `allow_raise_without_cap` **false**. - **Caps in all three states, in a realistic proportion.** Roughly 60 references at `cap_status = capped` with a `regulated_max_price` and a plainly fictional source reference, a few hundred at `not_regulated`, the rest at `unknown` — the state a real tenant reaches after a regente spends half an hour on the references they actually sell under control. **Two capped references are seeded above their cap**, so `Propuestas sobre el tope` is non-zero, the critical badge renders, and the absent-action rule is visible without anyone constructing it by hand.
- **Margin-rule suggestions, the bulk of the screen** — around 180 references below the margin goal, computed from the seeded catalog's own costs and prices, **none of them at `cap_status = unknown`**, because the default forbids raising those and a seed that contradicted its own engine would teach a build agent the wrong rule. A handful are cap-bound so `cap_bound_raise` renders, and a handful have no trailing sales so the `Sin volumen` impact cell renders.
- **Elasticity suggestions, the minority** — around 30 references whose synthetic sales carry two or three price points across the 26-week window, producing real fits rather than stamped numbers: mostly `inelastic_raise` across `high` and `medium` confidence, two `elastic_reduce`, and at least one `elastic_veto` where a weak elastic reading blocks a margin raise. This is the mix the screen exists to show, and it is what puts something on both sides of the `Base` chip.
- **Every no-proposal state populated at least once** — each `elasticity_status` and each `no_proposal_reason` value on some reference, because `Sin evaluar` and `Sin propuesta` can only be checked for conflation side by side.
- **Resolved suggestions in all three resolved states, dated about ten days back** — roughly 20 `taken`, 12 `modified` with `resolved_price` differing from `suggested_price` **in both directions**, so the median signed gap is not trivially one-sided, and 8 `dismissed`. Each carries its matching `item_prices` row at `source = manual` with `proposal_id` and a seeded `set_by_user_id`. This is what makes `Adopción` show a real ratio rather than an empty panel, puts a handful of references into `cooldown` with the reason rendered, exercises the `$16.000 · sugerido $15.600` cell, and hands S9 real price changes to compute its `+1,9 pp desde el ajuste de precios` footnote against. **The three resolved states are S1's writes even in the seed** — the fixture resolves them through S1's price-editor path, because a fixture that stamped them directly would be modelling a write path that does not exist.
- **A few stale suggestions** — two references whose price was changed by hand after the run, one whose cost basis moved 8% — so the greyed figures, the sentence naming what moved and the no-pre-fill action are all visible without waiting a week for them.

**The seed runs this stage's own engines rather than inserting suggestion rows by hand, on every profile.** It seeds prices, costs, caps and whatever sales the profile calls for, invokes `pricing.run`, then resolves a subset through S1's price editor. A fixture that bypasses the engine is a fixture that keeps rendering a convincing screen after the engine has broken, which is the one thing a completion test must not do — and it is why `cold` is a profile the engine runs against with nothing to fit, rather than a `default` somebody deleted the sales out of.

## Verification

Everything below runs against a tenant built by S0's `seed_demo_tenant` at **`--profile default`** unless the check names another profile, and against nothing else (§1) — no client export, no legacy history, no row inserted by hand, and no check that reaches an unusual state by deleting rows out from under a healthy one. That is the state every build session starts in, and a check that needs a customer's data is a check nobody runs. **Every figure asserted below is the seed's own**: a count this stage's fixtures build, or a count the product itself reports through `GET /api/pricing/summary` and `GET /api/pricing/adoption`. Nothing here is asserted against the handoff's 4.284-reference catalog or any other production sizing figure — those describe what a pilot's tenant will hold, the seed holds what its fixtures built, and a check that confuses the two fails on every run (§1). Where a check says *the latest run*, it means the highest `(computed_at, model_version)` pair on the rows that run wrote; there is no run table. Where it says *re-run*, it means `POST /api/pricing/runs` or `pricing.run` enqueued directly, and the assertion holds either way.

### Gates

- **Lint, type-check, the test suite and a production build**, all green before anything below is looked at. A type failure on this stage is almost always the generated API client drifting from the new routes rather than the routes being wrong — regenerate it from `/api/openapi.json` before reading the error as a bug (§9).
- **The migration reverses.** Applying this stage's migrations onto the previous schema and rolling them straight back leaves the two tables gone and every enum type this stage created dropped with them. A down migration that leaves an enum type behind passes locally and fails the next clean build on a name collision, which is a failure nobody sees until a new machine builds the project.
- **This stage's migrations create no index and add no column to another stage's table** — nothing on `item_prices`, `items`, `sale_lines` or `stock_moves` (ledger rule 4). A second index over columns S1, S3 or S6 already indexed is invisible on an incrementally-migrated database and fails on a clean one.
- **The model state is settled** — generating migrations after the suite passes produces nothing new. A pending migration here means an engine column exists in code and not in the database, and the run writes that number nowhere.
- **RLS on both new tables.** `elasticity_estimates` and `price_proposals` each carry a policy and `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, and the runtime role owns neither (A1). This is a gate rather than a check because a table that has already reached a second tenant cannot be repaired by an edit.
- **The grant.** The runtime role holds `SELECT` on `item_prices` and **no write privilege on any column of it**, and on `items` nothing beyond `SELECT` and `UPDATE (regulated_max_price)` — the cap is a column of `items`, so this stage needs no write anywhere on `item_prices` at all (A11). Run the suite and walk the screen with those grants in force. Anything that errors is a price write, and it is the one failure in this stage that must not ship.
- **`price_source` has exactly two values**, `manual` and `imported`. A third is a rebuilt write path whatever it has been named (A11).
- **The seed loads on every profile it is handed, and it runs the engine.** S0's `seed_demo_tenant` completes against an empty tenant and refuses one already holding rows; at `--profile default` and at `--profile cold` alike, this stage's fixture produces its proposals by invoking `pricing.run` and its resolutions through S1's price editor rather than by inserting them. A fixture that stops invoking the engine keeps rendering a convincing screen after the engine has broken, which is the one thing a completion test must not do. **`cold` is a profile, not a mutilated `default`** — nothing below reaches a state by emptying a table the healthy profile filled.

## Hands off

- **`elasticity_estimates`**, one row per evaluated item per run, with a **nullable** `elasticity` and a `status` that says why it is null. Anything reading this table treats null as *no estimate* and never coalesces it to zero.
- **`price_proposals`**, an analysis and never an instruction (A11), with the six-value `price_proposal_status`, `suggested_price` beside `resolved_price`, and `basis` on every row. **This is the table every judgement of this stage is computed from**, including S9's `+1,9 pp desde el ajuste de precios` footnote, which joins it to `item_prices` through `proposal_id` rather than reading a price source.
- **No price rows at all, and this is a contract rather than an omission.** There is no `source = model`, no model-written price, no batch and no `batch_id`, because there is no `model` value in `price_source` to write one under. A later stage looking for "the prices the model set" will not find them and must not invent them: the honest measure of *prices this stage informed, that a person made, and that stuck* is `item_prices.proposal_id` joined to `price_proposals.status`, and S9 must read it that way.
- **`items.regulated_max_price`** populated for whatever caps exist, and `items.cap_status` distinguishing `capped`, `not_regulated` and `unknown` — so no later stage has to re-derive "we have no cap" from a null. It is the one column of that table this stage writes, and a cap is a constraint rather than a price.
- **The `/api/settings/pricing` group**, with `margin_goal_pct` as the key S9's Panel tile and the Reportes margin reports read. It is a settings read, not a constant and not a copy, and **it ships unset**, so every consumer needs a `Sin meta definida` rendering rather than a fallback number.
- **The adoption contract** — `GET /api/pricing/adoption`, split by `basis`, never aggregated across it, every share carrying its denominator. S9 may present adoption and margin impact side by side and must not present either as the other.
- **The Precios screen**, built to §B.8.4·1 as a pricing *analytics* surface, and the `Precios y topes` settings section in the dialog's **Operación** group — the second and third of the seven undrawn surfaces to land, now also carrying the cap list and the CSV load.
- **No change to S2's sync registry, no new sync path, no new job-facing contract — and no burst.** This stage produces no synced row a till reads. The 500-reference batch cap that used to be this stage's precedent for bulk office writes is gone with the batch that needed it; a later stage moving many rows into a synced table owes §4's delta-pull budget its own answer.
- **The reason-code vocabulary**, fixed and closed. S9's margin reports render the same strings for the same codes rather than composing their own explanation of why a reference was or was not repriced, and no surface in the product explains a price with generated text.
- **`price_proposals.basis`** — `margin_rule` or `elasticity`, on every row, alongside the `confidence` that says how much demand signal stood behind it. **S9 must not aggregate the two into one claim.** A margin-rule suggestion's `estimated_monthly_impact` is computed at unchanged volume and is an assumption; an elasticity suggestion's is a projection from a fitted coefficient. Any report presenting estimated impact as a forecast must state what share of it was assumed.
- **The seed fixtures**, registered with S0's demo-seed command: caps in all three states, suggestions from both engines including one above-cap compliance finding, every no-proposal reason populated once, and resolved suggestions in all three resolved states with their `manual` price rows — which is also the fixture S9 computes its price-adjustment footnote against.
## Gated on

- **§11.4 — regulated maximum prices, and A11 changed what an unanswered §11.4 costs.** The engines' cap rules are unchanged: a null cap is *unknown*, never *uncapped*; an unknown cap blocks an upward suggestion from **either** engine unless an owner lifts the default tenant-wide and on the record; a price already above a loaded cap is a compliance finding rather than a pricing opportunity. What has changed is the blast radius of getting it wrong. **A suggestion this stage cannot check against a known cap is displayed and cannot be acted on from here** — an `above_cap` row carries no action at all, and an unknown-cap reference is not suggested upward in the first place — so the worst case is a number on a screen that a person would have to retype in another surface, against a warning naming the cap they do not have. Before this amendment the same mistake was a row in `item_prices` on every till at the next delta pull. §11.4 still matters, because the margin rule can suggest a rise across most of a freshly loaded catalog on the first morning and a legal ceiling is not a thing to be relaxed about, and the three answers still unblock it at very different prices: a maintained source of CNPMDM caps; a decision that the pilot sells nothing under control, which costs nothing and is worth asking in exactly that form; or a regente loading caps for the few dozen controlled references the network actually stocks, which is half an hour of work and is what the demo seed models. **It is no longer a blocker on safety. It is a blocker on the demo**: with no cap source and the default in force, both engines reduce to downward suggestions and diagnostics, which is not what the deck sells.
- **§11.2 — what the legacy system can export, above all sales history. Not a blocker** (§1). It was written as one and it is not. With no import at all the margin rule prices the catalog on day one, the demo seed fills every tile, and the pilot is demonstrable and runnable. What the export changes is *which engine is doing the work*: with it, elasticity appears immediately on the references that moved price inside the imported window and the screen leads with measured responses; without it, the 26-week window starts filling on go-live day, the first genuinely useful fits arrive roughly six months later, and until then most rows read `Margen` rather than `Elasticidad`. That is a quality and timing variable. The honest question at a pilot review is *how much of this screen is evidence yet*, the `Base` chip answers it in one click, and the answer being *not much, on the first Monday* is the expected reading rather than a defect. Worth chasing early because it is free accuracy; not worth blocking a pilot for.
- **Is `item_prices.price` gross or net of IVA?** Architecture.md does not say. **S7 decides gross**, and computes margin net of IVA per `vat_class`. S1 owns the column and must not contradict it; if it does, every margin figure in this stage and in S9's Panel inverts for the non-excluded share of the basket.
- **Who may approve — dissolved rather than answered.** §2 grants `owner` price approval and defines `admin` by subtraction, which grants an `admin` approval too; design-system.md §B.17·3 records the per-action detail as open. **After A11 this stage answers none of it**, because nothing here is approved: the permission question moves entirely to S1's price editor, where the actions are *edit a price* and *save it*, under S1's own roles and on a row carrying the person's name. What this stage still decides is smaller and safer — `owner` triggers a run and writes the settings group, both office roles read, and both may load caps. **§B.17·3's specific sub-question, whether an `admin` may approve above a cap, dissolves with the rest**: there is no approval, and an `above_cap` row carries no action for any role. If S1 wants a position for its editor, this stage's is that no role should be able to save a price above a loaded cap.
- **`imports` and the cap loader.** The ledger writes `imports` from S1 and S6 only, so S7's cap CSV records an `audit_log` row and nothing else. If a cap load should appear in the imports history alongside the catalog and sales-history loads, the ledger must grant S7 a write on `imports`; S7 does not assume it.
- **design-system.md is current on A11**, checked rather than assumed. §B.7.4 carries the six-value `price_proposal_status` with the Spanish labels this stage coined, and §B.8.4·1 now renders `Propuesto` read-only, drops the `Descartar` and `Aplicar propuestas` header pair and drops the bulk bar — the write path that section used to describe is gone from both documents. This stage keeps every geometry §B.8.4·1 fixes: the eight columns and their widths, the cap treatment, and the four KPI tiles.
