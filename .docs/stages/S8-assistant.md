# S8 — Assistant

## Outcome

A customer at the counter says *"lleva dos días con diarrea y algo de fiebre; adulto, toma losartán"*, and the person serving them — who is an auxiliar, not a pharmacist — types or dictates it into a box on the Mostrador screen. The words come back as chips: **diarrea**, **fiebre**, **adulto**, **tratamiento activo · losartán**. Under them a card with a blue sparkle says, in two registers, what to do and why: *"Prioriza rehidratación. Ofrece sales de rehidratación oral primero; el antidiarreico solo si no hay fiebre alta."* and *"En este punto el 64% de los clientes que compran antidiarreico lo llevan junto con suero. La sede tiene las tres referencias disponibles."* Under that, three cards — **Primera opción**, **Con condición**, **Se lleva junto** — each naming a product this sede has on the shelf right now, the units it has, the reason in a sentence a person can repeat out loud, the price, and an **Agregar** button that puts the line on the ticket. At the foot of the card, always, never removable: *"Con fiebre de más de dos días, remitir a consulta médica. Botica no diagnostica."*

Everything in that paragraph except one sentence works with the internet unplugged. The chips are extracted locally. The candidate products are filtered and ranked locally against this sede's own stock, the network's own `cross_sell_rules`, and the safety layer — both of the last two synced to the till (A8). Only the recommendation's prose needs the model, and when it is not there the card labels itself **MODO LOCAL** and writes the recommendation from the same deterministic ranking, in fewer words. A cashier who has learned to use the assistant on Tuesday can still use it on Thursday when the fibre is cut, which is the difference between a counter tool and a demo. And none of it waits for a sales history: the chips, the filter, the ranking and two of the three card types are computed from the tenant's own catalog, this sede's stock and the safety layer, so a droguería that has never sold anything through Botica has a working assistant on its first morning and earns the third card type with its own trading (§1, *Cold start*).

The safety layer is not advice printed next to a suggestion. A product carrying a blocking warning for what the customer actually said never becomes a candidate: it is removed before anything is ranked, before any prompt is built, and there is no path in this stage from a filtered item to a rendered card. Items requiring a prescription are never suggested at all, though a person who has seen the prescription can still sell them through S4's normal search. And the model is constrained twice — once in the prompt and once on the way out — because a prompt constraint on its own is a hope: a recommendation that names a condition, diagnoses, contradicts a medicine the customer said they are on, or names a product that was not in the candidate list is discarded and never reaches the screen, and the cashier sees the local recommendation instead of an error.

The last thing this stage produces is a number nobody can currently produce: how often the suggestions are taken. Every offer is recorded when it is shown, not when it is accepted, so there is a denominator; every acceptance stamps `assistant_suggestions.accepted` and `sale_lines.from_suggestion` in the same act. The Panel's *"58,6% de sugerencias aceptadas"*, its *"Combinaciones más aceptadas"* list and its *"Ticket con sugerencia $41.200 / Ticket sin sugerencia $28.700"* comparison are all reads over those two columns, and S9 derives none of them a second way.

### `triggers` — the structured condition, and why the vocabulary is closed

A trigger is a JSONB array of clauses, ORed; each clause is one object over a **closed English vocabulary**: `{"symptom": "fever", "operator": ">", "value": 38.5, "unit": "celsius"}`, `{"symptom": "blood_in_stool"}`, `{"interacts_with_ingredient": "losartan"}`, `{"population": "child"}`, `{"duration_days": {"operator": ">=", "value": 2}}`. The same vocabulary is what the extractor emits into `assistant_queries.symptoms`, whose shape is `[{"key": "diarrhea", "label": "diarrea", "kind": "symptom", "source": "lexicon"}]` — the English `key` is what the filter matches on, the Spanish `label` is the chip a cashier reads, and they are never derived from each other at runtime.

**This closure is the load-bearing decision of the safety layer.** If the extractor may emit a key the trigger vocabulary does not contain, or a warning may name a condition the extractor cannot produce, then the filter never fires and nothing anywhere raises: the assistant looks like it is working and the safety layer is decorative. So the vocabulary is one versioned list shipped with the release, the API refuses an `item_warnings` write whose `triggers` names a key outside it, and the daily job counts warnings whose triggers have never once matched an extraction in 30 days and surfaces them in Ajustes as *"esta advertencia nunca se ha activado"*. **What breaks if this is wrong:** the handoff's own example — *"no ofrecer si la fiebre pasa de 38,5 °C o si hay sangre"* — silently never fires, and loperamida is offered to a customer with blood in their stool by a piece of software that has a safety layer in its schema and none in its behaviour.

### The candidate pipeline, stated so it can be debugged

Five steps, deterministic, identical online and offline. **The model participates in exactly one of them, and it is not the one that chooses products.**

**1 · Extract.** The lexicon runs first, always, on the device, even when the network is up: surface forms → `symptom_key`, with negation handling (*"sin fiebre"*) and the active-treatment pattern (*"toma losartán"* → `{"kind": "active_treatment", "key": "losartan"}`). When the model is reachable it also extracts, and **it may add chips and may never remove one the lexicon found.** A model that could narrow the symptom set could un-filter a product the safety layer had excluded, which is the one thing the model must not be able to do.

**2 · Seed.** Each `symptom_key` maps to a set of `categories` through `symptom_category_map`, held in the `assistant` settings group because the category tree belongs to the tenant and no shipped file knows its ids. Seeded candidates are the active items in those categories. Where a key is unmapped, the seed falls back to matching its Spanish surface forms against `items.name`, `active_ingredient` and `categories.name` — weaker, and counted, so an unmapped vocabulary shows up in Ajustes rather than as a quiet drop in quality.

**3 · Filter.** In this order, each step removing candidates, all of it before anything is ranked and before any prompt exists:

1. `items.active`, and — for anything with `tracks_stock` — on-hand at **this location** greater than zero, excluding lots already expired. A service (`tracks_stock = false`, A7) is eligible and skips the stock test.
2. `requires_prescription = false`. **Never suggested** (§7). It remains sellable through S4's search by a person who has seen the prescription; the assistant simply never proposes it.
3. `controlled = false`. **A judgement call this stage takes:** §7 names only `requires_prescription`, but a controlled medicine proposed by software to a person who is not a pharmacist is the worst available version of this feature. *What breaks if this is wrong:* a droguería whose controlled lines are a real share of counter sales sees the assistant ignore them, which is a lost suggestion and never an unsafe one.
4. `invima_status <> 'expired'`. Botica does not block the **sale** of an item whose registration has lapsed — it surfaces the state and records the pharmacy's decision (§3, §12) — but it declines to **recommend** one. Suggesting is not selling.
5. Any `item_warnings` row at `severity = 'blocking'` and `active` whose `triggers` are **satisfied** by the extracted set. This covers all three types: an `interaction` against a named active treatment, a `contraindication` against a population or symptom, and a `do_not_suggest_if` whose condition is met.

**4 · Rank, and label.** Ranking is deterministic, local, and uses only what the till holds: seed strength (exact category 1,0 · active-ingredient match 0,8 · name match 0,5), then `lift` where the candidate was reached through a rule, then on-hand units descending, then price ascending. **The ranker deliberately reads no `demand_forecasts`.** S6 and S8 run in parallel off S4 (§13), and a ranker that needs a forecast cannot be demonstrated until S6 lands. *What breaks if this is wrong:* the first card is occasionally a slow mover the sede happens to be long on, which is a worse suggestion and never an unsafe one, and the fix is one term added to the score once `demand_forecasts` exists.

`suggestion_type` is **derived, never chosen by a model**:

| Value | Label | Derived when |
|---|---|---|
| `first_choice` | **Primera opción** | The highest-ranked candidate seeded directly by a symptom, carrying no unresolved warning |
| `conditional` | **Con condición** | A candidate carrying an **unresolved** `do_not_suggest_if`, or an `advisory` `contraindication` or `interaction`. Its `reason` **is the warning's `text`, verbatim**, and `warning_id` names the row |
| `bought_together` | **Se lleva junto** | A candidate reached through a `cross_sell_rule` from a symptom-seeded item or from a line already on the ticket — not from a symptom itself |

**`do_not_suggest_if` has exactly three outcomes, and the middle one is the whole of `conditional`.** *Satisfied* — the trigger's conditions are met by the extracted set — filters the item at step 3.5. *Unresolved* — the symptom the trigger keys on is present but its qualifier is not, as when *fiebre* is stated and no temperature is — makes the item a `conditional` card whose reason line is the warning itself. *Irrelevant* — the trigger's symptom is absent — leaves the item ranking normally. This is exactly the handoff's Loperamida card: **fiebre** is a chip, no temperature was given, so the trigger is unresolved, the card is **Con condición**, and its reason reads *"8 unidades en Chapinero · no ofrecer si la fiebre pasa de 38,5 °C o si hay sangre"*.

**5 · Prose.** The model receives the extracted symptoms, the ordered candidate list with its ids, availability, price and reason codes, and the cross-sell figures behind any `bought_together` card. It returns the recommendation's two registers and, optionally, a better `reason` line for a card whose reason is not already fixed by a warning — **a `conditional` card's reason is never rewritten**, because a safety string a model paraphrases has stopped being a safety string. It may not add, remove, reorder or re-price anything. With no model, the recommendation is written from the same ranking by a template over `reason_code`, in fewer words, and the card carries the `MODO LOCAL` eyebrow.

### `reason_code` — the deterministic cause behind every Spanish reason line

Every suggestion carries an English `reason_code` and a Spanish `reason`. The code is written by the pipeline and never by the model; the string is the code's fallback text unless a warning or the model supplied better. **This is what makes a reason line non-empty offline**, and it is what lets S9 group reasons without parsing Spanish prose — the same discipline S6 applies to `purchase_order_lines.reason_code`.

| `reason_code` | Fires when | Fallback string |
|---|---|---|
| `symptom_primary` | The highest-ranked candidate seeded directly by a symptom key | `repone la pérdida de líquidos, que es lo que más pesa en estos casos` — templated per symptom key |
| `symptom_secondary` | A symptom-seeded candidate below the first | `alivia el síntoma que describe el cliente` |
| `warning_conditional` | An unresolved `do_not_suggest_if`, `contraindication` or `interaction` | **The warning's own `text`, verbatim.** Never templated, never rewritten |
| `bought_together_location` | A rule at this sede above the support floor | **Two forms, selected by the rule's `confidence_band`.** At `medium` or `high`: `aparece en el 41% de los tickets con suero oral`, the figure being the rule's `confidence`. At `low`: the same sentence **without the figure** — `se lleva junto con suero oral en esta sede`. A percentage carried to two significant figures out of forty tickets is a false precision, and it is read out loud to a customer |
| `bought_together_network` | A network-wide rule, no sede-level rule for the pair | `en la red se lleva junto con suero oral` — no percentage, because a network figure with the word *punto* in front of it is false at most sedes |
| `ticket_companion` | Reached from a line already on the ticket rather than from a symptom | `se lleva junto con lo que ya está en el ticket` |
| `substitute_available` | The higher-ranked candidate for the same symptom is out of stock at this sede | `sustituto de una referencia agotada en la sede` |
| `no_candidates` | Nothing survived the filter; card C's empty state | `Las 3 referencias que aplican están agotadas en esta sede.` |

The reason line is always **units at this sede · the reason**, in that order, because the units are the fact the cashier verifies by looking at the shelf and the reason is the sentence they repeat out loud.

### What the miner counts, stated so a rule can be argued with

A **ticket** is one `sales` row at `status = 'closed'` with at least two `sale_lines` over distinct items, at `source IN ('counter','imported')`, inside `cross_sell_window_days` measured on `recorded_at` (rule 8: `recorded_at` for every report, `occurred_at` never). `support` is the number of tickets containing both items; `confidence` is P(B present | A present); `lift` is `confidence` over the share of all tickets containing B. Pairs are **directed** — A→B and B→A are two rows with different confidences — because the anchor is what the customer is being sold and the suggestion is what goes with it, and averaging the two directions loses exactly the asymmetry the assistant uses. An item is never paired with itself, a returned line is excluded from both sides, and a voided sale is excluded entirely.

Two scopes run per refresh: one per `location_id`, and one network-wide at `location_id IS NULL`. A pair that clears the floor at a sede gets a sede row; a pair that clears it only network-wide gets the network row. The till holds both and the ranker prefers the sede row where one exists, which is what makes *"en este punto el 64%"* a true sentence.

**Imported history is used and is labelled.** Where S6's loader has run, the window spans two systems with two definitions of a ticket, so **`basis` records which sources the run consumed** — `counter`, `imported` or `mixed` — while `algorithm_version` records only the algorithm, and Ajustes' provenance line names both populations and the ticket count behind them. *What breaks if this is ignored:* a legacy export that recorded one line per ticket produces no pairs at all and the miner reports a support floor nothing clears, which reads as a broken job rather than as an unusable export.

### Cold start — the assistant on a tenant with no history

`cross_sell_rules` is mined from the tenant's own sales, so **a new tenant has none, and that is the normal first state of this table rather than a failed job** (§1, *Cold start*). What follows is what the assistant does on day one, what it does as trading accumulates, and what it does in the case nobody plans for, which is no rules and no network at once.

**The regime is per card type, not per query, because only one of the three card types is learned at all.** `first_choice` and `conditional` are computed from the tenant's own catalog configuration, this sede's stock and the safety layer — the pharmacist's own parameters, in §1's sense — and they neither improve with history nor degrade without it. `bought_together` is the only output in this stage that needs sales to exist. So it is not the assistant that has a regime; it is card C's third slot.

| Regime | When | What card C shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Parametric** | no rule covers the anchor — day one, a new sede, a new product | **Primera opción** and **Con condición** only. The header counter tells the truth (`2 de 11 referencias`), nothing occupies the third slot, and there is no placeholder and no apology |
| **Learning** | a rule exists at `confidence_band = 'low'` | a **Se lleva junto** card whose reason line carries **no percentage** — `se lleva junto con suero oral en esta sede`, never `el 41%` computed out of forty tickets |
| **Learned** | `medium` or `high` | the full reason — `aparece en el 41% de los tickets con suero oral` — which is what the handoff draws |

**Card C renders in all three.** It is empty only when the *filter* leaves nothing, which is a stock statement and not a history statement, and that state is already drawn above with its own copy. **Card B works from day one too**: the recommendation needs the network and this sede's stock, not history. What changes is its second register. The handoff's *"En este punto el 64% de los clientes que compran antidiarreico lo llevan junto con suero"* is a cross-sell sentence and cannot be written before there are rules to write it from, so with none the model is sent no cross-sell figures and `recommendation_secondary` is written from availability instead — *"La sede tiene las tres referencias disponibles."*, which is the handoff's own second half of that sentence and is true on the first morning.

**The worst case, written down because it is the one nobody thinks about: no rules and no network at the same time.** A pilot's first morning, on a till that has never held a rule, with the fibre cut. The cashier types the transcript and sees: the chips, because the lexicon is a shipped asset and extraction is local; a recommendation in card B under the **MODO LOCAL** eyebrow, written by the template over `reason_code` with no cross-sell sentence in it; two suggestion cards in card C carrying units, price, reason and `Agregar`, because `items`, `item_prices`, `stock_on_hand`, `lots` and `item_warnings` are all in S2's registry and not one of them is history; the advisory notice; and S2's sync line reading `Sin conexión`. No error, no toast, no empty card, no spinner, nothing greyed. **That is the honest floor of this feature**, and it is a working counter tool rather than a degraded one.

**The floor has exactly one precondition, and it is configuration rather than data.** `symptom_category_map` must associate the tenant's own categories with the symptom vocabulary, because no shipped file knows a tenant's category ids. It is minutes of an administrator's work, it is done in Ajustes, and the demo seed ships it populated. The state where it is missing is already drawn — *"El asistente todavía no conoce el catálogo de esta droguería"* over a primary that goes and fixes it. Step 2's surface-form fallback covers a **key** the map does not cover; it does not stand in for a map that is entirely empty, because a fallback that fires on every key is not a fallback. *What breaks if the two are confused:* a demo opens on a configuration empty state, someone reads it as the cold-start floor, and the conclusion drawn is that the assistant needs a data migration before it can be shown — which is the precise claim §1 exists to deny.

**How the rules accumulate.** `assistant.cross_sell_refresh` runs every Sunday from the day the tenant exists, whether or not there is anything to mine — a job that only starts once there is history is a job nobody remembers to start — and a run that finds nothing above the floor writes nothing and moves no `computed_at`. It reads `sales` at `source IN ('counter','imported')`, so Botica's own trading feeds it from the first closed ticket and S6's imported history feeds it wherever §11.2's export existed; `basis` records which.

A pair becomes a rule at **`support >= cross_sell_min_support` (default 25 tickets) and `confidence >= cross_sell_min_confidence` (default 0,15)**, capped at `cross_sell_rules_per_item` (default 4) per anchor per scope and ranked by `lift`. The band is derived at write time from the pair's own support and the window's own denominator, and never entered by hand:

| `confidence_band` | Derived when |
|---|---|
| `low` | the pair cleared the floor but `support < 100`, **or** `ticket_count < 2.000` for that scope — a solid-looking ratio out of a window that barely exists is still `low` |
| `medium` | `support` between 100 and 499, over a window holding at least 2.000 minable tickets |
| `high` | `support >= 500`, over a window holding at least 10.000 minable tickets |

**Roughly how much trading it takes.** §4's own load figure is 600 tickets per sede per day. The minable share — closed, at least two distinct items — is roughly a third of them at the pilot's ticket size, which is itself an assumption the first month replaces with a measurement, so about 200 minable tickets a day and 1.400 a week at one sede. The two or three near-universal pairs at a Colombian counter clear a 25-ticket floor inside the **first fortnight**, at `low`. But the first rule is not the number that matters — **coverage** is, meaning the share of the anchors the assistant actually seeds that carry any rule at all, and at this volume that reaches roughly half at **six to eight weeks**, which is when a **Se lleva junto** card appears on most queries rather than on a few. The bands move later still: `medium` wants 100 tickets carrying the same pair. **So the honest sentence to say to a client is that two of the three cards are there on the first morning and the third earns its place over about two months of trading** — and that where §11.2's export exists, all three are there on day one instead.

*What breaks if this is presented the other way round:* someone sells a data migration as a precondition, the pilot waits on an export from a fifteen-year-old system, and a feature that worked on the first morning is demonstrated in the eighth week.

### What the model is sent, and what it may return

**Sent:** the extracted symptom set (English keys and Spanish labels), the ordered candidate list — item id, name, presentation, `available_quantity`, price, `type`, `reason_code` — and the cross-sell figures behind any `bought_together` candidate. **Not sent:** the customer's identity, `customers` in any form, the ticket's other lines' prices, the tenant's name, or anything from `item_warnings` beyond the `conditional` cards' own text. **The transcript is sent only where `retain_transcripts` permits it and `model_enabled` is true**; where it is not, the model receives the extracted keys and labels and nothing verbatim.

**Returned:** a fixed-shape response carrying `recommendation` (≤ 180 characters), `recommendation_secondary` (≤ 220 characters), and an optional `reason` per candidate id. Nothing else is read. A response that adds an id, omits a required field, exceeds a cap, or is not parseable is a rejection under the same path as any other — there is one failure treatment, and it is the local fallback.

### The output check

Applied to every model response before anything renders. It rejects a response that: names an item id or a product name outside the candidate list; matches the closed condition lexicon (a shipped Spanish list of condition nouns — `gastroenteritis`, `infección`, `amebiasis`, `virus`…); matches a diagnostic phrasing pattern (*"usted tiene"*, *"es un caso de"*, *"probablemente sea"*); contains a stop-or-change instruction near an ingredient the customer named as an active treatment (*suspenda*, *deje de tomar*, *cambie*, *reemplace*); or violates the response contract's length or shape.

**On rejection nothing model-written renders.** `output_check_passed` is false, `output_check_flags` names which rule fired, the rejected text is stored only where `retain_transcripts` is on, and the card falls back to the local recommendation with the `MODO LOCAL` eyebrow. **The cashier sees no error, no toast and no empty card** — an error at a counter with a customer waiting is worse than a quieter card, and §B.10.3 is binding that no error treatment at the counter ever obstructs a sale. **What breaks if the check is too tight:** good recommendations are discarded invisibly and the assistant looks worse than it is, which is why the rejection rate is a measured number with an alert (`output_check_alert_rate`, default 2% over a trailing 200 queries) and not something someone eventually notices.

### The amendment to S2's sync registry

Under **rule 9** and **A8**, S2's registry gains four collections. `$T` is the pinned tenant; `$L` is the device's location, resolved from the `device_key` and never from a parameter.

| Collection | Predicate | Scope | Push | Idempotency key | Rows / location | ≈ Bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cross_sell_rules` | `tenant_id = $T AND (location_id = $L OR location_id IS NULL) AND support >= $cross_sell_min_support AND rank <= $cross_sell_rules_per_item` | **location** | no | — | 6.400 | 0,80 MB |
| `item_warnings` | `tenant_id = $T AND active AND item_id IN (registry items)` | tenant-wide | no | — | 900 | 0,27 MB |
| `assistant_queries` | outbox only — nothing is pulled | **location** | **yes** | `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` | — | — |
| `assistant_suggestions` | outbox only — nothing is pulled | **location** | **yes** | `(tenant_id, client_uuid)` | — | — |
| **S8 total, pulled** | | | | | **≈ 7.300** | **≈ 1,07 MB** |

**The per-location cap, and where it is enforced.** Cross-sell pairs are quadratic in catalog size: 4.284 items is over nine million ordered pairs, and an uncapped mining job is the exact failure rule 9 exists to prevent. The cap is **`cross_sell_rules_per_item` rules per `item_a_id` per scope, default 4**, above `cross_sell_min_support` (default 25 tickets) and `cross_sell_min_confidence` (default 0,15), ranked by `lift`. At the pilot roughly 1.600 items clear the support floor, giving about 6.400 rows and 0,80 MB — of which roughly 0,10 MB is the three provenance columns §1 requires, carried on every row rather than in a run-scope table the till would have to join against. **That denormalisation is deliberate and it is rule 9's own trade:** `basis` and `ticket_count` are identical for every rule from one run at one scope, so the normalised shape is a second collection to register, pull, compact and reconcile for three values, and a fifth collection costs more on a till than 0,10 MB does. `confidence_band` is the one of the three the device actually reads, because it selects which form of the `bought_together_location` reason line renders; `basis` and `ticket_count` are read only by Ajustes, which is online-only. **The cap is enforced by the job that writes the rows, not by the predicate that reads them** — a cap enforced by the reader is a hope, because the table it is reading has already been written.

**`lift`, and why `confidence` alone is not enough.** Confidence is P(B|A), and a product almost every ticket carries scores high against everything. Ranking on `lift` — confidence over P(B) — is what stops every suggestion card in the product being the same three fast movers. *What breaks if this is wrong:* the assistant recommends bolsas and acetaminofén to everyone, the acceptance rate looks respectable because those sell anyway, and the tile measures nothing.

**The disk budget is shared, and safety is never the lever.** S2 measures **under 12 MB** on disk for a first sync, at roughly twice the document bytes; S3, S4 and S8 all draw on that ceiling. S8's delta is under 1,2 MB and its *Acceptance* measures the **total** on a pilot till rather than its own contribution. If the total is over, the lever is `cross_sell_rules_per_item`, which is a setting and degrades the breadth of `bought_together` suggestions. **`item_warnings` is never the lever**: a blackout must remove the network, not the safety layer (A8, ledger disputed columns).

**Why the reference bundle is not a collection.** The lexicon, the `symptom_key` vocabulary and the tenant's `symptom_category_map` are one document of roughly 6 KB with no per-row deltas and no natural key worth versioning. It is served by `GET /api/assistant/bundle`, cached with the device record in local storage and refreshed whenever its version changes — the same treatment S2 gives the sede's own name and code, and for the same reason. *What breaks if this is wrong:* a lexicon that must be a collection is a fifth collection to compact and reconcile for one document, and a bundle that is never refreshed leaves a till extracting against last quarter's vocabulary — which the version stamped on every `assistant_queries` row (`bundle_version`) makes visible rather than invisible.

Contract only. Every path is English and carries the `/api/` prefix (§3). Every endpoint runs behind S0's single permission dependency and inside a pinned transaction (rule 6).

**The `assistant` settings group** (the settings register): `enabled` · `model_enabled` · `model` · `monthly_spend_cap_usd` · `model_timeout_ms` · `retain_transcripts` · `transcript_retention_days` · `symptom_category_map` · `suggestion_card_count` · `cross_sell_min_support` · `cross_sell_min_confidence` · `cross_sell_rules_per_item` · `cross_sell_window_days` · `output_check_alert_rate`.

**Two switches, and they are different.** `enabled = false` removes the assistant column from Mostrador entirely — S4's `BUSCAR PRODUCTO` results list returns to the full-height left column it held before this stage landed, the 420px ticket panel keeps its fixed width, and nothing about the assistant is on screen. `model_enabled = false`, or the spend cap being reached, or §11.3 answering *no*, puts every till in `modo local` permanently: the chips, the filter, the ranking, the cards and the notice all still work, and no transcript ever reaches a model provider. This is the shape §11.3 needs — *"a 'no' costs no code"* (§7, §11.3) — and it is why the switch is two booleans rather than one.

**Acceptance is written twice by one act, and the two writes are one transaction.** Pressing `Agregar` calls S4's line-add and then flags the line. Online that is the accept endpoint. Offline both rows go into S2's outbox in one batch and are applied in one pinned transaction on arrival (rule 6, fourth context); a batch that applies the line and not the flag is a batch that under-reports the assistant forever, so they arrive together or not at all.

## Jobs

Three, on Procrastinate's Postgres queue, each pinning the tenant from the job payload before touching anything (rule 6). **The model call is not among them.** It runs inline on `POST /api/assistant/queries` under `model_timeout_ms`, because a queued call would need the till to poll for prose it has already rendered a local version of.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `assistant.cross_sell_refresh` | Cron, Sunday 03:00 tenant-local, one job per tenant fanned to one per location plus one network-wide pass; also `POST /api/cross-sell-rules/refresh` | `(tenant_id, location_id, window_end_date, algorithm_version)` | The previous rows stand and `computed_at` does not move. Tills keep the rules they have and the sync panel keeps showing the older date — it never shows a fresh timestamp over stale rules. Three consecutive failures raise a region-scope error in Ajustes · Asistente naming the sede. A partial run is not published: rows are written per scope in one transaction, so a till never holds half a refresh |
| `assistant.transcript_purge` | Cron, 02:00 daily, one job per tenant | `(tenant_id, purge_date)` | Nulls `transcript`, `recommendation`, `recommendation_secondary` and `symptoms` on `assistant_queries` older than `transcript_retention_days`, and every one of them immediately where `retain_transcripts` is false. **This job exists and runs whichever way §11.3 is answered**, because the transcript exists the moment a cashier types it. On failure the rows stay and the job retries with backoff; two consecutive failures raise in Ajustes, because a retention job that has quietly stopped is a Ley 1581 exposure and not an inconvenience |
| `assistant.health_check` | Cron, 06:00 daily, one job per tenant | `(tenant_id, run_date)` | Computes the output-check rejection rate over the trailing 200 queries, the share of extractions that produced no chip, and the list of active `item_warnings` whose triggers have not matched in 30 days. Writes nothing outside its own report and surfaces it in Ajustes. On failure the previous report stands with its own date |

**The spend cap is enforced on the request path, not by a job.** Before the gateway is called, the handler sums `cost_usd` for the tenant over the calendar month — one indexed aggregate over at most a few tens of thousands of rows — and where the cap is reached the query runs in `local` mode with no gateway call at all. The sum is off the sale's critical path by construction, because nothing about a sale waits on this endpoint (§4).

## Offline

**The assistant column on Mostrador is offline-capable and is the reason this stage exists in the shape it does** (A8, §5, §7).

**What is local, and unmarked.** The extraction lexicon, the `symptom_key` vocabulary and `symptom_category_map` (cached with the device record). `items`, `item_barcodes`, `categories`, `manufacturers` and this sede's `item_prices` (S2's registry). `stock_on_hand` and `lots` for this sede (S3's amendment). `cross_sell_rules` for this sede and the network, and `item_warnings` for the whole catalog (this stage's amendment). The filter, the ranker, the type derivation and the advisory notice are code and ship with the application shell. Every figure on a suggestion card — the units, the price, the ticket arithmetic — is this sede's own data read from the local store, which is **tier 1 of §B.9.2 and carries no staleness marker**, because marking the numbers that are right is how a marker stops meaning anything.

**What is stale, and how it says so.** The mined rules have a `computed_at` that can be a week old, and the percentage inside a `Se lleva junto` reason (*"aparece en el 41% de los tickets con suero oral"*) is a figure from that run. It carries **no per-card marker**: the rules' freshness is stated once, in the sync panel (§B.9.3), as one line — `Reglas del asistente · hace 3 días` — exactly as §B.9.2 states the price list's freshness once rather than on every ticket line. Forty dots on a counter screen is the alarm fatigue that convention exists to prevent.

**What needs the network, and what happens without it.** Only card B's prose. With no connection the card renders the local recommendation with the `MODO LOCAL` eyebrow and no error, no toast and no retry button — the fallback has already done everything a retry would do. The offer rows are written to the local store and queued in S2's outbox; the sync line reads **`Sin conexión · 12 por enviar`** with a hollow warning dot (§B.9.1), which is S2's component and not a second indicator this stage invents.

**Nothing here is on the critical path of a sale, and that is structural rather than a promise** (§4, §10). `POST /api/assistant/queries` is called from the assistant column and is awaited by nothing else: not the search, not the line add, not the totals, not `Cobrar`. The two paths share no lock, no transaction and no request. The `Cobrar` budget of **under 200ms p95, offline included** must hold with a gateway call in flight, and *Acceptance* measures it that way rather than on a quiet till.

**Transcripts on the device are health data, and the till treats them as such.** Where `retain_transcripts` is false the till pushes the extracted `symptoms` and the suggestion rows and **never pushes the transcript at all**; the local copy is deleted when the sale closes or the query is superseded, whichever comes first, rather than at the next compaction. Where retention is on, the transcript pushes with the row and is purged server-side by `assistant.transcript_purge`; the local copy still leaves the device's store at sale close, because a shared till in a shop is not a place for a queue of other people's symptoms. S2's `local_retention_days` governs the rest of the outbox, and nothing unpushed is compacted at any age (S2).

**Ajustes · Asistente and Registro del asistente are online-only and say so plainly.** Both are office surfaces (§4, A4); neither is in the registry and neither reaches a device. With no network each renders a route-scope state in the content region (§B.10.3 geometry): `Sin conexión` / `Los ajustes del asistente necesitan conexión. El mostrador sigue sugiriendo con normalidad.` / `[Reintentar]`. There is no offline editing of a safety warning and no queued write of one: a blocking warning approved during a blackout and applied twenty minutes later is a warning that did not exist for twenty minutes, and the till has no way to know that.

## Seed fixtures

S1 owns the demo-seed command and **this stage registers its own fixtures with it** (§1, ledger, cross-stage services). S8's fixtures run **after S4's sales**, because the rules are mined from those sales and every suggestion row hangs off one of them. **This stage is not finished until the surfaces above render convincingly from the seed** — which is a sharper test than the suite, because the suite never opens card C on a sede with no rules.

**The rules are mined, not inserted.** The fixture runs `assistant.cross_sell_refresh` over the seeded sales rather than writing `cross_sell_rules` rows directly, so `basis` comes out `counter` and the bands come out of the seeded volume instead of being chosen. *What breaks if they are inserted:* a seeded rule table hides the one failure this stage most needs the seed to catch — a miner that runs and produces nothing above the floor — and lets a demo pass on a job that has never executed.

**One sede is deliberately cold.** Usme is seeded with three weeks of sales against the other five sedes' six months, so the seed holds the parametric case and the learned case side by side and the cold-start path is seen on the way to a demo rather than at a pilot counter. Chapinero's card C shows three cards; Usme's shows two.

## Hands off

- **`assistant_suggestions` with `accepted`, `accepted_at`, `type`, `rank`, `reason_code`, `available_quantity` and `sale_line_id`**, one row per card *shown* — so the acceptance rate has a real denominator and not an inferred one.
- **`sale_lines.from_suggestion`**, written on every accepted line and on no other, with `UNIQUE (tenant_id, sale_line_id)` on the suggestion side guaranteeing one line is credited once. This is the column the Panel's headline tile rests on (ledger, disputed columns).
- **The three definitions S9 reads and does not recompute.** *Acceptance rate*: accepted suggestions over suggestions offered, over `assistant_queries` rows where `superseded_at IS NULL`, for closed `source = 'counter'` sales in the period — the handoff's `58,6%` and `3.412 de 5.824 sugerencias`. *Combinaciones más aceptadas*, **counted at category grain on both sides, and read by S9 exactly as written here rather than recomputed**: a combination is the ordered pair (anchor category, suggestion category), where the anchor is the category of the item on the sale's lowest-`position` line carrying `from_suggestion = false` — the reference the customer came in for — and the suggestion side is the category of each accepted `first_choice` or `bought_together` suggestion on that same sale. The category on each side is the item's own `items.category_id`, rendered as that row's `categories.name` and **never rolled up to a `parent_id`**: *antidiarreico*, *protector gástrico* and *vitamina C* are the grain the handoff's own labels sit at — *"Suero oral + antidiarreico"*, *"Analgésico + protector gástrico"* and *"Antigripal + vitamina C"* are drug classes, not products — and a roll-up to the parent makes every row read *Medicamentos + Medicamentos*. The window is the period the tile is drawn over, on closed sales at `source = 'counter'` by `recorded_at` (rule 8), the same population the acceptance rate uses. One sale contributes each distinct pair once however many lines carry it, and a pair whose two sides fall in the same category is not a combination and is not counted. Ranking is by the number of sales carrying the pair, descending; ties break on the accepted-suggestion count inside those sales, then on the rendered label alphabetically under Spanish collation, so two runs over the same period return the same rows in the same order. *What breaks if this is counted at item grain instead:* the list becomes SKU pairs, the same combination splits across every presentation and pack size of the same drug until nothing clears the top three, and the tile stops reading like the screen the handoff drew. *Ticket con / sin sugerencia*: the mean `sales.total` over closed counter sales in the period, split on whether the sale carries at least one line with `from_suggestion`, served by this stage's partial index. **A comparison whose definition is not written down is a number nobody can argue with and nobody should believe.**
- **`cross_sell_rules`**, capped, per sede and network-wide, with `support`, `confidence`, `lift`, `window` and `computed_at` — readable by S9's reports and by S6's `cross_sell_pair` reason code without a second mining job.
- **`item_warnings`** as a maintained safety layer with a validated structured trigger, on the server and on every till.
- **`assistant_queries.mode`, `model`, `cost_usd` and `latency_ms`** — the mode split, the spend and the latency distribution, per sede and per period.
- **The model gateway client**, with the per-tenant cap, the kill switch, the timeout and the spend accounting, callable by any later stage that needs prose. S6 uses it for `purchase_order.reason_text`.
- **The `assistant` settings group**, populated, behind its `GET`/`PATCH` pair on the pinned tenant with no tenant id in the path (rule 5).
- **`cross_sell_rules.basis`, `ticket_count` and `confidence_band`, and `assistant_suggestions.rule_confidence` frozen at offer time** — so a period's figures can say which regime produced them, and a report over a network's first eight weeks is not silently compared against a mature one (§1). S9 renders these; it derives no band of its own and recomputes no denominator.
- **A binding rule S9 inherits:** the acceptance tile counts `sales.source = 'counter'` only. An imported sale from the previous system was never offered a suggestion, and letting it into the denominator makes the rate look worse in exactly the months S6's history loader covers.

## Gated on

**§11.3 — may customer symptom text reach a model provider at all, and what is retained.** This is the stage's only true blocker and it blocks the demonstration, not the build. Symptom text is health data about an identifiable person standing at a counter, and under Ley 1581 it is sensitive personal data whose treatment requires prior, express and informed authorisation. Three things must be settled before this is shown to a real customer rather than after: **whether the transcript may leave the instance at all** (a *no* is implemented by `model_enabled = false`, costs no code, and leaves cards A and C and the entire safety layer working — §7, A8); **what is retained and for how long** (`retain_transcripts` and `transcript_retention_days` exist either way, and `assistant.transcript_purge` runs either way); and **whether the person at the counter is told and asked** — `customers.data_consent` exists (§3), but a query is asked before a customer is ever identified, so consent for the *transcript* is not the same act as consent for the *customer record*, and nothing in the architecture says which sign is on the wall. What survives a purge under every answer is the row's shape — `location_id`, `sale_id`, `mode`, `model`, `cost_usd`, `latency_ms`, timestamps and its suggestions with `accepted` — which is exactly what every metric in the product needs and none of what Ley 1581 is about.

**Found here, and nobody has decided:**

- **The model gateway is a cross-stage service and the ledger now carries it**, naming S8 as owner and S6 as caller. S6 calls it for purchase-order prose and explicitly defers the kill switch and the spend cap to S8's settings group; S8 owns the client, the cap, the switch, the timeout and the cost accounting. S6 and S8 run in parallel (§13), so **whichever lands first builds it** — and builds it as S8's, wherever it lands.
- **`cross_sell_rules.location_id` is coined.** §3 lists the table's columns and gives it no location grain, but the handoff's own copy is location-specific — *"En este punto el 64% de los clientes…"*, *"aparece en el 41% de los tickets con suero oral"* — and a network-wide figure rendered with the word *punto* in front of it is false at five of six sedes. The nullable column follows `item_prices` and `elasticity_estimates`, which scope the same way in §3. *What breaks if this is rejected:* the reason lines lose their locality and become network claims, and the sync predicate loses the scoping that keeps the per-location row count at 6.400 instead of the network's whole set.
- **`item_warnings` is the catalog's safety layer and the ledger gives its loading to nobody.** The ledger says *"loaded with the catalog, editable by `owner`/`admin`"*, and S1 owns the catalog load tool. S8 ships the API, the editor, the validation and the vocabulary; **who produces the initial content is a data question the pilot must answer** — the manufacturer's insert, a curated list, or the regente's own knowledge typed in. *What breaks if nobody owns it:* the schema has a safety layer and the pilot has an empty table, the filter never fires, and criterion 5 passes only on a fixture.
- **`symptom_category_map` lives in the settings group rather than in a table.** The map from a `symptom_key` to a tenant's `categories` is per tenant, is edited by an administrator, and is small — so it is a settings key written through rule 5's helper rather than a table the ledger has not assigned. *What breaks if this is wrong:* the map grows a history, a per-sede override or an approval flow, at which point it wants a table and this paragraph is the diff.
- **The condition lexicon and the diagnostic-phrasing patterns are shipped assets, not settings.** A deployment that can edit the output check's own vocabulary can turn the output check off, which is the same defect as a configurable advisory notice (A8). They are versioned with the release and `bundle_version` records which one ran.
- **Three stages will each need a basis and a confidence, and only this one has coined an enum for it.** §1 asks every model row to carry both, and S6's forecasts and S7's elasticities need the same two ideas on their own tables.
