# S9 — Dashboard

## Outcome

An owner opens **Panel** on Monday morning and sees the whole network in one screen, at a period they choose — 7, 30 or 90 days. What the network sold and how that compares to the period before it. What margin that left, against the goal the network set for itself. How many times a reference ran out on a shelf. How much inventory is about to expire and in how many lotes. Underneath, the shape of the last thirty days as a bar per day, the sedes ranked against each other, and how often the counter assistant's suggestions were actually taken. At the bottom, one row per sede with the same five columns and the same five states, so six sedes — or twenty — are compared on rules none of them can bend. On a network in its first weeks the same screen renders the same way with fewer of its numbers: every figure that has a basis, no comparison that does not, and a stated clause where the delta would be.

None of that is an aggregate over sale lines. Every flow figure on the screen is a sum over `daily_metrics`, one row per sede per day, recomputed from the ledger rather than incremented into. A twenty-sede network asking for ninety days reads eighteen hundred rows, and that is why the screen paints in under 1,2 seconds instead of grinding through millions of lines (§4). The rollup is recomputed, never accumulated: running the backfill twice over the same range produces byte-identical values, because a rollup nobody dares re-run is a rollup nobody can fix, and the day someone must fix one is the day an owner disputes a number in front of their accountant (ledger, `daily_metrics.*`).

Two numbers on that screen are honest problems, and this stage answers both rather than leaving them to a build agent. **A quiebre is an event, not a condition**: every time a tracked reference at a sede crosses from positive to zero or below, counted once per fall, so the per-sede column sums to the network figure and both sum over days. **Inventario por vencer is valued at the lote's own acquisition cost**, never at shelf price, and it excludes lotes already expired, which are a write-off decision and not a forecast. Both definitions are one click away from the figure they define, in Spanish, on the screen — because an owner comparing Chapinero to Bosa on a number they cannot define will not trust the tool a second time.

Beyond the Panel, this stage ships the two surfaces the network's management actually lives in and nobody drew: **Sedes**, where the per-location comparison gets its own screen with a card per sede and a record panel behind each one, and **Reportes**, the nav item the handoff draws with nothing behind it — a rail of report definitions, each one a chart, a table, a stated source and an **Exportar** that produces CSV and XLSX and never a PDF of a table (§B.8.4·2, §B.8.4·3). Reports can be scheduled to arrive by email on a cadence, to the network's own owners and administrators and to nobody else.

### `daily_metrics` — the columns, and what each one means

Architecture §3 fixes eleven of these. The rest are S9's, created by the table's owner (rule 1), and each is here because a figure on a screen or a reconciliation an owner will ask for cannot be produced without it.

| Column | Kind | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `location_id`, `date` | key | The location, and the calendar date of `recorded_at` in the tenant's timezone (`/api/settings/tenant`, S0) |
| `revenue` | money | Σ over the day's closed counter sale lines of `quantity × unit_price − discount`, **excluding IVA**, **net of returns credited that day** |
| `returns` | money | The absolute value credited by `sale_return_lines` that day, so the netting above is inspectable rather than baked in |
| `discount` | money | Σ line discounts. `revenue + discount` is the day's gross before IVA |
| `tax` | money | Σ `sale_lines.tax_amount`. `revenue + tax` reconciles to Σ `sales.total`, which is what a cash close is counted against |
| `cost` | money | Σ `sale_lines.unit_cost × quantity`, net of returns. Stamped at the moment of sale, never joined to a lote's current cost |
| `margin` | money | `revenue − cost`. **Money, not a percentage** |
| `uncosted_revenue` | money | Revenue of lines whose item has `tracks_stock = true` and whose stamped `unit_cost` is null or zero — the margin figure's own coverage, disclosed rather than hidden |
| `tickets` | count | Closed counter sales recorded that day. A return neither creates nor destroys a ticket |
| `average_ticket` | money | `revenue ÷ tickets` for that one day. **Stored, never aggregated** — see below |
| `units` | count | Σ `quantity` over lines whose item has `tracks_stock = true`. A service is in `tickets` and in `revenue` and is not a unit, because `units` is a rotation measure and a service moves no stock (A7) |
| `stockouts` | count | Positive-to-non-positive crossings at item grain — the definition below |
| `suggestions_offered` | count | `assistant_suggestions` rows recorded that day at that location |
| `suggestions_accepted` | count | Of those, the ones whose `accepted` is true |
| `suggested_tickets` | count | Closed counter sales that day carrying at least one line with `from_suggestion` |
| `suggested_revenue` | money | Those tickets' revenue. With `tickets` and `revenue` this yields the handoff's `Ticket con sugerencia $41.200` / `Ticket sin sugerencia $28.700` without a live aggregate on the Panel's hot path |
| `backdated_revenue`, `backdated_tickets` | money, count | Of the day's total, how much arrived from sales whose `occurred_at` fell on an earlier date — the offline disclosure below |
| `computed_at` | timestamp | Server time the row was last computed. Every freshness line on every S9 surface reads the **minimum** of this across the rows in scope |
| `definition_version` | smallint | The definition set that produced the row. A row computed under an older set is not silently comparable to a newer one |

**`average_ticket` is stored and never summed.** A period's average ticket is `Σ revenue ÷ Σ tickets`, and it is *not* the mean of the daily `average_ticket` values — an average of averages weights a Tuesday with nine tickets equally with a Saturday with two hundred. The column exists because architecture §3 lists it and because a per-day report reads it directly; every period figure on every S9 surface derives from the two sums. **What breaks if this is ignored:** the Panel and the Reportes `Ticket promedio` report show different numbers for the same period, and the one an owner believes is the wrong one.

**The margin percentage is derived the same way.** `Σ margin ÷ Σ revenue`, never the mean of daily percentages, and never `Σ margin ÷ Σ (revenue + tax)`.

### `stockouts` — the definition

`daily_metrics.stockouts` for a location and a day is **the number of times a tracked, active item's on-hand at that location crossed from strictly positive to zero or below during that day**, counted at **item grain**, once per crossing.

Four decisions are inside that sentence and each one is load-bearing.

**It counts events, not days.** An item that runs out on the 3rd and is replenished on the 9th is one quiebre, not six. This is what makes the column additive: the network's `37` is the sum of the six sedes' `4 · 9 · 6 · 5 · 11 · 2`, and the 30-day figure is the sum of thirty daily figures. The handoff's own numbers sum to `37` exactly, which is the check that this is the definition the screen was drawn to. **If it were days-at-zero**, the per-sede column and the KPI would be item-days, the same reference would be counted thirty times for one unresolved problem, and the number would be dominated by whichever sede is slowest to reorder rather than by how often the shelf failed.

**It is item grain, never lote grain.** FEFO empties a lote every few days by design (§6); counting lote exhaustion would report hundreds of quiebres a week at a healthy sede and the number would mean nothing.

**It uses `recorded_at`, like every other figure here** (rule 8), and it is computed by replaying `stock_moves` — the only append-only source that can answer the question historically (A3). The opening balance for an item is the sum of its moves before the day's boundary; the crossings are read off the running balance through the day's moves in `recorded_at` order. A crossing into negative territory — an offline sale against a stock snapshot that was already stale (§5 rule 2) — is a crossing and is counted, because the shelf was empty and someone still asked for it.

**A reference that has been at zero since before the period contributes nothing**, because there is no crossing. That is deliberate: a permanent hole is a catalog problem for the Existencias screen and its `Quiebre` badge (S3), not a recurring event on a trend line.

**Where the user reads it:** the tile's label carries a definition affordance opening the §B.9.3 popover geometry, titled `Cómo se calcula`, with the text `Un quiebre es cada vez que una referencia con control de existencias cae a cero en una sede. Se cuenta una vez por caída, no una vez por día sin existencias. Periodo: 30 días. Fuente: métricas diarias.`

**What breaks if this decision is wrong:** every quiebre figure in the product moves by an order of magnitude and the per-sede column stops summing to the KPI. It is a `definition_version` bump and a full backfill, which is exactly why the backfill has to be re-runnable.

### `Inventario por vencer` — the valuation

The tile draws `$18,9 M`, a warning badge `142 lotes`, and the footnote `4,6% del inventario valorizado`. Three decisions.

**The cost basis is the lote's own `unit_cost`, never the shelf price.** S3 already fixed this for `GET /api/stock/expiring` and S9 does not re-open it: an inventory-at-risk figure priced at retail overstates the loss by the whole margin, and it is the number an owner would repeat to their accountant. Valuation is per lote, so there is no cost method to choose — these units came in on that lote, at that cost (§6).

**The window is `[hoy, hoy + expiry_valuation_days)` and excludes lotes already expired.** The horizon is S3's `inventory` setting, defaulting to the 90 the handoff draws. Already-expired stock is a write-off decision with its own `stock_moves.type = expiry` (ledger) and a legal dimension; folding it into a forward-looking valuation hides a compliance problem inside a forecast. When the network holds expired stock the tile's footnote gains a second clause — `4,6% del inventario valorizado · $2,1 M ya vencido` — and the clause links to the Existencias filter that lists it.

**A lote with no `unit_cost` is counted and not valued, and the tile says so.** The ladder is: the lote's own `unit_cost`; failing that, the most recent non-null `unit_cost` for the same item in the tenant; failing that, the lote contributes to `142 lotes` and contributes nothing to `$18,9 M`, and the definition popover states how many lotes are uncosted. **What breaks if this is wrong:** imputing a cost onto opening stock loaded without one (§11.2) produces a confident valuation assembled out of nothing, and the first person to check it against an invoice stops believing the tile.

Popover text: `Valor de los lotes que vencen en los próximos 90 días, al costo de compra del lote. No incluye lotes ya vencidos. El porcentaje es sobre el inventario total valorizado al mismo costo.`

### The day boundary, and the late offline sale

**A day is the tenant's calendar day, and a row belongs to it by `recorded_at`** — the server's clock — because ledger rule 8 is explicit that every report, rollup and fiscal deadline uses `recorded_at` and that `occurred_at` is shown to the operator and never used for accounting. `occurred_at` is the till's wall clock; it is what a cashier will swear to, and it is also what is wrong on a machine nobody has set the time on (§5 rule 4).

**So a sale made during a three-day blackout and pushed on the fourth day belongs to the fourth day, and no earlier day is revised.** That is the correct accounting answer and it is also visibly odd on a histogram: three short bars and one tall one, describing a collapse and a spike that did not happen. The stage does not fix this by moving the sale — that would make the rollup disagree with the cash close, the fiscal record and the turno. It discloses it. `backdated_revenue` and `backdated_tickets` carry the part of the day that arrived late, and any day where they are non-zero renders a secondary clause under the histogram and in the day's row of the `Venta por día` report: `incluye 47 ventas de días anteriores por $4,2 M`.

**What breaks if this is wrong:** an owner who needs the shop-day view — comparing this Tuesday to last Tuesday across a blackout — does not get it from this stage, and would need a second rollup keyed on `occurred_at`. That is a second definition of a day, on the screen a network's owner opens on Monday, and two screens quietly disagreeing about what Tuesday was is worse than one screen that is honest about when the data arrived.

### What does force a recompute

Three things revise a day that has already been rolled up, and none of them is a late sale.

**A void.** `sales.status` moving to `voided` (S4) removes a ticket and its revenue from a day that may be weeks old. **A return.** A `sale_return` credited today nets against *today*, not against the original sale's day, which is the same `recorded_at` rule applied consistently. **A late-accepted suggestion.** `assistant_suggestions.accepted` is written when `Agregar` is pressed (ledger) and a suggestion offered at 23:58 and accepted at 00:01 updates a row whose own day has closed.

The nightly close finds all three the same way: it recomputes the trailing three days unconditionally, then recomputes every day for which a source row's `updated_at` is newer than that day's `computed_at`, capped at 90 days back per run and reported when the cap is hit. This is the only mechanism in the stage that requires the dirty-day index on `sales`, and it is what lets the answer to *"can we just re-run it?"* be yes.

### One rollup, three exclusions

`daily_metrics` counts **`sales.source = 'counter'`, `status = 'closed'`, and nothing else.** Imported history (S6's loader) is excluded because an imported sale from the previous system was never handed over by us and has a different definition of a sale behind it. Open sales are excluded because they are a ticket in progress. Voided sales are excluded, retroactively, by the mechanism above. A report that deliberately spans the legacy history names `imported` explicitly and labels itself as spanning two systems — that is S6's rule and it is inherited here without amendment.

## Jobs

Five, all on Procrastinate's Postgres queue, all pinning the tenant from the job payload before touching anything (rule 6). Cron times are tenant-local, so the freshness line an owner reads at 08:00 is true.

| Job | Trigger | Idempotency key | Failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `daily_metrics.rollup_today` | Cron every 10 minutes, one job per tenant fanned to one per location | `(tenant_id, location_id, date)` upsert. **It recomputes today's row from source rows; it never adds today's new rows to an existing count.** An incrementing counter is precisely the thing that cannot be re-run | The previous row stands with its previous `computed_at`, and every surface's freshness line shows that older stamp. A fresh timestamp is never shown over stale numbers. Three consecutive failures for one location raise a region-scope error (§B.10.3) on the Panel naming the sede |
| `daily_metrics.rollup_close` | Cron 00:20 tenant-local, one job per tenant fanned to one per location | Same key. Recomputes the trailing three days, then every day whose source rows moved since that day's `computed_at`, capped at 90 days back | Partial completion is safe: every day is independent and every recompute is an upsert. The uncompleted days stay dirty and the next night's run finds them again. Hitting the 90-day cap is reported rather than silently truncated |
| `daily_metrics.backfill` | `POST /api/daily-metrics/recompute`, the management command, and once over the whole history at first install | Same key, one job per location per day | A failed day leaves the previous row untouched; the run reports which days did not complete and the same request can be reissued. **Running the whole range twice produces identical rows** — this is an acceptance statement |
| `report_schedule.dispatch` | Cron hourly | `(tenant_id, schedule_id, scheduled_for)` | Enumerates tenants, pins each, scans `report_schedules` on `(tenant_id, next_run_at)` for the rows now due, and enqueues one send per schedule. An overlapping run finds the same key already enqueued and does nothing |
| `report_schedule.send` | Enqueued by the dispatcher | `(tenant_id, schedule_id, scheduled_for)` | Renders the report, attaches the file, and hands the message to S0's email path. A retry after a delivery that already succeeded does not re-send. After the last attempt the schedule's `last_run_status` records the failure and the `Exportaciones` settings section shows it — **an email nobody received is not a delivery, and a schedule that fails silently is worse than no schedule** |

**The rollup never runs unpinned.** Enumerating tenants is the cron dispatcher's only cross-tenant act and it resolves tenant ids and nothing else, exactly as rule 6's single unpinned resolution path permits; every read of a metric, a sale or a move happens inside a pinned transaction.

**Why ten minutes and not one.** The Panel is an office screen read at a person's own pace, and a one-minute cadence multiplies a whole-day scan by ten for a figure nobody watches tick. The cadence is visible in the freshness line, so an owner can see how current the number is rather than assume. If a pilot asks for closer to live, the cadence is a constant and the mechanism does not change — which is the point of recompute-not-accumulate.

The `Estado` column is the badge column of this table, and it is the only one (§B.7.3). Five states, one per §B.7.1 family, evaluated in this precedence — the first that matches wins.

| # | State | Family · dot | Label | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `unreported` | Critical · solid | **Sin reportar · 2 días** | No device at the sede has synced in over 24 h. The clause carries the elapsed time |
| 2 | `no_sales` | Neutral · hollow | **Sin ventas en el periodo** | `Σ tickets = 0`. The row's numeric cells render `—` in `#909090`, never zeros (§B.9.2 tier 3) |
| 3 | `margin_below_goal` | Warning · solid | **Margen bajo meta** | `Σ margin ÷ Σ revenue < margin_goal_pct` |
| 4 | `overstock` | Informative · solid | **Sobrestock** | Días de stock > `target_coverage_days × 4/3` — 40 days at the default 30-day target |
| 5 | `on_target` | Positive · solid | **En meta** | Everything else |

**Why `unreported` outranks everything.** A sede whose till has not reported in a day has no trustworthy figure in any other column, and the whole promise of this screen is that an owner sees the network without asking anyone. A silent sede is the one failure this screen exists to make impossible to miss, which is §B.7.1's reading of critical exactly.

**Why margin outranks overstock.** A sede below the goal is losing money now; a sede carrying too much stock is money that has not moved yet. Both matter and the first matters more, and the `Días de stock` column still shows the second.

**Why quiebres produce no state.** The handoff's own data settles it: Kennedy at 9 quiebres reads `En meta` and Bosa at 11 reads `Margen bajo meta`. The count is already its own column and a badge restating it would add nothing. **What breaks if this is wrong:** a network where quiebres are the presenting problem gets no colour for it, and the fix is a sixth state with a threshold relative to the sede's active reference count — one row in this table, not a redesign.

Checked against the handoff: Chapinero, Kennedy, Restrepo and Usme land `En meta`; Suba at 23,8% margin and 44 días lands `Sobrestock`; Bosa at 21,3% lands `Margen bajo meta`. That is what is drawn.

**Staleness on this table.** A sede whose most recent device sync is between 30 minutes and 24 hours old carries §B.9.2's hollow 4px marker after its `Venta` figure, with the reading `Chapinero · hace 47 min` in the row's secondary slot and on hover and focus. **The threshold is 30 minutes, not §B.9.2's 5-minute office default**, and the departure is deliberate: a till pushes on its own cadence and a 5-minute rule would mark six rows of six on a normal Colombian afternoon, which is exactly the alarm fatigue §B.9.2 exists to prevent. Past 24 hours the marker is replaced by the `unreported` badge.

### The young tenant — every comparison needs a no-comparison state

A tenant in its first weeks has figures and has no comparators, and those are two different problems. The Panel computes no model and has no regime of its own (§1, *Cold start*), but it inherits the rule that governs the ones that do: **a figure with a basis renders, a figure with no basis withholds, and a comparison is never scaled up to look complete.** The first closed sale produces a real `daily_metrics` row and the screen fills from it; what is missing on day twelve is the thirty days before day one, and no arithmetic recovers them.

**The rule, stated once for every comparator on the screen.** A comparator renders only when the preceding window of equal length is **fully covered** by the tenant's own history — the network's first metric day is `MIN(daily_metrics.date)` across the visible locations, and a sede's own is that location's minimum. A partially covered previous window is **not pro-rated, not annualised and not extrapolated**: comparing thirty days against the eleven that exist and calling the difference a rise is a fabricated number with a percent sign on it. Where the comparator is unavailable the figure still renders at full size, no arrow is drawn, and the comparison line is replaced by `Sin periodo previo · 12 días de historia` at 11px `#909090` — one addition to a drawn string, flagged so a reviewer can reject it in one line. **The comparison slot keeps its height in every state**: it carries the delta or the clause and never collapses, because four cards in a `repeat(4,1fr)` row that change height on the day a comparator becomes available re-lay the screen under the reader.

**The period control is never disabled and never refuses.** `90 días` on a twelve-day tenant is twelve days of history summed honestly and labelled as such, not a blocked option — a control that disables itself teaches an owner the screen is broken, and the state it would be hiding is the one every first client is in.

**Row 1 — the four cards.**

| Card | With a young tenant |
|---|---|
| `Venta de la red` | The figure sums the days that exist inside the period and is **never projected** to the period's full length. `▲ 6,4%` and `contra $388,0 M en los 30 días previos` are both suppressed and the clause takes their place. The figure itself is never `—`: one day of sales is a real sum |
| `Margen bruto` | The percentage is a ratio inside the period, needs no previous period, and renders from the first costed sale. Two things under it can be missing, separately. **No goal set** — `margin_goal_pct` absent from S7's `pricing` group: the bar keeps its 0–35% scale and its fill, the `≥ 22%` reference and the 62% marker are **not drawn**, and the footnote reads `Sin meta de margen definida` with a ghost link to the pricing settings section. **No previous period**: the `+1,9 pp` footnote has nothing to measure against and the slot carries the no-comparison clause instead. Where both hold, the missing goal wins the slot, because it is the one an owner fixes in ten seconds. The uncosted-revenue dot matters more here than anywhere else — a young tenant's catalog is the least likely to carry costs, and a margin computed over half the revenue is the most flattering wrong number on the screen |
| `Quiebres de stock` | Computed from the ledger from the first receipt onward, so it has a basis on day two. `▼ 41%` and `contra 63 en los 30 días previos` are suppressed with the clause. **Zero renders as `0`, not as `—`**: no crossing over a window we actually observed is a measurement, and the popover states the window's length so a low count on a short window is not read as a healthy shelf. Its two failure modes are denominators, not history. **No lote inside the window**: the value renders `$0`, the `142 lotes` badge is **not drawn** — a warning-family badge reading `0 lotes` is a warning about nothing — and the percentage line reads `Sin lotes por vencer en 90 días`. **No valued inventory at all** — catalog loaded, nothing received: the percentage's denominator is zero, so the line reads `Sin inventario valorizado` and no division is attempted; the value stays `$0` |

**Row 2 — the three panels.**

- **`Venta diaria de la red`** — **the series is truncated at the network's first metric day and never padded.** Seventy-eight empty tracks in front of twelve real bars read as a network that stopped trading, which is the same misreading the *Offline* section guards against; the axis's first date is the first metric day and the bar count is the days that exist. The bar is still one day, so the unit never changes under the reader. **The subtitle's mean divides by the days of history inside the period, never by 30** — a day before go-live is not a day the network sold nothing, and counting it understates the average by whatever fraction of the period the tenant has not existed for. While the period is not fully covered the subtitle carries `· 12 días de historia`, the second flagged addition to a drawn string. A day *inside* the history with no sales still divides and still draws no fill (§B.12.3): that one is a real zero.
- **`Venta por sede`** — needs no history and ranks from the first day. **A sede with no revenue in the period keeps its row and draws no fill**, and is never dropped: an absent row reads as a sede that does not exist, while a zero-length bar reads as a sede that sold nothing, which is the true statement and the row an owner most needs to see. A sede whose own first metric day falls inside the period carries `desde el 12/08` in its secondary slot — a sede open for five of thirty days is not being compared like for like, and without the clause the ranking says it is the network's worst performer.
- **`Sugerencias del asistente aceptadas`** — **three states that must not look alike**, and the whole of this panel's honesty is keeping them apart. **(i) Assistant off** — the deliberately-empty state in the table below, `El asistente no está activo`, and never a donut. **(ii) On, nothing offered in the period** — `suggestions_offered = 0`: the track draws with **no arc**, the centre reads `—`, the count line reads `Sin sugerencias en el periodo`, and both sub-blocks are gated out. **Never `0%`.** **(iii) Offered, none accepted** — `0%` is a true measurement, drawn as an empty arc on a full track with `0 de 47 sugerencias` beneath it. It is the one place on the Panel where a zero is the most informative number on screen, and folding it into (ii) hides the fact that the assistant is being ignored.

  The two sub-blocks degrade on their own. `Combinaciones más aceptadas` renders the pairs it has — one or two, never three padded rows — and where there are none the eyebrow and its rows are gated at the header rather than drawn empty (§B.10.2). **The ticket comparison renders only when both sides carry at least 30 tickets in the period**; below that it is replaced by `Todavía no hay tickets suficientes para comparar` at 11px `#6b6b6b`. It is the figure most likely to be quoted back as the reason to keep the assistant switched on, and `$41.200` against `$28.700` computed off three suggested tickets is not evidence, it is one customer who bought a lot. **What breaks if this threshold is dropped:** the first week's Panel reports a spectacular lift, the number regresses as tickets accumulate, and the owner concludes the product got worse.

**Row 3 — the table, and the state that assumes a goal.** `Sin ventas en el periodo` with em dashes already covers a sede that has not sold, and that is the drawn behaviour. One state needs amending for a network that has set nothing yet: **with no `margin_goal_pct`, state 3 (`Margen bajo meta`) can never match, and state 5's label is then a claim nobody made.** In that case state 5 renders **`Sin meta definida`** in the neutral family with a hollow dot; the precedence is unchanged and `Sobrestock` still takes the row first where it matches. **What breaks if this is ignored:** every sede in a network that never set a goal reads `En meta` in the positive family, which is the product congratulating an owner for hitting a target that does not exist.

### Reportes

Built to §B.8.4·3. A 240px L0 rail of report definitions under `t-10` mono group labels, and a pane holding the report. Every report is the same four things: a `t-20` title, a filter bar carrying the period segmented control, a sede multi-select and a category chip, **one** chart from §B.12.5's four forms, one table under the grid contract, and an `sm` secondary `Exportar`. **Every report states its source and its freshness in one 11px line under the title** — `Calculado sobre métricas diarias · al 31/08 06:00`, or `Calculado en vivo sobre existencias · 31/08 09:12` for the two that are not rollup-served — because a report whose provenance is invisible is a report someone will dispute in a meeting.

| `Venta` | `Venta por sede`, `Venta por día`, `Venta por categoría`, `Margen por sede`, `Margen por categoría`, `Ticket promedio`, `Devoluciones` | ranked bars, histogram, ranked bars, ranked bars, ranked bars, histogram, histogram |

**Every report inherits *The young tenant*'s rule.** A report whose window extends before the network's first metric day states the range it actually covers in its provenance line, pads no day the tenant did not exist for, and computes no period-over-period column it has no second period for. A report is where a disputed number gets checked, and a padded window is the one thing that would make it useless for that.

**The registry is a contribution point, not a fixed list.** A report definition names its group, its title, its parameters, its chart form, its columns and its source; S10 contributes the `Cumplimiento` group's definitions against its own tables and adds no surface. **A group with no available definitions is not rendered** — gated at its header, per §B.10.2, because a section header with nothing under it reads as a failure to load.

### Exports

`Exportar` is a secondary button opening a two-item menu: `Excel (XLSX)` first and `CSV` second.

**XLSX is the default and carries four sheets on the Panel export** — `Resumen` (the four KPI figures with their comparators), `Venta diaria` (one row per day), `Por sede` (the table as drawn), and **`Definiciones`**, which names every metric, its formula, the period, the locations, the rollup's `computed_at` and its `definition_version`. **CSV is one table and one header row and nothing else** — a comment block would break the parser of whoever asked for CSV. That is why XLSX is listed first: a CSV mailed onward carries no definition, and the definitions are the point of this stage.

Every export is synchronous and capped at **50.000 rows**. A report that would exceed the cap refuses with §B.10.3's region treatment naming the narrower filter — `El reporte supera 50.000 filas. Acote el periodo o seleccione menos sedes.` — rather than starting a job whose output nobody can find. File name: `botica-{report}-{tenant-slug}-{period}-{yyyy-mm-dd}.{ext}`.

### Scheduled reports

Created in the `Exportaciones` section of the settings dialog's **Registros** group (§B.8.4·4), one `report_schedules` row per schedule. A schedule names a report, its parameters, a cadence (`Diario`, `Semanal · lunes`, `Mensual · día 1`), a delivery hour in the tenant's timezone, a format, and its recipients. **Recipients are chosen from the tenant's `owner` and `admin` users; there is no free-text address field.** An emailed export of a droguería's whole sales history is tenant data leaving the tenant, and a settings field that accepts any address is the cheapest exfiltration path in the product. The mail carries the headline figures in its body and the file as an attachment, under the same 50.000-row cap. The section lists every schedule with its last run, its status and, on failure, the reason.

## Offline

**Every surface in this stage is an office surface and every one is online-only. There is no local copy of any figure on any of them, and none is offline-capable.**

This follows from A4 rather than from convenience: what syncs is one sede's operating set, and a twenty-sede network's ninety-day rollup is the exact opposite of that. Putting `daily_metrics` on a till would sync eighteen hundred rows of other sedes' money into a browser to serve a screen a `cashier` cannot open. S9 adds nothing to S2's registry (rule 9) and reads the local store nowhere.

**What each surface shows with no network.** The route renders §B.10.3's route-scope treatment — the empty-state geometry with a `Reintentar` and a correlation id — reading `No hay conexión con el servidor.` / `El Panel se calcula en el servidor y necesita conexión. Las pantallas del mostrador siguen funcionando.` That second clause matters: the person reading it is standing in a droguería whose till is still selling, and a message that implies the whole product is down would send someone to check on a counter that is fine.

**A Panel already open when the connection drops** keeps its figures — they were true when they were fetched — and its freshness line stops being relative and prints the absolute stamp it was fetched at, per §B.9.1's relative-time ladder at and beyond 12 hours, brought forward the moment the connection is known to be gone: `Calculado sobre métricas diarias · al 31/08 06:00 · sin conexión desde las 09:14`. Nothing is dimmed, nothing is struck through, and no figure is replaced by a zero.

**`SyncStatus` is not rendered on any S9 surface.** §B.16 rule 10 requires it on every surface that reads the local store; none of these does, and a sync indicator on a screen with no local store would be reporting on something the screen does not use. What *is* rendered is the sede-level reporting state — the 30-minute staleness marker and the `Sin reportar` badge — which is the office's own version of the same question: not *is my copy current* but *is that sede still talking to us*.

**One consequence worth stating plainly.** During a network-wide blackout the Panel's figures freeze while the tills keep selling, and when connectivity returns the missing revenue lands on the day it arrived, disclosed by `backdated_revenue`. An owner watching the Panel through an outage sees the network appear to stop trading. The freshness line and the `Sin reportar` badges are what keep that from being read as a collapse, and they are the reason both are on the screen rather than in a settings page.

## Demo seed

The seed is one command, S1's, and every later stage registers its own fixtures with it (ledger, cross-stage services; §1). **Almost nothing the Panel draws is S9's to seed.** The revenue, the margin and the tickets are S4's `sales` and their stamped `unit_cost`; the quiebres are S3's `stock_moves`; the expiring value is S3's lots at `lots.unit_cost`; the donut, the combination list and the ticket split are S8's `assistant_queries` and `assistant_suggestions`; `Cumplimiento del modelo` is S6's orders; the `Margen bruto` footnote is S7's applied batches; and the fiscal strip is **absent by construction**, because the demo tenant configures no invoicing target — the state §8 specifies and the one every demo runs in. S9 registers three things and nothing else.

**1 · The rolled `daily_metrics` rows — computed, never fabricated.** The fixture inserts no metric row. It **invokes `daily_metrics.backfill`** over the seeded range across the seeded locations — the same job production runs — and lets it read the seeded sales, moves and suggestions. **What breaks if this is ignored:** hand-written metric rows would let the Panel show a revenue the seeded sales do not add up to, the `Venta por día` report would disagree with the tile above it, and the first person to check one against the other would be a prospect at a demo. Computing them also exercises the rollup, the day boundary and the netting against real rows before a pilot does.

**This fixture runs last**, and registration carries that order dependency on every stage that writes a source row — S3, S4, S6, S7 and S8. A sale seeded after the backfill is a sale the Panel cannot see; a stage adding a fixture later re-runs the backfill or contributes nothing to the Panel, and the reconciliation criterion below is what catches the mistake rather than a reviewer's eye.

**2 · `report_schedules` — two rows.** One weekly schedule with a successful `last_run_at`, and **one carrying a failed `last_run_status` and its reason**, so the `Exportaciones` settings section renders both rows it can ever render: a section that only ever shows success has never shown the row that matters. Recipients are the seed tenant's own synthetic `owner` and `admin` users, whose addresses are non-routable by construction (S0's fixture) — the seed sends no mail, and a dispatcher that does fire in a demo environment delivers nowhere real.

**3 · The seed's range, taken as given.** Every state in *The young tenant* is invisible on a ninety-day seed, and they are the states most likely to be met on the first day at a real client. S9's fixture reads whatever range the command is run with and assumes no particular length, so a short run produces a Panel with a truncated histogram, suppressed comparators and their clauses rather than an empty screen. Whether the command exposes that short run as a named option is S1's call; nothing here depends on how it is spelled.

## Hands off

- **`daily_metrics`**, populated per location per day, with `UNIQUE (tenant_id, location_id, date)`, a stated definition per column, and the guarantee that any range can be recomputed from source at any time to identical values. Every later stage that needs a network flow figure reads this table rather than aggregating `sales`.
- **The three definitions that were nobody's** — a quiebre is a crossing counted once per fall at item grain; an at-risk lote is valued at its own acquisition cost inside a settings-driven window with expired stock excluded; a day is `recorded_at` in the tenant's timezone and there is exactly one of them. Each is visible to a user in the product, and changing one is a `definition_version` bump plus a backfill, not an edit.
- **The recompute path**, callable per location per day range from an endpoint and a command, and the dirty-day detector behind it. This is what makes the rollup fixable, and S10's restore drill has a rollup it can rebuild rather than restore. It is also how the demo seed fills `daily_metrics` — the seed runs the backfill over the seeded range and inserts no metric row of its own, so the seeded Panel is produced by the production path and reconciles with the rows behind it.

The Panel's first row stays `repeat(4,1fr)`: it is the row the client signed off on at high fidelity, and re-laying it is a redesign, not a rendering detail. **The contract, stated completely enough to build against without asking:** a contributor registers a strip and supplies, per render, one of §B.7.1's five **families**; a **title** at `t-14`/500 carrying the figure inside the sentence (`12 ventas sin confirmar por el sistema de facturación`, `4 requisitos de cumplimiento por vencer`); one **detail line** at `t-12` `#555555` saying what happens next; and **at most one `sm` secondary action** — a label and a route, never two actions and never a primary. **Nothing renders when a contributor has nothing to say, and an integration nobody has configured is one of those cases** — absence, never a zero and never a `sin configurar` placeholder (§8).
